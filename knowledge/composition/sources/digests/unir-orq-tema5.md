# UNIR Instrumentación y Orquestación, Tema 5 (woodwinds): scans 1–35

Based on Piston, *Orchestration* pp. 139–216, and Adler, *The Study of Orchestration* (register charts).

**Pitch convention.** The course text names octaves one lower than this vault (Franco-Belgian style,
middle C = "do3"): e.g. the flute foot joint gives "si2, do3, do#3", which the range figure shows as
B3, C4, C#4; clarinet throat tones "sol3–sib3" are written G4–Bb4. **All pitches below are converted to
C4 = middle C = MIDI 60.** Ranges come from the figures (scan p.6, 10, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 24, 28).
Ranges are **sounding** unless marked *written*.

Skipped: 5.1 study guide, "Lo + recomendado", "+ Información" (p.29–32). Test (p.33–35) used only where it
adds a fact (marked).

## 5.2 Flute (flauta) (scan p.4–10)

**Concepts**
- Modern flute is metal but counted as woodwind: before the Böhm key system (~1840) it was wooden. Böhm keys
  gave reliable chromatic notes, better intonation and more power.
- Three parts: head (cabeza), body (cuerpo), foot (pie). The foot joint adds B3 (optional, "B foot"),
  C4 and C#4.
- Range (Fig. 3, "registro habitual"): **B3 (59, optional) / C4 (60) – C7 (96)**; a further extreme top
  note is printed above it under 8va (reads ~F7 [?], figure too small to read reliably).

**Register / dynamics behaviour**
- R1 [AVOID] Forte on the lowest four notes (C4–D#4, 60–63): velvety (aterciopelado) but cannot play loud
  and is easily covered. *Why:* weak upper partials.
- R2 [PREFER] First octave (C4–B4) is slightly brighter; the second octave (C5–C6) has far more clarity —
  the default melodic zone.
- R3 [ALLOWED] Third octave (C6 up) is brilliant without shrillness at least up to **A6/Bb6 (93/94)**
  ("one octave above the staff").
- Unlike the oboe, flute power grows as it ascends (contrast stated in 5.3).

**Agility**
- R4 [ALLOWED] Most agile wind instrument (only the piccolo is more agile): scales, arpeggios, register
  leaps at speed are idiomatic.
- R5 [PREFER] For long fast/loud high passages, alternate two flutes (breathing: high forte needs more air).
  Overlap the last note of one flute with the first 16th of the next for a seamless hand-off
  (Stravinsky, *Firebird*).

**Trills and tremolos**
- R6 [AVOID] Trills on the foot-joint notes: C4–Db4, Db4–Eb4, C#4–D#4, B3–C#4 (Fig. 6, difficult).
- R7 [AVOID] Fingered tremolos below G4 (second line of treble staff) are difficult.
- R8 [MUST] Tremolo width ≤ P5 (7 st) in the lower octave and ≤ M3 (4 st) in the high registers.
- Test Q2 (inferred correct option): flutter-tongue (Flatterzunge) is the wind equivalent of the string tremolo.

**Orchestral role**
- R9 [PREFER] Mainly melodic. For accompaniment chords it combines best with clarinets and bassoons, more
  rarely with oboes.
- Style fact: Baroque to ~1770 the flute was ornamental, usually doubling violins, often a single flute
  as colour; from the Classical "winds a 2" standard it gets autonomous, virtuosic parts (Smetana,
  *Moldau*: running flute figuration over harp/violin punctuation).

**Examples**
- Ravel, *Ma mère l'oye*: low vs. middle-register flute colours. Debussy, *Syrinx*: tour of all registers.

**Agent applicability**
- Flute melody default: C5–A6 (72–93). Never give the flute a loud line at 60–63 against brass or tutti
  strings; if a low flute line is needed, thin the accompaniment to pp.
- Fast runs/arpeggios: give to flute (or piccolo/clarinet), not oboe. Long fast passages: split across
  Fl1/Fl2 tracks with a one-note overlap.

### Piccolo (flautín) (scan p.10–11)
- Transposition: **sounds one octave higher than written** (+12).
- Range (Fig. 9): written D4 (C4 optional on some instruments) – C7 (one higher optional note [?]);
  **sounding D5 (74) – C8 (108)**.
- Sound brilliant, extremely light, cuts through the whole orchestra; even more agile than the flute.
- R10 [ALLOWED] Middle register blends well and yields delicate colours in combination with other
  instruments.
- Example: Vivaldi, Concerto for piccolo, I — piccolo doubled by Vn I at pitch notation.

### Alto flute in G (flauta en sol) (scan p.11)
- Transposing: **sounds a P4 (5 st) lower than written**.
- Range (Fig. 11): written C4–C7 → **sounding G3 (55) – G6 (91)**.
- R11 [PREFER] Use its lowest octave (G3–G4, its most characteristic zone); middle register is subtler
  than the C flute. (Inferred: avoid covering it — it is soft like the low flute.)

## 5.3 Oboe (scan p.12–17)

**Concepts**
- Conical wooden tube (grenadilla, rosewood, cocobolo) in three sections plus bell; **double reed**
  (doble caña / doble lengüeta), like the bassoon.
- Piston's adjectives: acrid, nasal, penetrating, harsh, cutting, hoarse.
- Range (Fig. 13): **Bb3 (58) – F6 (89)**, extreme A6 (93) in parentheses.

**Register / dynamics behaviour**
- R1 [MUST] Treat the oboe as getting **louder as it descends** (opposite of the flute).
- R2 [AVOID] Soft attacks on low Bb3 — it cannot be attacked gently.
- R3 [AVOID] Notes from **F#6** up: hard to produce; if used, approach by step.
- R4 [AVOID] Relying on oboe colour above A5: from A5 (first ledger line) it thins, and above D6 it loses
  its characteristic timbre — that zone is used mainly to double other woodwinds.

**Agility, trills, tremolos**
- R5 [AVOID] Fast arpeggios — agility is not its strength compared with flutes or clarinets.
- R6 [ALLOWED] Crisp rhythmic articulation (Ravel, *Le tombeau de Couperin*, Prélude).
- R7 [ALLOWED] All major and minor trills (player-dependent).
- R8 [AVOID] Tremolos wider than a P4 (5 st): unreliable. Tremolos above the staff sound poor.

**Orchestral role**
- R9 [PREFER] Solo melodic instrument; especially suited to solos and exotic/folk-like melodies (Falla,
  *El amor brujo*, Ritual Fire Dance).
- R10 [AVOID] Using oboes as blending filler inside the woodwind choir: the aggressive timbre penetrates
  flute, clarinet and bassoon and sticks out. Only in a very full tutti do they blend in.
- Style fact: oboes were in the orchestra from the start, **in pairs, together with two horns**; even late
  18th-c. works use strings + 2 oboes + 2 horns (Mozart Sym. 29).

**Agent applicability**
- Oboe solo zone: roughly D4–D6 (62–86); keep it exposed (thin texture) rather than buried in chords.
- Don't write ppp entries on the lowest notes (58–60); velocity/CC1 floor higher there.

### English horn (corno inglés) (scan p.17–18)
- In F: **sounds a P5 (7 st) lower than written**.
- Range (Fig. 18): written B3 – E6 (G6 extreme) → **sounding E3 (52) – A5 (81)** (C6/84 extreme).
- R11 [PREFER] Use for a clearly different melodic colour from the oboe; oboe ↔ English horn alternation
  of phrases is common (Berlioz, *Symphonie fantastique* III, "Scène aux champs").

## 5.4 Clarinet (clarinete) (scan p.18–23)

**Concepts**
- Cylindrical tube; parts: mouthpiece, barrel, upper joint, lower joint, bell. **Single reed** (caña
  simple), like the saxophones (which count as woodwinds because of it).
- Transpositions:
  - Bb soprano: sounds **M2 (2 st) lower**.
  - A soprano: sounds **m3 (3 st) lower**.
  - Eb (requinto): sounds **m3 (3 st) higher**.
  - Bass clarinet in Bb: sounds **M9 (14 st) lower** (an octave below the Bb clarinet).
- Registers (Fig. 21, Adler; *written* pitch; sounding for Bb clarinet in brackets):
  - Chalumeau: written E3–F#4 [D3–E4, 50–64] — dark, muted, dramatic, "deep and rich"; unlike anything
    else in the orchestra; used for melodies and accompaniments.
  - Throat tones (sonidos de garganta): written G4–Bb4 [F4–Ab4, 65–68] — rather pale, dull.
  - Clarino: written B4–C6 [A4–Bb5, 69–82] — bright, incisive, warm, expressive.
  - Top: written ~C#6–G6 [B5–F6, ~83–89] — "piercing, shrill".

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] Exposed melodies sitting on the throat tones (written G4–Bb4); use them moderately.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Clarino melody over chalumeau accompaniment within the same section (Mozart Sym. 39, Trio).
- R3 [ALLOWED] All major and minor trills.
- R4 [PREFER] Tremolos: excellent in chalumeau up to a M6 (9 st); in clarino limit to P4 (5 st), and to
  M3 (4 st) above the staff.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Widest attack palette of the reeds: anything from explosive sforzando to an entry from
  niente (fade-in from silence).
- R6 [PREFER] Treat as the most versatile woodwind: melody or accompaniment figures in any register
  (Borodin, *Prince Igor* overture: combined-register accompaniment).
- Style fact: late arrival (Mannheim); many Haydn/Mozart symphonies have no clarinets; standard "a 2"
  from the early 19th c.

**Agent applicability**
- For niente swells or pp entries in a wind chord, give the soft entry to clarinet (CC1 ramp from 0).
- Low dark melody (D3–E4 concert): clarinet chalumeau (Tchaikovsky 5 opening, two clarinets low).
- Sample libraries play concert pitch; only convert to written pitch when producing parts.

### Bass clarinet (clarinete bajo) (scan p.23)
- Two notations: **French** = treble clef, sounds **M9 (14 st) lower**; **German** = bass clef, sounds
  **M2 (2 st) lower**.
- Range (Fig. 27, French notation): written C3 (instruments vary: Eb3 / E3 bottom) – G6 (C7 extreme) →
  **sounding Bb1 (34) / Db2 (37) / D2 (38) – F5 (77)** (Bb5/82 extreme).
- R7 [PREFER] Orchestral role (fixed member from ~1850): bass lines together with bassoon or double bass.
- R8 [ALLOWED] Soft attacks and pp in the low register (which the bassoon cannot do well; see 5.5 R2).

**Tags:** #instrument/bass-clarinet #bass-line

## 5.5 Bassoon (fagot) (scan p.24–28)

**Concepts**
- Maple wood; bell, long joint, wing joint (tubo), small/boot joint, metal crook (bocal), **double reed**.
- Notated in bass and tenor clef (like the cello).
- No sharply defined timbral boundaries between registers. Range per Adler chart (Fig. 29):
  **Bb1 (34) – ~Eb5 (75)**. Approximate register map (boundary pitches hard to read [?]):
  - ~Bb1–G2: "sonorous, dark, vibrant".
  - ~A2–C4: "sweet, more subdued, but expressive".
  - ~D4/E4–Bb4: "thin, but intense".
  - ~A4/B4–Eb5: "thin and often pinched".

**Register / dynamics behaviour**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Low register: sonorous, vibrant, a bit rough, rich in overtones.
- R2 [AVOID] Soft attacks or pp in the low register (hard on bassoon; use bass clarinet instead).
- R3 [AVOID] Leaving middle-register bassoon unsupported under heavy scoring: it is softer there and can
  be neutralised by other instruments.
- R4 [PREFER] For tender, delicate lines use the **upper part of the middle register** (weak, little
  power, but gently expressive).
- R5 [ALLOWED] High register: intense, penetrating, even rough — used deliberately for a strained,
  primitive effect (Stravinsky, *Rite of Spring* opening solo).
- R6 [PREFER] Staccato is especially effective (Dukas, *The Sorcerer's Apprentice*).

**Orchestral role / style facts**
- Baroque: continuo bass with cello or keyboard; also melodic in chamber groups of 2 oboes + bassoon
  (+ continuo). The minuet's "trio" originally meant this 2 oboes + bassoon interlude against the tutti.
- Before Mozart: bass line in the low register; from the last two decades of the 18th c. higher parts;
  19th c. frequent solos.

**Agent applicability**
- Comic/light staccato bass or mid lines: bassoon short articulation.
- Woodwind chords: bassoon = bass of the woodwind choir (see flute R9).

**Tags:** #orchestration/woodwinds #instrument/bassoon #registers

### Contrabassoon (contrafagot) (scan p.27–28)
- **Sounds one octave lower than written** (written an octave higher than sounding).
- Range (Fig. 34, sounding): **Bb0 (22) – A3 (57)**, top extreme ~C4 (60); a note one step below Bb0
  (~A0) is also printed [?] (extended instruments).
- Thicker than bassoon; very low notes have audible slow vibrations.
- R9 [MUST] Give the part regular rests: low notes consume a lot of breath.
- Test Q10 (inferred): it is the bassoon family's auxiliary instrument and is *not* written at sounding pitch.

**Tags:** #instrument/contrabassoon #bass-line #transposition

## Cross-instrument summary for agents (inferred from the above)
- Dynamics vs register: flute weak low / strong high; oboe strong low / thin high; bassoon rough low,
  soft middle, intense high; clarinet even and controllable everywhere (best for pp/niente).
- Blend: flute + clarinet + bassoon blend in chords; oboe sticks out (solo colour).
- Bass-line woodwinds: bassoon, bass clarinet (soft entries), contrabassoon (rests needed).
