## 10 La trompeta — introduction and family (scan p.330–331, printed p.325–326)

**Concepts**
- Trumpet (tromba / trompette / Trompete): soprano and most agile member of the brass family.
- Family pictured: piccolo trumpet, trumpet in D, C, Bb, Eb, bass trumpet in Bb, cornet in Bb,
  flugelhorn (fiscorno) in Bb.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Use the trumpet for very high passages, loud or soft, and for passages exploiting the whole
  range at several dynamic levels.
- R2 [PREFER] Trumpet (fast or slow passages) is the composers' favoured colour for an aura of
  anticipation or agitation (anticipación o agitación).

## La trompeta natural — natural trumpet (scan p.331–334, printed p.326–329)

**Concepts**
- Natural trumpet: valveless, like the natural horn; fundamental set by tube length; brilliant timbre.
- Clarino art (arte de la interpretación del clarino): Baroque high-register virtuoso trumpet playing,
  rivalling oboe and violin parts.
- Crooks (varas de entonación): inserted tubing that pitches the natural trumpet in a key. Unlike horn
  crooks they carry no alto/basso designation.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] [era: Baroque clarino, c.1700–1750] Natural trumpet parts may run in fast diatonic
  16th-note lines in the high register, up to the 16th partial.
- R2 [PREFER] [era: Classical, c.1750–1820] After the clarino died out, keep the natural trumpet
  accompanimental: long tonic/dominant pedal notes and chord-tone (arpeggio/fanfare) figures in tutti.
  *Why:* the new homophonic diatonic melodies would sound too penetrating and strident on the trumpet.
- R3 [AVOID] [era: Classical] Writing above the 12th partial (Baroque went to the 16th).
- R4 [NEVER] [era: Classical natural trumpet] Partials 1–2; only the F trumpet could attempt them and even
  there they were unreliable.
- R5 Available notes, Classical natural trumpet in C (written = sounding), partials 3–12:
  G3 (55), C4 (60), E4 (64), G4 (67), Bb4 (70, partial 7), C5 (72), D5 (74), E5 (76), F/F#5 (11th partial,
  between 77 and 78), G5 (79). For another crook, transpose by the crook interval (table below).
- R6 [AVOID] [era: natural trumpet] Relying on partial 7 (always very flat; lipped up) and partial 11
  (between F and F#; must be lipped by the player). Hand-in-bell correction is impossible on the trumpet.
- R7 [ERA FACT] Crook transpositions (sounding vs written C): F = P4 up (+5), E = M3 up (+4), Eb = m3 up (+3),
  D = M2 up (+2), C = as written (0); B = m2 down (−1), Bb = M2 down (−2), A = m3 down (−3). Some Classical
  works ask for G or Ab trumpets; players used the common crooks and adjusted.
- R8 [ERA FACT] Missing notes forced odd voice-leading: e.g. trumpet 2 leaps a major 9th because the C
  trumpet could neither play nor double any note of the D-minor chord (Beethoven 6/v, bars 221–222).
- R9 [ERA FACT] Classical concertos (Haydn, Hummel) still exploited the full trumpet potential, unlike
  orchestral parts.
- R10 [ERA FACT] Classical and early-Romantic orchestras used the natural horn much more than the natural
  trumpet, probably because the horn is less penetrating even high up. The cornet (from the post-horn family,
  wider bore) sounded much sweeter for the same reason.
- R11 [ERA FACT] Berlioz, *Symphonie fantastique*: dull parts for the 2 trumpets, interesting chromatic parts
  for the 2 (already valved) cornets.

**Examples**
- Bach, Cantata 51, last mvt "Alleluja" (C tpt/clarino, f): long continuous 16th-note runs in the high
  register; the typical clarino part.
- Haydn, Symphony 94 "Surprise", iv, bars 249–268 (C tpt, ff): sustained tied pedal notes and simple chord tones.
- Beethoven, Symphony 3 "Eroica", i, bars 37–45 (Eb tpts a2, ff/sf): tonic-triad notes in quarter/half notes.
- Mozart, Piano Concerto K.503, iii, bars 24–32 (C tpt, f): two-part fanfare rhythms on chord tones, repeated
  16th figures.
- Beethoven, Symphony 6, v, bars 219–223 (2 C tpts, ff): the "unclassical" major-9th leap in tpt 2 (R8).
- Beethoven, Symphony 5, ii, bars 147–158 (C tpt, ff → pp): more idiomatic writing; dotted fanfare in thirds
  with trill-like figures.
- Beethoven, Symphony 5, iv, bars 1–6 (C tpt, ff): C-major triad fanfare, two-part.
- Beethoven, *Leonore* Overture 3, bars 295–300 (Bb tpt, offstage, "freely"): the brilliant fanfare call with
  repeated-note rhythms ending on a fermata.
- Tchaikovsky, *Capriccio Italien*, bars 1–7 (2 E tpts, ff → p): late natural-trumpet-style fanfare on
  arpeggio notes, accents.
- Mendelssohn, *Midsummer Night's Dream*, Wedding March, bars 1–5 (3 C tpts a3, f): repeated-note triplet
  fanfare on one pitch, then chordal triplets.

**Agent applicability**
- For a Classical-period pastiche, restrict each trumpet to the crook's partials 3–12 (R5/R7), keep it on
  tonic/dominant pedals and tutti fanfares, and accept gaps in the voice-leading (R8).
- For a Baroque pastiche, use a D (or C) trumpet in the clarino register with fast diatonic 16ths.

## La trompeta de válvulas — valved F trumpet (scan p.334–335, printed p.329–330)

**Concepts**
- Early valved trumpet (mid-19th c.): natural F trumpet plus 3 valves, fully chromatic from the 3rd to the
  12th partial.

**Rules**
- R1 [ERA FACT] [era: valved F trumpet, c.1850–1900] Range: written C#3–G5, sounding F#3 (54)–C6 (84);
  transposes a P4 up (+5).
- R2 [PREFER] [era: late Romantic] For broad, noble melodies (Mahler, Bruckner, R. Strauss) the F trumpet's
  big sound dominates the orchestra; these melodies sound more impressive on it.
- R3 [ERA FACT] Its projection swamped the other brass, so most composers dropped it around 1900 in favour
  of the smaller valved C and Bb trumpets.
- R4 [ERA FACT] Until mid-19th c. composers still wrote in crook keys; later players probably played those
  parts on the valved F trumpet. R. Strauss (revising Berlioz's treatise) advised writing trumpet parts in C
  and letting the player transpose for the instrument chosen.

**Examples**
- Bruckner, Symphony 7, i, bars 233–241 (F tpts 1–2 and 3, ff → fff): marcato accented lines in E major;
  upper pair on long chorale notes, tpt 3 in descending chromatic/arpeggio lines.
- Mahler, Symphony 2, i, bars 192–197 (4 F tpts zu 2, ff): triplet arpeggio upbeat leading to accented
  long notes, with crescendo/sudden p.

## La trompeta moderna — modern C and Bb trumpets (scan p.335–337, printed p.330–332)

**Concepts**
- Modern orchestral standard: small Bb and C trumpets (brighter sound, more agility, higher tessitura).
  Also used: trumpet in D and piccolo trumpet in Bb, for Baroque music and modern high writing.

**Rules — ranges (Ex. 10-45)**
- R1 C trumpet (non-transposing): F#3 (54)–C6 (84).
- R2 Bb trumpet: written F#3–D6; sounds a M2 lower (−2): E3 (52)–C6 (84).
- R3 [AVOID] Writing above the top of these ranges. Many players go higher, but it is risky (footnote; the
  translation labels the limit "DO5", which matches the written C6 at the top of the example only if read as
  the high C of the example [?]).
- R4 [MUST] Write the Bb, D and piccolo trumpets transposed; the C trumpet as sounding.
- R5 Whatever trumpet the score names, the player chooses the instrument for the passage (many pros own a D
  trumpet for high passages; most amateurs own only a Bb).

**Rules — timbre and register**
- R6 C trumpet: smaller, brighter, more concentrated sound, easier high notes; preferred by some composers.
- R7 Bb trumpet: slightly "thicker" and richer, excellent low register; standard in bands, jazz and the orchestra.
- R8 [ERA FACT] Bb trumpets once had a small slide to switch to A; unreliable, abandoned.
- R9 Register colours (Ex. 10-46, written, C/Bb): F#3–B3 rather dull/muffled (bastante apagado);
  C4 to about A5 [?] clear, brilliant, more articulate; about B5–D6 brilliant but strident.
- R10 [PREFER] Loud rather than very soft writing, especially high: loud is far easier than pp. In the low
  register pp is also hard to control; it is safer to write loud, even very loud, than soft.
- R11 [PREFER] Middle register, middle C (C4) to Bb5 written (book: "SIb5"), is the most practical for all
  players; professionals play every dynamic there.
- R12 [AVOID] Soft dynamics anywhere in the range for non-professional players.

**Rules — valves and fingering (C trumpet chart)**
- R13 Valve 1 lowers the open note a whole tone (2 semitones), valve 2 a semitone (1), valve 3 a tone and a
  half (3); combinations add: 2 = −1, 1 = −2, 3 or 1+2 = −3, 2+3 = −4, 1+3 = −5, 1+2+3 = −6.
- R14 Open notes (written, C trumpet): C4, G4, C5, E5, G5, with C6 [?] and higher partials in parentheses.
  Each valve combination gives the same partial series transposed down (inferred from the chart).

**Examples**
- Copland, *Outdoor Overture*, bars 16–31 (Bb tpt solo, mp, "freely, with natural expression"): lyrical
  20th-c. solo covering the whole range, wide leaps, triplets, ending low and p.

**Additional passages:** one-line pointer: Bartók *Concerto for Orchestra* v; Schoenberg *Five Pieces* no.2;
Shostakovich Sym. 1 ii and Sym. 5 finale; Stravinsky *Renard*, *The Nightingale*.

**Agent applicability**
- Default orchestral trumpet track: sounding range E3 (52)–C6 (84) for Bb, F#3 (54)–C6 (84) for C. Keep
  melodic writing mostly in 60–80; above ~83 expect strident, loud-only notes.
- Map soft dynamics carefully: avoid pp/ppp in the extremes; low trumpet notes at mf+ sound dull, not loud.

## Articulación y picado — articulation and tonguing (scan p.337–338, printed p.332–333)

**Rules**
- R1 The trumpet is the fastest-phrasing brass instrument.
- R2 Notes under a slur are played in one breath (legato); unslurred notes are tongued (picado).
- R3 [ALLOWED] Fast slurred and tongued passages of every kind; single, double and triple tonguing
  (picado sencillo, doble, triple) are used constantly.

**Examples**
- Puccini, *La Bohème* Act II opening (3 F tpts, marcatissimo): accented three-part chords in fast repeated
  rhythms; double tonguing only on the 16th-note pairs.
- Verdi, *Aida* Act I, "Celeste Aida" bars 1–13 (2 C tpts, ff): repeated-note triplets (triple tonguing)
  alternating with dotted fanfare figures.

**Additional passages:** one-line pointer: Bruckner 7 iii; Dukas *Sorcerer's Apprentice*; Ravel *Daphnis*
Suite 2; Rimsky-Korsakov *Capriccio espagnol* iii; Rossini *William Tell* Ov.; Shostakovich 1 i;
R. Strauss *Till*; Wagner *Tannhäuser* II.

**Agent applicability**
- Fast repeated notes (16ths, triplets) are idiomatic for trumpet at f–ff; in MIDI use short, accented,
  evenly spaced notes rather than legato overlap.

## Trompeta con sordina — muted trumpet (scan p.338–340, printed p.333–335)

**Concepts**
- con sordino / senza sordino; [era: late-19th-c. German scores] *gestopft* ("stopped") for muted and
  *offen* for open; recent scores often write "open".
- Mute types: straight (sordina recta, default), plunger, harmon (wa-wa), bucket, solotone, cup, whispa.
- "In the hat" / "in the stand" (en el sombrero / en el atril): playing into a plastic or felt hat held by
  the hand, or very close to the music stand.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] When the score just says "mute", the orchestral player uses the straight mute unless another is named.
- R2 The trumpet straight mute does not change pitch/transposition (unlike the horn's), so no correction is
  needed; like the horn mute it softens the sound and changes the colour.
- R3 [NEVER] Hand-stopping on the trumpet: impossible.
- R4 [ALLOWED] The hand holds the plunger (strongly softens the sound) or moves near the harmon stem to make
  the jazzy "wa-wa" effect.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Bucket, solotone, cup and whispa mutes came from dance/theatre bands and now appear in recent
  symphonic repertoire; players also invent their own mutes.

**Examples**
- Mahler, Symphony 1, iv, bars 623–625 vs 592–594 (4 F tpts): same triplet figure first open fff, then
  "mit Dämpfer" mf, a half step lower, ending pp; shows the muted colour versus open.
- Debussy, *Nocturnes* "Fêtes", bars 124–131 (3 F tpts con sord., pp): the distant-march fanfare in
  triplets, three parts.
- Gershwin, *Rhapsody in Blue*, bars 16–19 (Bb tpt, harmon "wa-wa" mute, mf, "jazzy"): accented blues
  figures.

**Additional passages:** one-line pointer: Bartók *Concerto for Orchestra* ii; Stravinsky *Petrushka* tableau 2.

**Agent applicability**
- Muted trumpet pp is the standard "distant fanfare" colour; use a muted-trumpet patch rather than lowering
  velocity on an open patch. Harmon/wa-wa = jazz idiom.

**Tags:** #orchestration/brass/trumpet #orchestration/mutes

## Efectos especiales — special effects (scan p.340–341, printed p.335–336)

**Rules — trills (trinos)**
- R1 Most trumpet trills are valve trills; some lip trills are possible in the high register.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Trills changing one valve: very playable.
- R3 [AVOID] Trills changing two valves: more forced.
- R4 [AVOID] Trills involving all three valves, especially in the low register: very hard; avoid.
  Ex. 10-53 (written, low register; pitches identified mainly from the fingerings [?]): A3–Bb3 (2+3 ↔ 1),
  C4–Db4 (open ↔ 1+2+3), B3–C#4 (2 ↔ 1+2+3).

**Rules — glissandos**
- R5 [ALLOWED] Upward glissando, effective only in the highest register where partials lie close together.
- R6 [ALLOWED] [era: recent/20th c.] Bending a note down a quarter tone or semitone (downward "fall"); used
  only by recent composers.

**Agent applicability**
- Write trumpet glissandos only upward and only to high notes (written ~G5 and up, inferred from the
  example); a downward bend is a short pitch-bend at the end of a note.

## Trompetas auxiliares — D, Eb and piccolo trumpets (scan p.341–342, printed p.336–337)

**Rules**
- R1 D trumpet: small, brilliant, very high and piercing timbre; range and technique similar to C and Bb.
- R2 [AVOID] The low register of the D trumpet (below middle C): not very practical. D and Eb trumpets are
  normally used in the high register.
- R3 D trumpet: written F#3–D6, sounds a M2 higher (+2): G#3 (56)–E6 (88).
- R4 Eb trumpet: written F#3–D6, sounds a m3 higher (+3): A3 (57)–F6 (89).
- R5 Piccolo trumpet in Bb: written F#3–A5, sounds a m7 higher (+10): E4 (64)–G6 (91). Effective range per
  text "SIb3 to LA5" (written; sounding Ab4 (68)–G6 (91)).
- R6 [PREFER] Today Baroque clarino parts are played on D or Eb trumpets or the Bb piccolo.

**Examples**
- Stravinsky, *Rite of Spring* Part I, "Ritual of the Rival Tribes", bars 38–42 (D tpt, sf/f): angular high
  figures across changing meters (4/4, 3/4).
- Bach, *Christmas Oratorio* no.64 (final chorale intro) (D tpt, f): clarino 16th-note runs with trills.
- d'Indy, Symphony in Bb, iv, bars 336–343 (Eb tpt, mf → ff/sffz): high lyrical line with accents.

**Agent applicability**
- For a bright high trumpet lead above ~C6 concert, choose a D/Eb/piccolo patch; keep the piccolo above
  ~Ab4 (68) concert.

## La corneta — cornet (scan p.342–343, printed p.337–338)

**Concepts**
- Cornet (corneta): valved since its invention (early 19th c.), so composers wrote chromatic parts for it
  before the valved trumpet was accepted. Looks like a modern trumpet, cup mouthpiece. Bore two-thirds conical,
  one-third cylindrical: sweeter tone, a hybrid between horn and trumpet.

**Rules**
- R1 Transposition standardized in Bb; same range as the Bb trumpet: written F#3–D6, sounding E3 (52)–C6 (84).
- R2 As agile as the trumpet and very precise in intonation.
- R3 [ERA FACT] Never a regular member of the symphony orchestra; largely replaced by the brighter valved
  trumpet. Regular in brass bands/ensembles, used (with restraint) in many theatre orchestras, especially in Europe.
- R4 [PREFER] Ideal for military-style music; in many 19th- and 20th-c. scores it gets folk-like melodies.

**Examples**
- Tchaikovsky, *Capriccio Italien*, bars 232–240 (2 cornets in A, mf → f → dim.): folk-like tune in
  parallel thirds/sixths with chordal repeated notes.
- Stravinsky, *Petrushka* tableau 2, "Russian Dance" bars 1–29 (cornet 1 with military drum, mf, sub. p):
  folk-dance solo in running 16ths, military colour.

**Additional passages:** one-line pointer: Berlioz *Fantastique* iv bars 62–69; Prokofiev *Lieutenant Kijé* i.

**Agent applicability**
- For a softer, rounder trumpet-range melody (folk, march, military, theatre/period colour) use a cornet
  patch with Bb-trumpet ranges.
