## 16 El coro — the chorus with orchestra (scan p.659–666, printed p.658–665)

**Concepts**
- Chorus and orchestra (coro y orquesta): a favoured medium since Monteverdi.
- Pitch support (ayuda en la entonación): choir singers are often not professionals, so they need more
  help finding and holding pitch than soloists do.
- Doubling (duplicación) of the choir: instruments playing the vocal lines colla parte, at unison or
  octaves.
- Accompanying figure (figura acompañante): an ostinato/figuration under the choir that supplies
  harmony and rhythm without covering it.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Give a choir more pitch support than a soloist: doubling, clear harmony, or a supporting
  figure. *Why:* choirs are often amateur and tune less securely.
- R2 [ALLOWED] In Baroque and Classical writing, a choir that is not doubled can take its pitches from
  the orchestra's clear (diatonic) harmony. [era: Baroque/Classical]
- R3 [PREFER] As the harmony grows more complex and the orchestra larger, add explicit orientation:
  doublings, pitch cues, supporting figures. [era: Romantic → 20th c.]
- R4 [PREFER] Baroque model: the continuo (organ + bass, bassoon) gives the singers the harmony at all
  times; upper instruments enter only to point up some starts and ends of fugal phrases.
  [era: Baroque]
- R5 [ALLOWED] Classical model: double the whole choir with the orchestra (each voice has at least one
  instrument colla parte). [era: Classical]
- R6 [PREFER] When doubling the whole choir, vary the doubling between phrases, e.g. move the flute from
  the soprano line to the tenor two octaves higher. *Why:* gives variety to the accompaniment.
- R7 [MUST] With a large modern orchestra, keep the choir audible by three means (Orff model):
  (a) frequent doubling of the vocal lines, especially at the start;
  (b) writing every choral part in its most favourable register;
  (c) an accompanying figure that does not obscure the choir but supports its intonation and rhythm.
- R8 [PREFER] Study scores of choral-orchestral works to learn accompaniment of soloists and choirs;
  operatic choir + orchestra models: *Fidelio*, *Der Freischütz*, *Carmen*, *Otello*, *Turandot*,
  especially *Boris Godunov* and *Wozzeck*.

**Examples**
- Ex. 16-23 — J. S. Bach, Cantata BWV 21 "Ich hatte viel Bekümmernis", opening chorus, bars 1–12
  (C minor; Ob, Vln 1–2, Vla, SATB, Bsn, organ/continuo with figured bass). Bars 1–3: block-chord
  "Ich, ich, ich" exclamations with the upper strings/oboe in short chords around them [exact
  alignment ?]. Then fugal entries (soprano bar 2, tenor bar 3, alto bar 5, bass bar 6 [approx.]) are
  carried by the continuo bass line alone; Ob/strings/Bsn re-enter for a bar or so at phrase ends
  (bars 5–6, 8–9, 11–12) with short cadential figures. Shows R4.
- Ex. 16-24 — Haydn, *The Creation* No. 13 "Die Himmel erzählen", bars 1–12 (C major, Allegro, alla
  breve; 2 Fl, 2 Ob, 2 C Cl, 2 Bsn, Hn and Tpt in C, Alt/Ten/Bass Trb, Cbsn, Timp, strings, SATB).
  Phrase 1 (bars 1–4, *f*): Fl a2 double the soprano an octave up; Ob + Cl double soprano/alto; Alt/Ten
  Trb double alto/tenor; Bsn, Bass Trb/Cbsn, Vc, Db double the choral bass; Hn, Tpt and Timp hold
  tonic/dominant; violins are silent. Bars 5–8: the choir rests and the violins (in thirds) answer with
  the orchestral continuation. Phrase 2 (bars 8–11, "Und seiner Hände Werk"): Fl now doubles the tenor
  two octaves up; Hn/Tpt/Trb play repeated staccato quarter chords; a tutti *fz* on "Werk"; Vla keeps an
  independent line while violins rest. Shows R5–R6, and trombones colla parte with alto/tenor/bass
  [era: Classical church/oratorio practice].
- Ex. 16-25 — Orff, *Carmina Burana* "O Fortuna", bars 1–14 (D minor). Bars 1–4 (Pesante, half = 60):
  *ff* tutti (3 Fl, 2 Ob, EH, Eb/Bb Cl, 2 Bsn, Cbsn, 4 Hn, 3 Tpt, 3 Trb + Tuba, Timp, cymbal, 2 pianos,
  strings) in accented block chords doubling the SATB choir, closing on a fermata with timpani roll and
  cymbal *ff*. From bar 5 (half = 120–132), sudden *pp*: the choir sings repeated-note half/quarter
  lines in its comfortable middle register; Ob 1–2 and EH double the choral melody; Hn 1–4 hold pedal
  tones; Db holds the tonic pedal; Bsn, both pianos (staccato bass ostinato with low grace-note
  octaves), Vla and Vc play a repeating staccato figure; Vln 1–2 play off-beat quarters; timpani
  repeat accented half notes *pp*. Shows R7 (a)–(c).

**Agent applicability**
- When writing SATB choir with orchestra, give every vocal line at least one instrument doubling at
  unison or octave at entries and after rests (inferred from R1, R7a).
- Classical-style chorus: Ob/Cl on soprano/alto, Fl an octave above soprano, Alt/Ten/Bass Trb on
  alto/tenor/bass, Bsn + Vc + Db (+ Cbsn) on the choral bass; Hn/Tpt/Timp on tonic–dominant
  sustains; free the violins for answers between choral phrases (Ex. 16-24).
- Re-voice the doubling in the next phrase (e.g. Fl jumps to the tenor two octaves up) instead of
  repeating it (R6).
- Baroque-style fugal chorus: continuo (organ + bass + Bsn) under all entries; upper strings/oboe only
  on phrase starts and cadences (Ex. 16-23).
- Modern big-orchestra chorus: alternate *ff* tutti that doubles the choir with long *pp* stretches
  where a low, staccato, repetitive ostinato (pianos, low strings, bassoons, timpani) plus a pedal and
  one reed doubling of the melody carries the choir (Ex. 16-25).
- Keep choral parts in their favourable registers; check them against the vault's voice-range data
  before rendering (R7b; ranges not given in this range).
