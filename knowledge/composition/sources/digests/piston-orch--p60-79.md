## The Violin — harmonics, continued (printed p.60)

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Harmonic glissando on one string, muted, legato, ppp, as a rippling colour (Ex.65). Only the natural-harmonic nodes sound, no portamento between them (links to p.40–59 digest R65).
- R2 [ALLOWED] An arpeggio across the strings in harmonics gives an unusual, delicate effect (Ex.66).
- R3 [ALLOWED] Artificial harmonic with the light finger a P5 (7) above the stopped note sounds a 12th (19 semitones) above the stopped note. Rarely used orchestrally; some instances in modern music (Ex.67). Compare the usual touch-a-P4 harmonic, which sounds two octaves (24) above (standard practice, inferred from earlier pages).

**Examples**
- Ex.65 Stravinsky, *L'Oiseau de feu* (Vn II, Molto moderato, con sord., legato, "gliss. sul D", ppp): continuous wave-like 32nd-note harmonic glissandi up and down the D string's natural harmonics.
- Ex.66 Ravel, *L'Heure espagnole* (Vn I div., Presque lent): upper half plays slurred harmonic arpeggios across the strings, p; lower half holds a pp chord.
- Ex.67 Bartók, *Dance Suite* (Vn II, 2 soli, pp): artificial harmonics with the touched note a fifth above, sounding a 12th up.

**Agent applicability**
- Harmonic-glissando "shimmer" layers: only when the library has a harmonic-gliss or FX patch. Do not imitate with pitch-bend portamento (the real effect has no audible slide).

## First and second violins (primeros y segundos violines) (printed p.60–63)

**Concepts**
- A string part is played by a section (e.g. 16 first violins), unlike one player per wind/brass part. The two violin sections far outnumber any other group though they look equal to other staves in the score.

**Rules**
- R4 [ALLOWED] Vn II technical demands are close to Vn I; give them comparably difficult material when needed.
- R5 [PREFER] One of the commonest Vn II functions: double Vn I at the unison or octave for weight.
- R6 [MUST] [era: Classical orchestra, few violins] Vn II doubling Vn I was necessary to balance horns and trumpets with the small number of violins available.
- R7 [PREFER] Modern: even double the classical number of violins cannot match a greatly expanded brass section; double the violins (or thin the brass) in loud tutti.
- R8 [PREFER] Vn I is normally the top voice of the strings and carries most of the prominent thematic material.
- R9 [ALLOWED] Vn II may carry a secondary melody, replace Vn I for variety, or play accompaniment figures alone or with Vn I or other strings.
- R10 [ALLOWED] Split one fast line between Vn I and Vn II in alternating fragments that overlap at a unison at each joint: each part is simpler and the line gains security and energy (Ex.68).
- R11 [ALLOWED] Divided violins can play in parts unaccompanied, even reduced to a few solo players (Ex.69).

**Exceptions / conditions**
- Seating [era: mid-20th c. US seating]: Vn I on the conductor's left at the stage edge, instruments turned to the audience. Vn II on the right (traditional) face away and lose some sound; massed beside Vn I they are dominated by them. Either way the antiphony a composer imagines between Vn I and Vn II is much reduced in practice.

**Examples**
- Ex.68 Brahms, Symphony 4 (Allegro energico, f): Vn I and Vn II trade 16th/triplet fragments of one line, meeting on shared unison notes.
- Ex.69 Liszt, *A Faust Symphony* (Andante soave, p): four solo first violins alone; the top plays legato 16ths (sempre legato e dolce), the others have a dolce espress. countermelody and sustained lower lines.

**Agent applicability**
- Hocket-style split of a busy line: write Vn I and Vn II as alternating half-bar (or beat) fragments, each starting on the last note of the other (shared unison), instead of one exhausting part.
- Do not rely on left/right Vn I–Vn II antiphony for clarity unless the library/pan places Vn II on the right; even then keep it as a colour, not a structural device.
- For a chamber-like moment, a separate "solo violins" track (2–4 players) differs from a section patch.

## Solo violin (violín solo) (printed p.63–64)

**Rules**
- R12 [ALLOWED] 19th–20th-c. scores often contain solo violin parts for the concertmaster, from occasional phrases (Wagner, Mahler) to concerto-like obbligato (R. Strauss, *Ein Heldenleben*).
- R13 [PREFER] Choose a solo violin for its intimate, intense, personal tone, not only for less volume; the full section sounds formal and impersonal by comparison.
- R14 [ALLOWED] A solo violin with an independent part carries surprisingly well in a large orchestra. *Why:* two players on one part do not give twice the sound of one; slight pitch differences dull overtones and average out expression, so a soloist is not 1/16 of the section.
- R15 [PREFER] Write the solo part for the soloist's attitude: assertive, projecting with full vigour and authority (versus orchestral style, where the player blends into the mass, and chamber style, individual but equal with partners).

**Agent applicability**
- Use a solo-violin patch (not a section at low velocity) for intimate or featured lines; it can sit at moderate CC1 and still cut through.
- Unison doubling adds weight, not loudness in proportion; don't stack many unison tracks to "make it louder".

## Chapter Three — The Viola (alto, viola, Bratsche): construction (printed p.65–66)

**Concepts**
- Size is not standardized. Hypothetical norm: body 16 5/8 in, neck 6 1/16 in, overall 27 1/4 in, sounding string length 15 1/4 in. Fine violas differ by 1.5–2 in in body length.
- Even the largest violas are too small for a true fifth below the violin; this mismatch largely makes the viola's distinctive tone. Larger instruments are harder to handle, especially in upper positions.
- Bow: thicker and heavier than the violin bow.
- Strings [era: 1955]: lower two wire-wound, upper two plain gut; some players use all four wound, and metal A strings are used.

**Rules**
- R16 [PREFER] Heavier strings speak more reluctantly; tone needs the bow to "dig in". Light, airy bowings (e.g. spiccato/flying strokes) are less natural on viola than on violin. *Why:* string mass.
- R17 [ALLOWED] Light bowings are not forbidden, but sound effective only with skilled players on good instruments.

**Agent applicability**
- Viola short articulations: prefer weightier détaché/staccato over feather-light spiccato for fast airy figures; give those to violins.

**Tags:** #orchestration/strings/viola #orchestration/instruments

## Viola fingering, positions and upper limit (printed p.66–67)

**Concepts**
- Fingering system identical to the violin, but the string is ~2.5 in longer, so M2/m2 finger spacings are proportionally wider. Needs a large hand and strong fingers, especially the more extended 4th finger.
- Positions (Fig. 28), notes per string (fingers 0–4 / 1–4):
  - First position: C string C3 D3 E3 F3 G3 (48–55); G string G3–D4 (55–62); D string D4–A4 (62–69); A string A4 B4 C5 D5 E5 (69–76). First-position compass C3–E5 (48–76).
  - Third position: 1st finger a P4 above the open string, 4th finger a m7 above it: A string D5–G5 (74–79), D string G4–C5 (67–72), G string C4–F4 (60–65), C string F3–Bb3 (53–58) (read from Fig. 28, cross-checked by computation; accidentals follow the key).
  - Seventh position: 1st finger an octave above the open string, 4th finger a 5th above that by extension: A string A5–E6 (81–88), D string D5–A5 (74–81), G string G4–D5 (67–74), C string C4–G4 (60–67). The extended 4th-finger note is marked as a harmonic (o) at the 1/3-string node (computed from Fig. 28; pitch names partly inferred [?]).

**Rules**
- R18 [AVOID] Long stretches in first position with the left forearm fully extended: tiring.
- R19 [AVOID] Positions above the third are awkward (the hand must get round the viola's shoulder); keep fast or long passages at or below 3rd position when possible.
- R20 [MUST] Practical upper limit for orchestral viola: the harmonic at the extended 4th finger in seventh position on the A string, E6 (88) (computed). Except as harmonics, higher notes are rarely written and are nearly always doubled by violins.
- R21 [ALLOWED] Half position (media posición) is more convenient and more used on viola than on violin, thanks to the wider finger spacing (Ex.71).

**Examples**
- Ex.70 Prokofiev, *Chout* (Va, Moderato, f↔p swells, treble clef): a high, chromatic, rapid line that shows the upper register near the practical limit.
- Ex.71 Brahms, Symphony 2 (Va, Allegro con spirito, pp): a legato line fingered in half position (fingerings 2-4-3-2-1…).

**Agent applicability**
- Viola range check: hard ceiling 88 (E6); treat ~76–81 as the top of comfortable writing, and double anything above ~81 with violins.

## Characteristics of the viola strings (printed p.67–70)

**Rules**
- R25 [ALLOWED] A string: nasal, piercing, penetrating, "sandy" timbre in sharp contrast to the lower three; tends to stand out (Ex.72). A good player balances the change from D to A, but expect prominence.
- R26 [PREFER] D string: unobtrusive and gentle, with more weight than the violin's D. With the G string, the best part of the viola for the many accompaniment figures it is given; also excellent for gentle melodies (Ex.73).
- R27 [ALLOWED] G string: subdued compared with A and C, but richer and warmer than the violin's G (Ex.74).
- R28 [ALLOWED] C string: the only viola string below the violin range; powerful and distinctive, sharply contrasted with the cello's D string at the same pitches; often heard as foreboding or menacing. Vigorous détaché in the lower half of the bow gives solidity and energy to a string unison (Ex.75).
- R29 [ALLOWED] The C string is also capable of soft, delicate tones, e.g. pp accompaniment figures for divided violas (Ex.76).

**Examples**
- Ex.72 Shostakovich, Symphony 5 (Va, Moderato, p espr.): a long, high A-string melody in treble clef, then back to alto clef; shows the A string's penetrating character.
- Ex.73 Brahms, Symphony 3 (Va, Allegro, mezza voce, p grazioso): a flowing D-string melody; the violas double the oboe at the octave for two bars, then the flute, for the rest of the phrase.
- Ex.74 Debussy, *Nocturnes: Nuages* (solo Va, p espressif → più p): a quiet, expressive solo line low on the instrument (G-string colour).
- Ex.75 Bartók, Concerto for Orchestra (Presto, ff): Vn I and II on the G string (IV), violas on the C string and cellos play one 16th-note figure in octaves/unison; violas dig in on the C string.
- Ex.76 R. Strauss, *Tod und Verklärung* (Va div., Allegro, pp): two halves of the violas in interlocking slurred triplet/eighth figures low on the instrument.

**Agent applicability**
- Viola register colours: 69–88 (A string) bright, cutting; 62–69 (D) soft accompaniment and gentle melody; 55–62 (G) warm; 48–55 (C) dark, menacing in loud détaché, delicate at pp.
- For a heavy string unison riff, put the violas low (C string, 48–55) under violins on the G string and cellos.

## Melodic uses of the viola (printed p.70)

**Rules**
- R30 [ALLOWED] Middle-of-the-strings position makes the viola the busiest member: own melodies, plus doubling violins at the octave or unison, or doubling cellos, even basses.
- R31 [PREFER] The viola's character suits singing melody more than agile figuration.

**Agent applicability**
- Default viola roles in generated string writing: inner harmony, doubling Vn at the lower octave or cellos at the upper octave; give it lyrical rather than virtuosic melodic solos.

## Double notes and divisi in violas (printed p.70–73)

**Concepts**
- Harmonic filling (relleno armónico) has always been a large share of viola work, often written as double notes.

**Rules**
- R32 [ALLOWED] [era: Classical and later] Classical viola parts abound in double notes, often with no indication whether to divide; nearly all are playable as double stops by good players.
- R33 [PREFER] Divided performance is neater and better in tune than double stops.
- R34 [MUST] Two simultaneous notes both lying on the C string (both below G3, 55) must be played divisi (Ex.79).
- R35 [MUST] [era: modern, 1955] Mark every double-note passage div. or non div. explicitly.
- R36 [ALLOWED] Divided violas can carry an important accompaniment alone (Ex.77).
- R37 [ALLOWED] Avoiding double notes in the violas keeps a texture transparent and fleet (Mozart, *Magic Flute* overture); three- and four-note tremolo chords in the violas make a tutti massively vibrant (Ex.78).
- R38 [ALLOWED] [era: Haydn–Beethoven orchestra] Divided violas were thought enough to balance Vn I + Vn II combined. Section sizes then: at most 5 violas against 6–10 first and 6–10 second violins. Modern orchestra: usually 12 violas.
- R39 [ALLOWED] Divided violas often combine with winds, e.g. doubling an oboe pair in thirds (Ex.80).
- R40 [ALLOWED] Dividing violas is widespread but not universal: some scores give violas two staves throughout (Ravel, *Daphnis et Chloé*); others rarely divide (Stravinsky, Symphony in C).

**Examples**
- Ex.77 Mozart, Symphony in G minor K.550 opening (Va div., Allegro molto, p): the violas alone play the repeated eighth-note accompaniment in two parts (mostly thirds/sixths [?]) under the theme.
- Ex.78 Beethoven, Violin Concerto tutti (Allegro ma non troppo, ff): Vn II and divided violas in measured tremolo; the violas hold 3–4-note chords on the lower strings while Vn I has the tune and cellos/basses the bass. Held wind chords omitted.
- Ex.79 Wagner, *Parsifal* Prelude (Va, Sehr langsam, poco f): violas in double notes (mostly thirds); where both notes fall on the C string, divisi is required.
- Ex.80 Mozart, Symphony in C K.200 (Allegro spiritoso, p): oboes in thirds doubled at the unison by divided violas, staccato repeated quarters and grace-note figures; violins only add short slurred fragments.

**Agent applicability**
- Write viola harmony as two tracks/voices (div.) rather than double stops in samples; double stops in a section patch are fine only for open-spaced fifths/sixths in the middle register.
- Never place two viola notes both under 55 on one voice.
- Classical-style pastiche: small viola section (~5) can still be divided; let divided violas double the oboes in thirds.
- Tutti weight: viola 3–4-note tremolo chords (open-spaced) beneath the melody add vibrancy; omit them for a light, transparent texture.

## Violas as bass (violas como bajo) (printed p.73)

**Rules**
- R41 [PREFER] A light bass in the octave below middle C (C3–B3, 48–59) is sometimes better given to violas than cellos, arco or pizzicato (Ex.81).

**Examples**
- Ex.81 Mahler, Symphony 7 (Grazioso): 3 fl + 3 ob carry the melody mf, Vn I pp; violas p take the bass line, first arco then pizz. "deutlich" (distinctly); cellos/basses give only one pp pizz downbeat.

**Agent applicability**
- For a light, transparent passage without low weight, drop the cellos/basses and give the bass line to violas in 48–59.

## Viola double stops and chords (printed p.73–74)

**Rules**
- R42 [MUST] Maximum stretch 1st→4th finger on one string = P4 (5 semitones) for chords and double stops in the lower positions. Check playability with string/finger diagrams as for violin.
- R43 [PREFER] Three-note chords are more generally useful than four-note chords, unless a fairly heavy effect is wanted.
- R44 [PREFER] Open-spaced chords sound better than close-position chords.
- R45 [PREFER] In tutti, choose double stops and chords using many open strings (C3, G3, D4, A4) for maximum sonority (Ex.82).

**Examples**
- Ex.82 d'Indy, *Symphony on a French Mountain Air* (Va in full tutti, quarter = 54, ff, marcato ^): repeated double stops and 3–4-note chords built on open strings, accented.

**Agent applicability**
- Viola chord check: adjacent strings only; ≤5 semitones between 1st and 4th finger on one string; favour voicings that include open C/G/D/A.

## Viola harmonics (printed p.74–75)

**Rules**
- R46 [ALLOWED] All harmonics are good on the viola, as on the violin.
- R47 [PREFER] Artificial harmonics are seldom written with the stopped note above third-position D on the A string, D5 (74); with the usual touch-a-4th harmonic that sounds up to about D7 (98) (computed).
- R48 [AVOID] Higher artificial harmonics on viola: playable, but there is little reason not to give them to violins. The viola's lower harmonics are the more useful range.
- R49 [ALLOWED] Harmonic glissando: the longer string extends the usable natural series up to the 9th partial, including the 7th (Ex.84). On the C string that series is C3 C4 G4 C5 E5 G5 Bb5(flat) C6 D6 (48, 60, 67, 72, 76, 79, ~82, 84, 86; computed).

**Examples**
- Ex.83 Schoenberg, *Serenade* (Va, Sehr lebhaft, fp): isolated high flageolet (Flag.) harmonics, with the sounding pitches shown in parentheses.
- Ex.84 Stravinsky, *Le Sacre du printemps* (Va div., "glissando sul Do"): both halves sweep up and down the C-string natural harmonics in 32nds, offset; the 7th partial (Bb) is included.

## Viola pizzicato (printed p.75–76)

**Rules**
- R50 [ALLOWED] Longer strings give more resonant pizzicato: viola pizz is slightly rounder, less dry and short than violin pizz in the same register (general rule for the string family).
- R51 [ALLOWED] High A-string pizz above about E–F (E5–F5, 76–77; octave inferred) sounds hard and wooden; can be used on purpose (Ex.85).
- R52 [ALLOWED] Extreme high viola pizz can double winds and harp in unison (Berg, Violin Concerto: harp, 2 fl, 2 cl ff, violas only f).
- R53 [ALLOWED] Pizz position variants: over the fingerboard (Griffbrett, sul tasto) vs near the bridge (Steg) change the tone; little studied by composers but used by players.
- R54 [ALLOWED] Left-hand pizzicato on open strings (marked +) can sound while the bow plays on another string (Ex.87).

**Examples**
- Ex.85 Roussel, *Suite en Fa* (Va pizz., Allegro molto, p, treble clef): a staccato eighth-note line doubling the first oboe at the unison, the first flute an octave above; uses the dry high pizz colour.
- Ex.86 Berg, Violin Concerto (Va pizz., f, Allegro rubato): a fast high pizz line in triplets, partly in treble clef.
- Ex.87 Berg, Violin Concerto (Va, tranquillo, arco mf espress.): bowed melody on the G string with left-hand pizz on the open C and D strings between bow notes.

**Agent applicability**
- A viola pizz doubling an oboe at the unison sharpens a staccato wind line; keep velocity moderate (the source marks the strings one level softer than the winds).
- Left-hand pizz only on open-string pitches (48, 62 here) under a bowed line.

## Ponticello on viola (sul ponticello) (printed p.76–77)

**Rules**
- R55 [PREFER] The viola's tone suits sul ponticello especially well; most often with bowed tremolo (Ex.88) or fingered tremolo (Ex.89).
- R56 [ALLOWED] Players move the bow nearer the bridge in normal playing for more bite and a crisper rhythmic attack; some sul pont. markings mean only this.
- R57 [AVOID] Combining mute and a real ponticello: the mute physically hinders the bow position needed (Ex.90 nevertheless writes con sord. + sul pont.).

**Examples**
- Ex.88 Stravinsky, *L'Oiseau de feu* (Va, Molto moderato, pp sul ponticello): bowed-tremolo double notes moving chromatically.
- Ex.89 Debussy, *Gigues* (Va div., Modéré, pp "sur le chevalet"): fingered (slurred) tremolo between two notes per half, sustained over several bars.
- Ex.90 R. Strauss, *Der Rosenkavalier* (Va, dotted quarter = 80, con sord. pp sul ponticello): rapid 16th-note figures.

**Agent applicability**
- Eerie inner pad: violas divided, pp, sul ponticello tremolo or fingered tremolo (major/minor 2nd–4th) in 55–72.

## Solo viola and extreme divisi (printed p.77–79)

**Rules**
- R58 [ALLOWED] Solo viola is less frequent than solo violin; its pitch and tone are easily covered by accompaniment, so keep the accompaniment very light.
- R59 [ALLOWED] A solo viola can carry a lively, staccato dance melody, not only the usual flowing expressive phrase (Ex.91 vs Ex.74).
- R60 [ALLOWED] Several solo violas (six) can play a close multi-part chorale-like passage over light accompaniment (Ex.92).
- R61 [ALLOWED] Ultimate divisi: violas in 12 parts, one per player (six desks all divisi) (Ex.93).

**Examples**
- Ex.91 Enesco, *Romanian Rhapsody* 2 (solo Va, Vite, mp gaiement): a playful staccato/tenuto dance tune; accompaniment only a held D in the strings, staccato clarinets, harp and two solo violins.
- Ex.92 Stravinsky, *Le Sacre du printemps* (6 viole sole, Andante con moto, molto cantabile ma non f): six slurred lines moving mostly in parallel close harmony, changing meters; accompanied by harmonics and pizz in cellos and basses.
- Ex.93 R. Strauss, *Don Quixote* (Va div., con sord., Langsam): desks 1–2 play ppp triplet bowed-tremolo chords, desks 3–6 pp trilled dyads; a dense, muted, shimmering cluster.

**Agent applicability**
- Solo viola feature: thin the texture to sustained strings pp plus light staccato winds; keep the solo in 55–76.
- Muted multi-divisi viola pads (trem. + trills, pp) give a dense but soft inner colour; in samples layer 2–3 viola tracks with tremolo and trill articulations at low CC1.
