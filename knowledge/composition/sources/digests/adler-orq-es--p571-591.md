## 15 (cont.) Doubling in a chordal texture (duplicación en una textura de acordes) (scan p.571–579, printed p.570–578)

Context (from printed p.569, previous range): the two reasons to double are (1) raise the dynamic level,
(2) add subtle shades of colour. Octave doubling tends to erase each instrument's individual colour, repeated
doublings become tiring, and octave doubling often gives a clearer, more brilliant and louder result.

**Concepts**
- Unison doubling (duplicación al unísono): two or more instruments on the same pitch. When they are the
  same instrument, they are never perfectly in tune with each other.
- Octave doubling (duplicación a la octava): same line at one or more octaves; mainly for power and brilliance.
- Foreground / background (primer plano / plano de fondo): melody vs. harmony layers; orchestration's job in a
  homophonic passage is to keep them perceptually apart.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Doubling taste varies by composer: Rimsky-Korsakov favoured timbral mixtures and large unison
  combinations; Beethoven often octave-doubled to raise the dynamic; Mozart, Mendelssohn, Debussy and Ravel
  preferred unmixed, pure, soloistic melody colours, using octave/unison doublings only occasionally.
  *Why:* doubling is a conscious, taste-dependent choice; decide it from knowledge of scores.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Same-instrument unison doubling (2–4 of the same instrument) produces many inharmonic partials:
  it blurs the upper harmonics, "flattens" the sound and lowers its perceived dynamic. Use it on purpose for
  a warm, slightly unstable, less "clean" line. *Why:* small tuning differences between players.
- R3 [PREFER] When a solo line would sound "too clean" for the intent, replace it with the same instrument
  in unison ×3–4 rather than adding other colours (Mahler 4 flutes). A single instrument would be equally loud,
  only purer. (inferred for MIDI: use a section/ensemble patch or layer several solo patches with slight
  detune/timing offsets.)
- R4 [PREFER] For brilliance and punch (not subtle colour), give the melody to a strong unison pair (e.g. 2
  trumpets a 2) and double the accompanying chords across the whole orchestra in octaves.
- R5 [MUST] Two simultaneous ideas stay distinguishable when written in contrasting timbres AND different
  rhythmic gestures (e.g. sustained melody vs. syncopated chord stabs). Use orchestration to clarify ideas.
- R6 [PREFER] When moving from a unison/octave melodic statement to a dense chordal texture, bridge it with
  pedal notes held in brass and timpani (tonic, dominant in timpani).
- R7 [PREFER] Leave a wide registral gap between the top wind line (1st flute, later both flutes) and the
  strings; separating melody and harmony by colour, register and texture makes foreground/background obvious.
- R8 [ALLOWED] Strings can hold off from chordal playing for a few bars before starting a fast figuration;
  the contrast of texture prepares the figuration's entrance (Schubert).
- R9 [PREFER] When a passage recurs (e.g. a refrain three times), re-orchestrate each return with different
  colours but keep the recognizable texture and the same chord structure. *Why:* variety of timbre with
  unity of structure binds the movement together.
- R10 [ALLOWED] Celestial, soft partial tutti: parallel triads in soft winds/horns/harp, with the bass in
  contrary motion to them in the last fragment.
- R11 [PREFER] Save muted strings (con sordina) for one special sonority (e.g. the final open fifth) instead
  of using them throughout. Remove mutes (Dämpfer ab) right after when needed.
- R12 [ALLOWED] Soft suspended-cymbal roll with sponge mallets (Schwammschlägel; Spanish text says "escobillas"),
  pp, let ring (klingen lassen), under the first bars of a delicate passage.
- R13 [ALLOWED] Piccolo in its LOW register as a soft complement to muted strings (Mahler); harp first in
  thirds then in octaves, blocked (nicht brechen = not arpeggiated), to soften unison writing.
- R14 [PREFER] Close a soft passage on a full triad (fl, cl, bass cl, horn, harp, pp) for warmth; an open
  fifth (empty) sounds hollow. Choose deliberately.
- R15 [ALLOWED] Rare but charming soft colour: flutes + clarinets + muted double basses (arco, pp).
- R16 [PREFER] Very soft brass chords (horns pp, trumpets ppp) can carry parallel harmony under a voice; give
  the harp (with low horns) contrary motion against the brass's parallel chords. Keep one constant colour
  (harp) across contrasting re-scorings of a phrase.
- R17 [PREFER] Dark, strong, majestic tutti colour (Wagner): split the texture into several semi-independent
  parts that constantly trade material between instruments.
- R18 [ALLOWED] Strings may play only a powerful opening chord with multiple stops (double/triple stops),
  plus a strong accent on the melody's first note, then rest until the next bar where they join the wind
  melody. (Strong attack + entry after a short gap.)
- R19 [ALLOWED] Melody in trumpets doubled at the unison by "acoustically compatible" winds (2 oboes + 2
  clarinets) gives a heavy, dark, band-like colour.
- R20 [PREFER] Do not spend the brass's most incisive colour on the first statement of a theme if you want it
  for the climax: give trumpets a secondary harmonic-background role at first, in a register where they can't
  dominate, and the full theme later. *Why:* keeps the climax in reserve.
- R21 [era: mid-19th-c. valve trumpets] Possible secondary reason for Wagner taking the melody from trumpets:
  lack of trust in early valve trumpets for a noble cantabile line (Adler calls this speculation).
- R22 [PREFER] Grandeur of a broad theme: 1st + 2nd violins on the melody, doubled by oboes and clarinets to
  make the strings more incisive.
- R23 [PREFER] Warm, rich inner harmony = 2 bassoons + 4 horns + trombones 1–2 + violas.
- R24 [PREFER] Very solid bass line = trombone 3 + tuba + cellos + basses in octaves.
- R25 [PREFER] Reserve the highest registers early in a piece and bring them in to "light up" the texture and
  build a crescendo (Wagner reaches them at m.18 of the prelude).

**Exceptions / conditions**
- R2/R3 vs. R4: unison same-instrument = warm/blurred; octave/cross-section doubling = brilliant/loud.
- R20 depends on form: keep in reserve only if a later, bigger statement is planned.

**Examples**
- Mahler, Symphony 4, I, mm.126–141 (Ex.15-18): 4 flutes (zu 4) in unison, A major, 4/4, high register,
  f/p hairpins; shrill, pleading line over thin texture; warm due to tuning differences.
- Tchaikovsky, Romeo and Juliet, mm.334–337 (Ex.15-19): E trumpets a 2 in unison with long-note melody;
  picc, fl, ob, cl, EH, bsn, 4 hn, trb/tba, timp, bass drum ff and strings (unis.) play off-beat chord stabs
  doubled through the orchestra: power, not colour.
- Schubert, Symphony 8, I, mm.170–176 (Ex.15-20): ff unison/octave statement over tonic pedal (timpani on
  dominant); at m.174 strings take the melodic figuration (16ths) while winds/brass give chords (fz);
  brass/timp pedals ease the change; wide flute–strings gap.
- Mahler, Symphony 4, IV, mm.36–39 (Ex.15-21): under soprano: flute 1 middle register, hns 1/3 muted, hns 2/4
  open ppp (old bass-clef horn notation, footnote "transpose up a P4"), harp in octaves, cymbal pp sponge
  mallet roll; muted strings only for the final open fifth (pp, divisi).
- Mahler, Symphony 4, IV, mm.72–76 (Ex.15-22): muted strings carry the accompaniment pp; piccolo 1 low;
  harp blocked thirds then octaves; triangle pp; ends on full D major (fl, cl, bass cl, hn 1, harp pp).
- Mahler, Symphony 4, IV, mm.106–111 (Ex.15-23): first very soft brass (hns pp, F trumpets ppp) parallel
  chords + harp/horns 2–4 in contrary motion; then flutes + clarinets + muted DB; harp constant.
- Wagner, Die Meistersinger, Prelude, mm.1–9 (Ex.15-24): f "sehr gehalten"; trumpets + 2 ob + 2 cl melody
  in m.1, strings' multiple-stop chord + accent, then vln I/II "sehr kräftig" take the melody from m.2;
  bassoons/horns/trb 1–2/violas inner harmony; trb 3 + bass tuba + vc + DB bass line in octaves; trumpets
  drop to harmonic support.

**Agent applicability**
- For a "warm, slightly smeared" melody, use an ensemble patch (e.g. "Flutes a4") rather than a solo + other colours.
- For a "powerful" hit: melody in 2 trumpets unison; chord stabs in every other section, octave-doubled,
  rhythmically contrasted with the melody.
- Unison → tutti transition: sustain a tonic pedal in horns/trombones and timpani for the bars of change.
- Keep ≥ an octave of empty space between the top wind voice and the string harmony (inferred from "wide
  separation").
- On repeats of a refrain: same voicing/chords, different instrument assignment each time; one constant colour.
- Majestic tutti template: melody vln I+II + ob + cl; inner bsn ×2 + hn ×4 + trb 1–2 + vla; bass trb 3 + tuba
  + vc + cb in octaves; keep highest octave (picc/fl top, vln high) for the later crescendo.

## 15 (cont.) In a polyphonic texture (en una textura polifónica) (scan p.579–585, printed p.578–584)

**Concepts**
- Foreground, middleground, background (primer plano, plano medio, plano de fondo): three or more main
  musical elements sounding at once in an orchestral context. Adler notes skill in polyphonic orchestral
  textures takes longer to acquire than homophonic. (Cross-ref: ch.5, Beethoven 7 II, Ex.5-20.)
- Quadruple counterpoint (contrapunto cuádruple): four themes combinable in any vertical order.

**Rules**
- R26 [MUST] Give each simultaneous theme its own rhythm and melodic profile so it is recognizable inside
  a complex texture.
- R27 [PREFER] In a fugal build-up, double only the main (cantus-firmus-like, long-note) theme; let each other
  melody be played by a single string part as it enters.
- R28 [PREFER] Re-colour each entry of the main theme slightly: one string part doubled by instruments from
  other sections (e.g. bsns + horns + cellos; fl + obs + violas; fl + obs + vln II; fl + obs + vln I).
- R29 [ALLOWED] [era: Classical — unusual then] Treat the double bass as a fully independent contrapuntal part
  (not just the cello line an octave down).
- R30 [PREFER] As the texture thickens, reinforce an inner theme in parallel thirds by two string sections
  (violas + cellos, later violas + vln II).
- R31 [MUST] Once all themes are combined, hold each colour pairing constant long enough for every theme to be
  heard complete; do not keep switching colours. *Why:* otherwise the listener must dig ideas out of a maze of
  changing colours.
- R32 [PREFER] Pair each string part with one wind at the unison (vln I+fl, vln II+ob 1, vla+ob 2,
  vc+bsn 1) and the DB with bsn 2 at the octave; brass and timpani play non-thematic tonic–dominant pedals for
  cohesion.
- R33 [ALLOWED] Mark the climax by an abrupt switch from polyphony to homophony, introducing a new theme there;
  restate the key theme complete several times (unison and with homophonic accompaniment), heavily doubled for
  dynamic and clarity.
- R34 [MUST] For three simultaneous themes to be heard separately: (1) give each a distinct colour combination,
  (2) a different rhythmic profile, (3) ideally let each be heard alone earlier in the piece.
- R35 [PREFER] [era: 19th-c. Romantic] Colour each theme with a combination of at least one string, one
  woodwind and one brass instrument. This is how to emulate the big 19th-c. sound when doubling, at f or p.
- R36 [MUST] Give each thematic element its own articulation: e.g. bass theme detached and marked (sehr
  markiert), melody legato and expressive (sehr ausdrucksvoll), inner figuration staccato, evenly light
  (immer gleichmässig leicht).

**Examples**
- Mozart, Symphony 41 "Jupiter", IV, mm.368–424 (Ex.15-25/15-26): five themes, four in quadruple
  counterpoint, the fifth enters at the climax (m.402). Theme 1 = whole-note C–D–F–E motto (also in mvts 1, 3).
  mm.372–388 only theme 1 is doubled; theme 3 thirds in vla+vc (382–383), vla+vln II (386–387); m.388 four
  themes + brass pedal; mm.389–400 constant pairings (R32). (Text's "(13)" is a typo for "(3)".)
- Wagner, Meistersinger Prelude, mm.158–161 (Ex.15-27): theme 1 (bass) = DB + tuba + 2 bsn, mf, sehr
  markiert (DB sounds an octave lower); theme 2 = vln I + cl 1 + hn 1 unison, vc an octave lower, p
  ausdrucksvoll legato; theme 3 = vln II + vla + 2 fl + 2 ob + cl 2 + hns 2–4 + tpt 2 (some notes), three-voice
  harmony doubled at the octave, staccato scherzando; trombones single p "aber sehr markiert" note, triangle p.

**Agent applicability**
- Counterpoint check per theme: distinct rhythm values, distinct articulation (MIDI: note length/keyswitch:
  marcato vs. legato vs. staccato), distinct timbre group.
- Doubling recipe for "big Romantic" polyphony: each line = string + woodwind + brass (horn usually).
- In a contrapuntal tutti, put brass/timpani on pedals, not on themes, unless one theme must dominate.
- Don't change a line's doubling instruments mid-theme once the full combination is reached.

## 15 (cont.) In a varied texture (en una textura variada) (scan p.587–591, printed p.586–590)

Four examples combining three or more elements of different thematic weight (foreground–middleground–
background), a frequent situation in the repertoire. This range covers three (Rimsky-Korsakov, Holst, Bloch
started).

**Rules**
- R37 [ALLOWED] Moving, high-register melody: cello in its high register doubled at the unison by two horns,
  with two clarinets an octave above (clarinets in a register where they are heard clearly).
- R38 [PREFER] Middleground dance-like accompaniment: piccolo + 2 flutes staccato with double tonguing, and
  violins spiccato, on repeated fast notes.
- R39 [PREFER] Background: sustained notes in oboes, bassoon 2, horns 3–4, violas, DB, with a light arpeggiated
  layer in bassoon 1 and violas.
- R40 [ALLOWED] Use harp arpeggios as punctuation: to separate phrase 1 from phrase 2 and to support the
  final cadence.
- R41 [ALLOWED] Brilliant, virtuosic tutti (Holst): foreground theme first in 4 trumpets, then repeated by
  tenor trombones, tenor and bass tubas, violas and cellos; middleground second theme first in 2 tenor
  trombones, then 6 horns unison + trumpets 1–2; background harmonic figuration (violin ostinato) coloured by
  2 piccolos, 2 flutes, 3 oboes, 3 clarinets and glockenspiel.
- R42 [PREFER] Add the rest of the orchestra for the big tutti only after the layers are established (Holst
  m.148); trumpets may then reinforce the background melodically.
- R43 [PREFER] Stabilize the rhythmic flow of fast figuration with short secco chords (beats) and later with
  strong low bass notes on the harmony.
- R44 [ALLOWED] Make an arrival chord sound "glorious" by a surprising modulation plus the sudden stop of all
  motion, then follow with a brass fanfare.
- R45 [ALLOWED] A melodic fragment can first appear as a unison melody in octaves, then its harmonic
  implications become the background a few bars later (Bloch m.230 → m.235).
- R46 [ALLOWED] [era: 20th-c., esp. American composers] Instead of colouring by mixing sections, keep the
  sections separate (strings / woodwinds / brass blocks) so that each musical element is perceived as a
  distinct entity. Adler says Bloch, like Holst, works this way.
- R47 [ALLOWED] To build a climax, add contrabassoon joining DB to double the main theme played by trumpets
  and trombones; flutes 2–3 join [continues on next page].

**Examples**
- Rimsky-Korsakov, Scheherazade, IV, mm.482–493 (Ex.15-28): Vivo, E major, 2/8–3/8 [?]; cello theme high
  (~E4–G#4 [?]) + hns 1–2 unison + 2 A clarinets an octave above, accents and hairpins; picc/fl a2 and
  violins (arco) repeated-note spiccato p; viola mf off-beat figure; harp f rolled chords at m.482 and m.486.
- Holst, The Planets, "Jupiter", mm.140–156 (Ex.15-29): Allegro giocoso, 3/4, ff; tenor trb a2 "f non legato";
  violins ff non legato ostinato doubled by glockenspiel and high woodwinds; low winds/DB on beat-1 bass
  notes; tutti from m.148, climax chord m.156 fff.
- Bloch, Schelomo (cello rhapsody), Ex.15-30: two main ideas (first idea in F horn with triplets/sextuplets
  and changing meters 4/4–3/4–6/4; second idea with dotted triplet figures) plus a third short viola-clef
  fragment from the opening. Ex.15-31 (m.230 ff.) continues past this range.

**Agent applicability**
- Three-plane template: foreground = one mixed-timbre melody (string + brass unison, woodwind octave above);
  middleground = fast repeated-note/staccato rhythm layer (flutes + violins); background = sustained pads +
  simple arpeggio (oboes, bassoons, low horns, violas, basses); harp only at phrase seams.
- For an energetic 3/4 tutti, keep a constant high ostinato layer (violins + picc/fl/ob/cl + glockenspiel) and
  move the themes between brass groups and low strings.
- Section-block scoring (R46) suits modern/film textures: assign each idea to one family for clarity.
