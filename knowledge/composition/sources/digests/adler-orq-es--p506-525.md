## 14 Percussion as reinforcement of accents and rhythm: Copland, *Appalachian Spring* (scan p.506–512, printed p.507–513)

Adler numbers the functions percussion (and piano) serve in *Appalachian Spring*. Item 1 falls before this
range, and items 2–5 are here. In every case one dry percussion colour or the piano doubles a rhythm or
accent that another section already plays, so it marks the accent without adding a new line.

**Concepts**
- Wood block (caja china / Wood Bl.): a dry, high wooden click. Here it sticks to offbeat accents.
- Claves (claves): a "very dry sound with a wooden timbre". Here it reinforces the strong accents of an
  irregular-metre passage.
- Snare drum played with a brush (escobilla, "Brush on S. Dr.") and then with a stick on the rim
  ("Stick (on rim)"): two quiet snare colours used for a soft backbeat, which Adler calls "a softer strong
  part".
- Half pizz. (half pizz.): half the section plucks while the other half bows, as in Ex. 14‑17 (vla, vlc, *p*).

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] Use the piano to reinforce a string *sfp* attack. *Example:* the piano plays an accented
  octave (8va, *sfp*) at the moment the violins attack their *sfp* chord (Ex. 14‑17, m.58). The piano's
  hammer adds bite to the string attack and then dies away, which strengthens the *sfp* shape.
- R2 [PREFER] Double offbeat accents in the violins with wood block. Write staccato eighths on the offbeats,
  in the same rhythm as the violins (Ex. 14‑18, mm.225–228, Fast ♩=132, vlns *mf*).
- R3 [ALLOWED] Use a single soft triangle stroke (*p*) as a colour punctuation when a phrase changes (Ex. 14‑18
  m.228, the moment the violins switch to pizz.).
- R4 [PREFER] Add triangle to a rising scale that builds tension. Write it as one note or roll, *mf* < *sf*,
  under the flute/piccolo/string scale run that ends on a strong accent (Ex. 14‑19, m.235).
- R5 [PREFER] For a light, propulsive backbeat, put snare drum on offbeat eighths at *p*. Use brush first and
  then stick on the rim (Ex. 14‑19, after m.235 to m.244). It reinforces the offbeat already in the
  accompaniment: div. vln 2 and violas *secco*, bassoons *secco*, piano *mp* staccato offbeat chords.
- R6 [PREFER] Reinforce the tutti accents of a passage in changing metres (5/8, 3/4, 3/8, 2/4, 6/8 …) with
  claves. Let the claves play only the accented attacks that winds, harp and piano play together
  (Ex. 14‑20, mm.375–382, *mf* to *sf*).
- R7 [PREFER] To crown the climax when this music returns, add xylophone and strings, then end with a series
  of solo timpani strokes supported by pizzicato in the basses (described in the text, not shown).
  *Why:* the percussion grows in stages: dry woods first, keyboard-mallet colour later, timpani at the peak.

**Examples**
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring*, mm.55–61 (Ex. 14‑17): solos for woodwinds in A (oboe, clarinets, bassoon),
  half-pizz. violas and cellos *p*, and a piano octave doubling the violins' *sfp* at m.58. Tutti winds and
  strings then rise together *mf*.
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring*, mm.225–228 (Ex. 14‑18): wood block plays offbeat staccato eighths with the
  violins. Oboes a2 and vln 2 play the same dotted figure. The 1st trombone plays *non legato* and the cellos
  pizz. *f* bass line. A single triangle note *p* closes the passage.
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring*, mm.229–244 (Ex. 14‑19): flute + piccolo play 16th-note scale runs 8va. The
  triangle swells into the accent at m.235. At that accent the horns play *cuivré* stopped (+), and the 2nd
  horn is *con sord.* Then come snare with brush and then stick on rim on the offbeats, vln 2 div. *secco* and
  viola *secco* chord offbeats, the piano's left-hand bass on the beat with the right-hand chord on the
  offbeat, and the clarinets change from B♭ to A.
- Copland, *Appalachian Spring*, mm.375–382 (Ex. 14‑20): claves, piano, harp and woodwinds play the same
  accented hits. The horns, trumpets and trombones are *senza sord.* a2. The meter changes almost every bar.

**Agent applicability**
- For syncopated or "Americana" textures, double the string offbeats with one dry percussion voice (wood
  block, rim, or claves). Write that voice on the identical rhythm at about the same dynamic. Do not give it
  a pattern of its own.
- For a soft backbeat, use snare brush or rim click at *p*. Do not use a normal snare stroke.
- Doubling an *sfp* string chord with a piano attack gives an "attack + swell-down" contour. In MIDI, put a
  piano note at the attack and fade the strings' CC11 right after it.
- In irregular-metre tutti hits, give claves (or a similar dry wood) only the accent attacks.

## 14 Creación de un clímax: building or sustaining a climax with percussion (scan p.512–521, printed p.513–522)

**Concepts**
- Three ways to use percussion for a climax:
  (a) Build it gradually with an ostinato or other repeated figure (elaborar un clímax).
  (b) Sustain it (apoyar/mantener).
  (c) Keep the percussion back until the climax itself (reservar la percusión) so that it joins only at the
  last moment.
- Adler's first two examples (Debussy, Benson) show (a)/(b). The third (Brahms) shows (c).

**Rules**
- R8 [PREFER] Build a climax with a percussion ostinato that starts extremely soft (*ppp*) well before the
  peak. Change the ostinato in stages as the music approaches the climax:
  1. Steady eighths.
  2. Isolated strokes on each beat.
  3. The piece's main rhythmic figure (Debussy, "Fiestas").
- R9 [PREFER] At the start, support a soft timpani ostinato with harps and string pizzicato on the same
  pulse. This makes a pitched, rhythmic carpet under the theme (Fiestas, [10]).
- R10 [PREFER] Add percussion in layers as the crescendo grows. Debussy's order:
  1. Timpani alone (from [10]).
  2. Snare drum entering two bars before [13], *mf molto cresc.* in triplet figures.
  3. Cymbals at [13] (*f*), then *ff* crashes every two bars at the peak.
  *Why:* each new colour marks a step up in intensity without needing a louder dynamic.
- R11 [PREFER] Hold a continuous *crescendo* under fragmentary melodic entries in woodwinds and horns. The
  percussion and string pulse carry the growth while the melodic material stays in fragments
  (Fiestas, [12]–[13]).
- R12 [ALLOWED] End a built-up climax abruptly by a change of metre and the return of earlier material
  (Fiestas: sudden switch to 6/8 at [14], *pp*).
- R13 [ALLOWED] Sustain a climax with percussion alone after the rest of the orchestra stops. Combine the
  repeated rhythmic motives the percussion parts played separately earlier in the movement (Benson,
  *Symphony for Drums and Wind Orchestra*, mvt 3, from [N]: "Solo drums to end"). Adler calls ending a
  movement with percussion "a contemporary device". `[era: 20th-c. wind-ensemble / contemporary practice]`
- R14 [PREFER] When percussion alone carries an ending, keep tempo and accents fixed and let the dynamic
  fade gradually. The score is marked "Dying away gradually to end — maintain tempo and accent" and ends
  with "silence".
- R15 [PREFER] Reserve the timpani during the build-up and bring them in late, at the climax. The delayed
  entry is what makes the climax land (Brahms, Symphony No. 2, finale). Good brass writing (trombones, tuba)
  helps, but Adler credits the effect mainly to Brahms's restraint with the timpani. `[era: Romantic
  symphonic practice]` (see the next digest for the example)

**Examples**
- Debussy, *Nocturnes*, "Fiestas", [10]–[14] (Ex. 14‑21, D♭ major, "Modéré mais toujours très rythmé"):
  - [10]: timpani play *ppp* eighths on A♭, the dominant, as a pedal (A♭2 [?]). Harp 1 plays offbeat chords
    *ppp*, harp 2 plays rolled bass chords, cellos (div.) and basses play pizz. *pp/ppp*, and muted trumpets
    ("sourdines", *pp*) play the triplet theme in parallel.
  - [11]: the timpani switch to single strokes. Woodwinds enter *p* "peu à peu" cresc. with triplet
    fragments, and violins/violas go pizz.
  - [12]: the strings play repeated arco eighth chords, *cresc.* The horns enter *f*.
  - Two bars before [13]: the snare enters with the triplet figure, *mf molto cresc.*
  - [13]: the climax at *f*/*ff*. Timpani and snare play the ♩ + triplet-eighth figure, and cymbals crash.
    The violins and violas play triplet 16th-note runs 8va. The cellos play div. repeated accented chords,
    and the basses play a pizz.-like eighth on the beat (arco). The trumpets and trombones are marked
    "vibrant sans dureté", the tuba holds a pedal, and harp 2 plays sustained chords.
  - Final *ff* "très soutenu": the brass and horns play accented chords, woodwinds play triplet 16th-note
    figures, and the cymbals play *ff* every two bars.
- Wagner, *Rienzi* overture, mm.47–65: a comparable percussion-built climax (pointer only).
- W. Schuman, *New England Triptych*, mvt 1, mm.235–269: a comparable percussion-built climax (pointer only).
- J. Schwantner, *…and the mountains rising nowhere*, mm.91–120: a comparable percussion-built climax
  (pointer only).
- W. Benson, *Symphony for Drums and Wind Orchestra*, mvt 3, from 3 bars before [M] to the end
  (Ex. 14‑22, Allegro ♩=180):
  - Before [N]: a full wind band with harps and piano (glissandi, 8va clusters), timpani + Perc 1–5. Perc 3
    switches to xylophone with glissandi, and Perc 4 plays a cymbal *mp*–*ff*.
  - At [N] the winds cut off and the drums continue alone.
  - The timpani loop a one-bar accented figure. Perc 1 plays tied, accented syncopations, Perc 2 a
    16th-note snare figure, Perc 3 eighth/16th patterns, Perc 4 a field drum (Field dr.) entering *ff* in
    continuous eighths, and Perc 5 "2 wood stix" with written sticking (R L L R L L R).
  - The parts enter one after another and each has its own < > swells. The "Dying away gradually" marking
    leads to silence.

**Agent applicability**
- To build a climax over 8–20 bars:
  1. Start the timpani on a dominant or tonic pedal at *ppp*–*pp* (velocity ~25–35), doubled by harp and
     string pizz. on the same pulse.
  2. Thin to one stroke per beat.
  3. Switch to the piece's main rhythmic motive at the start of the final crescendo.
  4. Add snare about two bars before the peak and cymbals on the downbeat of the peak.
- Do not start every percussion instrument at once. Stagger entries so each one marks a new stage.
- For a percussion-only coda, give each drum part its own short repeating motive, already heard earlier.
  Overlap them, keep the tempo strict, and fade with velocity/CC (not ritardando).
- For the "reserve" strategy, leave the timpani tacet through the build-up and give them their first entry
  at the climax.
