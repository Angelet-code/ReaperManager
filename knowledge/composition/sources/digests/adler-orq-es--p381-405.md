## 11 — Melody presentation: brass in combination with other instruments, cont. (scan p.381–391, printed p.382–392)

**Concepts**
- Low-unison "power" doubling (duplicación de los graves): trombones (and/or tuba) playing the same line
  as bassoons, violas, cellos and basses. Adler treats it as one of the most popular and powerful
  combinations of the symphony orchestra.
- Thinning diminuendo (diminuendo orquestado): a written-out fade made by withdrawing doubling
  instruments one by one, not only by dynamics.
- Chorale-type melody (melodía de tipo coral): homophonic, sustained brass hymn texture.

**Examples and rules**
- Wagner, *Siegfried*, Prelude to Act III, last bars of Ex. 11-14 (printed p.382): bass tuba 1 carries the
  theme with bass clarinet, bassoons 1–2 and cellos/basses; bassoon 3, bass tuba 2, contrabass tuba and the
  two Eb tenor tubas hold sustained chord tones; timpani roll; all p → cresc.; violins/violas in
  sixteenth-note arpeggio figures.
  - R1 [ALLOWED] Build a dark, heavy bass melody from a full family of low reeds + one tuba + low strings,
    with the other tubas holding a sustained low chord under it.
- Schubert, *Rosamunde*, Entr'acte I, bars 106–123 (Ex. 11-15, Allegro molto moderato, D): opening: 2
  bassoons, 3 trombones and double basses sustain soft chords with hairpins (< f > pp), strings carry a
  rising line; D horns add an ff > pp swell at bar 112–113. From bar 114 a full tutti (f) where trombones
  play the melody in unison/octaves with bassoons, violas, cellos and basses (upper winds and violins on
  the same line higher up); E trumpets (a2) and timpani only punctuate with accented short notes. Then
  Schubert drops trombones 1–2 and leaves trombone 3 alone with the lowest strings (p >), ending on a pp
  chord.
  - R2 [PREFER] For a powerful tutti melody, double the low strings (vla + vc + db) and bassoons with
    trombones on the same line. *Why:* the trombones give the tutti its weight.
  - R3 [PREFER] Orchestrate a diminuendo by removing brass doublings in steps (trb 1–2 out, trb 3 stays
    only on the lowest string line), then reduce dynamics.
  - R4 [ALLOWED] Soft sustained trombone chords (p, with < > swells) under a string melody: trombones do not
    have to be loud.
  - R5 `[era: natural trumpets/horns, early 19th c.]` Trumpets in E and horns in D only punctuate (short
    accented tonic/dominant notes) or swell on sustained chord tones; the melody goes to trombones,
    woodwinds and strings.
- Liszt, *Les Préludes*, bars 405–410 (Ex. 11-16, Andante maestoso, 12/8, ff): the theme in dotted rhythms
  is played by tenor trombones 1–2 (a2), bass trombone + tuba (a2), bassoons, cellos and basses; piccolo,
  flute, oboe, C clarinet, 4 C horns and C trumpets hold or reiterate chords; violins and violas run
  sixteenth-note scales "sempre staccato"; timpani, snare drum, cymbals and bass drum.
  - R6 [PREFER] Tuba + trombones + bassoons + cellos + basses on one line is one of the strongest
    combinations in the orchestra; use it for a heroic low theme at ff.
  - R7 [PREFER] Against such a low unison theme, keep the upper winds/horns on sustained or rhythmic chords
    and give the upper strings fast figuration, so the three layers (theme low, harmony middle, motion high)
    stay separate (inferred from the score).
- Tchaikovsky, *1812 Overture*, bars 386–398 (Ex. 11-17, Allegro giusto, Eb): the Russian anthem "God Save
  the Tsar" (Dios salve al Zar) in long notes in the low register by trombones, tuba, horns, bassoons and
  low strings (vla, vc, db), ffff, against a very dense counterpoint of fast sixteenth-note figures in
  piccolo, flute, oboe, Bb clarinet, English horn, Bb cornets, Eb trumpets and violins; snare drum,
  cymbals, two bass drums; an optional extra band of brass ("Band", to be played by additional brass)
  doubles the whole texture. From bar 396 the winds, horns and cornets switch to triplet figures.
  - R8 [PREFER] Compare with Liszt (R6): there the low brass theme is rhythmic and active; here it is a slow
    hymn in long values under busy upper counterpoint. Both work because theme and counterpoint sit in
    different registers and rhythmic values.
  - R9 [ALLOWED] Horns can join the low hymn group (sustained chord tones) while cornets/trumpets join the
    fast upper counterpoint: the brass section is split between the two layers.
  - R10 `[era: late-19th-c. Russian/French band practice]` Cornets (Bb) alongside Eb trumpets, and an ad lib
    extra brass band for maximum volume in festive overtures.
- Wagner, *Lohengrin*, Prelude to Act I, bars 50–58 (Ex. 11-18, Andante molto, A major; printed p.389–391):
  after a p cresc. build (strings tremolo, timpani roll), 3 D trumpets, trombones and tuba play a
  chorale-like melody f "sehr gehalten" (very sustained/held), with accents; the strings double it first
  (ff, block chords, violin 1 on top), then the strings drop out and the woodwinds (flutes, oboes,
  English horn, A clarinet, bass clarinet, bassoons, all ff with triplet figures) take over the doubling;
  E and D horns fill the middle; then a diminuendo leads to a pp chord (strings re-enter p).
  - R11 [PREFER] Re-colour a repeated brass chorale by changing its doubling family: first strings, then
    woodwinds, keeping the brass as the constant core.
  - R12 [PREFER] End a brass climax with a written diminuendo into a pp chord where strings re-enter softly
    under the fading brass/winds.
  - R13 [PREFER] Study dense Wagner doublings by reducing them to piano, marking every principal voice, the
    instruments doubling it, and where another instrument takes over the doubling.
- Additional passages for study: Copland, *Appalachian Spring*; Prokofiev, *Lieutenant Kijé*, "Troika";
  Stravinsky, *Pulcinella* (pointer only).

**Agent applicability**
- Heroic low theme at ff: copy the melody notes to trombone 1–2, bass trombone, tuba, bassoon, cello and
  bass tracks (octave-adjust per range); put upper strings on fast staccato scales, woodwinds/horns on chords.
- Hymn under a busy tutti: low brass + horns + low strings in whole/half notes, velocity/CC1 high; upper
  winds, trumpets and violins in sixteenths.
- Fade-out: drop trombone 1–2 notes first, keep trombone 3 on the bass line at p, end on a pp chord with
  strings entering softly (CC1 low).
- Repeated chorale: second statement keeps brass but swaps the doubling track group (strings → woodwinds).

## 11 — Contrapuntal writing for the brass section (Escritura contrapuntística para la sección de metal) (scan p.391–405, printed p.392–406)

**Concepts**
- Brass counterpoint: brass used in contrapuntal textures, either combined with other sections or within
  the brass section alone; some passages in strict imitation (canon, fugato), others freer.
- Canon by colour and register (canon por color y registro): each entry of the canonic subject is given to
  a different timbre group in a different register, so entries are heard as separate.
- Fugato (fugato): a fugal exposition inside a non-fugal movement.
- Brass punctuation (puntuación del metal): short brass chords/hits that punctuate another instrument's
  line; a favourite device of Hindemith and others.
- Clarino-type part (parte de tipo clarino): florid, ornamented Baroque-trumpet style line.
- Stretto (stretto): entries of the subject overlapping at short distance for cumulative tension.

**Examples and rules**
- Berlioz, *Roman Carnival* Overture, bars 298–344 (Ex. 11-19, Allegro vivace, 6/8, A major): the canonic
  subject is stated first by 2 bassoons in unison (p), then by the trombones (with tuba on the bottom staff,
  p), then by flutes and oboes in octaves (p, later with Bb clarinet); six bars earlier flutes and oboes in
  unison anticipate it with a slight intervallic variant (mf). Accompaniment: second violins/cellos on
  repeated eighths (pp–p), first violins on short trilled fragments, basses pizz then arco. A long
  "crescendo poco a poco" adds C horn sustained notes (p), then piccolo, timpani, cresc. molto, and lands on
  an ff tutti (trumpets, cornets, cymbals, tambourine, triangle) at bar 344.
  - R14 [PREFER] In a canon, separate entries by both timbre family and register (low reeds → trombones →
    high woodwinds). *Why:* contrast of colour and register makes each entry stand out.
  - R15 [ALLOWED] Trombones can state a canonic subject at p; soft brass counterpoint is idiomatic.
  - R16 [PREFER] Build a long crescendo by adding sustained horns first, then upper woodwinds/piccolo,
    keeping trumpets and cymbals in reserve for the arrival.
  - R17 [ALLOWED] Anticipate a coming entry with a slightly varied version of the subject in another group.
- Stravinsky, *Chorale Variations on "Vom Himmel hoch"* (J. S. Bach), Variation IV, bars 1–8 (Ex. 11-20,
  quarter = 80, 4/4): imitative counterpoint in which Stravinsky imitates the many
  registers (stops) of a Baroque organ by assigning the canon voices to woodwinds and brass: two C trumpets
  (senza sord., p, florid sixteenths with trills), tenor and bass trombones (p ma marcato), flute 1 +
  bassoon 1 paired (mf). The chorale melody is sung by choir (alto/tenor/bass) and doubled by violas, double
  basses (div., p legato), cellos and the lower bassoons/contrabassoon (pp); organ optional.
  - R18 [ALLOWED] Imitate organ registrations by giving each contrapuntal voice to a different wind/brass
    colour and doubling the cantus firmus with soft low strings and low reeds.
  - R19 [ALLOWED] Write clarino-style florid trumpet lines in the middle register, not only in the high
    register: the style lies in the figuration, not the height. `[era: Baroque clarino idiom evoked]`
  - R20 [PREFER] Transpose an arrangement if the new key gives a brighter orchestral sound (Stravinsky moved
    Bach's original key for brilliance).
  - R21 [PREFER] Mark brass counterpoint "p ma marcato" (soft but articulated) so it stays clear without
    covering the chorale.
- Hindemith, *Symphonic Metamorphosis*, 2nd movement (Turandot Scherzo), bars 160–195 (Ex. 11-21, Lively,
  4/4): a light-hearted fugato for the whole brass section. Entries are made by unison groups: 3 trombones
  in unison (mf), then 3 horns in unison (mf) while the trombones continue at p, then 2 trumpets in unison
  (mf), then tuba (mf), then 4 horns; the fugato ends when the timpani state their own version of the
  subject at bar 192, punctuated by the brass from bar 195.
  - R22 [MUST] In a brass fugato keep every instrument in its best register so the subject is clear at each
    entry.
  - R23 [PREFER] Give each fugal voice to a unison group (trb a3, hn a3, tpt a2) rather than to a single
    player when the brass section plays alone. (inferred from the score)
  - R24 [PREFER] Enter the subject at mf and drop the continuing counter-voices to p, so each new entry
    leads. (inferred from the score dynamics)
  - R25 [ALLOWED] Let the timpani take a fugue subject (pitched), with brass punctuating it.
- Bartók, *Concerto for Orchestra*, I, bars 316–387 (Ex. 11-22, Tempo I, dotted quarter = 83–90, 3/8;
  brass alone at 316–339 and 380–387): one of the best-known 20th-c. brass passages. Tutti ff chord hits
  (woodwinds, strings, timpani) frame it. Trombones and trumpets present the main theme in a fugato
  (each entry "f, ben marcato", accompanying voices mf, accents and tenuto marks); from bar 342 the theme is
  inverted by the horns (1–3 a2 and 2–4 a2) and then by the rest of the brass; stretto at bars 363–386
  with trumpets 1–3 and trombones 1–3 entering one bar apart ("marc."), a six-voice brass texture.
  - R26 [PREFER] Treat two horns in unison as equal in strength to one trumpet or one trombone when
    balancing contrapuntal brass voices (a recurring principle in this chapter).
  - R27 [PREFER] Mark each new fugal entry "f, ben marcato" and the continuing voices mf.
  - R28 [PREFER] Use inversion of the subject in a new brass family (horns) for the second exposition.
  - R29 [PREFER] Use stretto (entries 1 bar apart in 3/8) at the end of a brass fugato for cumulative
    excitement.
  - R30 [ALLOWED] Frame a brass-alone fugato with short ff tutti chord punctuations from the rest of the
    orchestra.

**Agent applicability**
- Canon/fugato: assign each entry to a different track group (bassoons → trombones → flutes+oboes), each
  in its own register; never two entries in the same group back-to-back.
- Brass fugato velocity map: entering subject velocity ~ f (≈ 95–105), other voices ~ mf/p (≈ 60–75); horns
  doubled a2 (two horn tracks or an a2 patch) where one trumpet/trombone carries an equal voice.
- Stretto: shift copies of the subject one bar apart across tpt 1–3 / trb 1–3 tracks, accents on entries.
- Organ-like texture: soft florid lines in trumpets/flute/bassoon, cantus firmus doubled by violas, basses
  and low reeds in long legato notes.
