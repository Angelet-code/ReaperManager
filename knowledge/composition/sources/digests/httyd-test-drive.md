## Techniques observed (by category)

**Doublings across families (doblajes)**
- Theme statements are always **multi-family unison**: whistle + oboes a3 + bassoons + celli (b.13);
  horns a12 + Violin II (b.21); flutes + horns + violins + celli (b.32); oboes + horns + trumpets (b.59).
- **Celli in treble clef double the melody at the woodwinds' pitch** (b.13, b.17, b.32) — adds body to a
  mid-register tune without lowering it an octave. The other celli/basses keep the bass.
- Only the solo colour instrument carries ornaments; doubling instruments play the plain line (b.13–20).
- The high register is reinforced by piccolo 8va over flute runs, and by Violin I 8va over Violin II.

**Ostinati and motor rhythm**
- Intro: neighbour-note eighth motif in celesta + harp + Vln I, over a harp-LH repeated D.
- Violas as "chug": repeated 16ths on one pitch, then 8th double-stops.
- Gallop: 8th + two 16ths in basses/low winds/timpani/snare, with BD on 1 and 3.
- b.59: violins in unison 16th broken-chord turns as motor under a brass melody.
- Ostinato pitch cells stay fixed while the harmony changes over/under them (pedal + chord-symbol shifts).

**Pedals**
- Tonic pedal under the opening (D5, 10 bars), under the theme-A climax (b.32–39) and under the gallop
  chromatic triads (b.46–49). bVI pedal (Bb) for the suspense stall.
- Pedals are **re-attacked with accent + cymbal every 2 or 4 bars** instead of tied indefinitely.

**Countermelodies (contramelodías)**
- Horns answer in the gaps of the string melody (b.9–11, b.27, b.36–39, b.63–65).
- Bassoons + celli tenor-register countermelody under high repeated-note violins (b.30–31).

**Brass voicing**
- 12 horns used mostly as **one unison line** (or two octaves) — power, not harmony.
- Trombones 1–4 in close-position triads in the tenor register; bass trombones a2 doubling tuba or a
  third/fifth above it.
- Trumpets withheld until b.31 and used first as a short repeated-note fanfare stinger; later in triads
  carrying the tune (b.36, b.59).
- Suspense: chromatic close clusters in horns + trombones moving by semitone.

**String figures**
- Repeated-note 16ths (each pitch twice) for brilliance without new pitches.
- 16th sextuplet scale runs in violins/flutes/piccolo as **transition sweeps into downbeats** (b.29, b.39).
- Aleatoric tremolo-gliss "start anywhere between two pitches, rise to very high" for 4-bar tension.
- Interlocking rising 16th cells between violas and celli (b.46–54).

**Woodwind roles**
- Fills on beats 3–4 (sextuplet runs) answering the melody's long notes on beats 1–2.
- Hocket: oboes and clarinets split a 16th figure with rests so neither section plays continuously.
- Fast staccato rising scale pickup (oboes/bassoons/Vln II, b.17–20) as a recurring rhythmic signature.

**Percussion role**
- Cymbal/suspended cymbal mark every phrase downbeat (every 2–4 bars); suspended-cymbal swell p<
  on the bar before.
- Snare: roll p<f into beat 3 accent (b.17–20) as a lift; march figure only at the big climax (b.36→);
  gallop rhythm in the action section.
- Timpani: single strokes on phrase downbeats, rolls on sustains, gallop doubling the basses.
- Celesta + glockenspiel as "sparkle" only in the light sections; removed for tutti.
- Secco hits (b.69) to cut a climax off cleanly.

**How climaxes are built and released**
- Build 1 (b.21→32): horns alone (f) → octave tutti ff with sextuplet runs (b.25) → full-bar ascending
  run + crescendo rolls (b.29) → D5 landing → one-beat trumpet stinger (b.31) → theme A tutti ff over pedal.
- Build 2 (b.40→45): harmonic stall on bVI + clusters + aleatoric gliss crescendo → ff D5 hit → gallop.
- Build 3 (b.50→59): chromatic rising bass + triplet acceleration in the bass + click accelerando
  (111→117) + key lift D → E.
- Releases: subito f restart (b.55), and secco percussion + thin woodwind texture (b.69).
- Themes return in a new orchestration each time: folk solo (whistle) → full tutti; horns → oboes/brass.

## Transferable techniques (imperative rules for agents)

**Melody / doubling**
- T1 PREFER: for a heroic flying theme, put the tune in all horns in unison (or 2 octaves) and double it
  at unison with Violin II; keep trombones on close triads and tuba/basses on half-note roots.
- T2 PREFER: for a mid-register folk tune (around F#4–B4), double the solo colour instrument
  (whistle/flute) with oboes a3 and celli in their high register at the **same pitch**; give grace notes
  and turns only to the solo instrument.
- T3 PREFER: at the climax restatement, move the earlier solo theme to flutes + horns + violins (octaves)
  + celli high, over a tonic pedal in basses and low winds.
- T4 PREFER: when the melody moves to the brass, turn the violins into a unison 16th broken-chord motor
  (2-beat cell) instead of doubling the tune.
- T5 ALLOWED: harmonise a brass tune in trumpet triads (close position) once the unison statement has
  already been heard.

**Accompaniment / ostinato**
- T6 PREFER: open an action cue with a fixed 2-bar neighbour-note eighth ostinato in celesta + harp RH
  + Violin I, a repeated tonic in harp LH, and violas chugging repeated 16ths; let only the octave
  layers increase as it repeats.
- T7 PREFER: for a gallop, give basses + bassoons + contrabassoon + timpani + snare the same
  "8th + two 16ths" rhythm, bass drum on beats 1 and 3, and change chords only in the brass above a
  fixed bass pedal.
- T8 PREFER: split fast woodwind 16ths between two sections in hocket (each plays a slurred pair and
  rests) rather than giving one section continuous 16ths — cleaner with sample libraries too.
