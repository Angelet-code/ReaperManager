## 6.2 Horn (trompa) (scan p.4–12)

**Concepts**
- Descends from the natural horn (18th–19th c.): coiled tube with interchangeable crooks (cilindros/tonos)
  for each key; only natural harmonics. Classical symphonies used **two pairs of horns crooked in two
  different (related) keys**, to get more notes.
- Valves (~1840) made it chromatic. Standard today: horn in F; most common instrument is the **double horn
  (F + Bb)**.
- Transposition: in F, **sounds a P5 (7 st) lower than written**. Usually notated in treble clef, less
  often bass clef (the Adler chart uses the modern bass-clef convention, also P5 down).
- Range (Fig. 2): written F#2 – C6 → **sounding B1 (35) – F5 (77)**. Extra pedal notes of the Bb side:
  written D2–F2 → sounding G1–Bb1 (31–34).
- Registers (Fig. 3, Adler), written → sounding:
  - ~C3–G3 → F2–C3 (41–48): "dark, may be a bit unfocused".
  - ~A3–C4 → D3–F3 (50–53): "deep and solid".
  - C4–G5 → F3–C5 (53–72): "bright and heroic".
  - G5–C6 → C5–F5 (72–77): "brilliant and loud".

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] Write horns mainly in the central registers (most comfortable).
- R2 [AVOID] Frequent pedal (lowest) notes: hard to tune; use sparingly.
- R3 [MUST] After passages in the top register (even fast ones are possible) give rests — very high notes
  are tiring.
- R4 [PREFER] Four horns as **harmonic filler in the middle register** (Humperdinck, *Hänsel und Gretel*
  overture: 4 horns in close chorale, "sehr weich", with 2 bassoons).
- R5 [ALLOWED] Stopped notes (notas tapadas, bouché/stopped/chiuso): hand pushed into the bell gives a
  muted, nasal sound **a semitone higher**, so the player fingers a semitone lower (some horns have a
  transposing valve). Mark stopped "+", open "o" (ouvert/open/aperto). Example: Debussy, *Faune*.
- R6 [ALLOWED] Cuivré ("brassy"; German *schmetternd*): extra lip tension for a metallic, blaring tone
  (Bizet, *L'Arlésienne* Suite 1, Carillon).
- R7 [ALLOWED] Double and triple tonguing exist, but horn attacks are **less penetrating and vigorous than
  trumpet** (long tube). Give the tongue rest after fast passages. Soft tonguing example: Tchaikovsky 5/II.

**Orchestral role / style facts**
- Since Mannheim: the **glue between woodwinds and strings**; chiefly harmonic filling, always in the
  central registers. Early symphonies: strings + 2 oboes + 2 horns (Stamitz).
- 19th c.: soloist, prized for the warmth of the middle register (Ravel, *Pavane*).
- Late Romantic: hard solo and accompaniment parts; section grows from 4 (Classical) to 7–8 (Mahler 1,
  R. Strauss *Don Juan*).

**Agent applicability**
- Horn pads / sustained harmony: concert F3–C5 (53–72); 4 horns in close position there.
- Heroic melody: horns in unison concert ~C4–F5; reserve concert C5–F5 for climaxes and rest afterwards.
- Avoid concert notes below ~F2 except as rare low pedals.

## 6.3 Trumpet (trompeta) (scan p.13–19)

**Concepts**
- Classical natural trumpet: thin coiled tube with bell, many sizes/keys, harmonic notes only; 19th c.
  standard was F trumpet with crooks. Today: trumpet **in C or Bb**.
- Valves (Fig. 12): 2 = −1 semitone; 1 = −1 tone; 3 = −1½ tones; 1+2 = −1½; 2+3 = −2 tones; 1+3 = −2½;
  1+2+3 = −3 tones (6 st). Open harmonics (written): C4, G4, C5, E5, G5, (Bb5), C6.
- Bb trumpet keeps some of the fullness of old trumpets; C trumpet has more intensity and delicacy.
- Ranges (Fig. 13):
  - C trumpet (non-transposing): **F#3 (54) – C6 (84)**.
  - Bb trumpet: written F#3 – D6, **sounds M2 (2 st) lower: E3 (52) – C6 (84)**.
- Registers (Fig. 14, Adler, written): ~F#3–~D4 "rather dull"; ~C4/D4–G5 "clear, bright and most
  articulate"; ~A5–C6 "brilliant but strident".

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Low register: solemn but somewhat sombre.
- R2 [PREFER] Middle register for melodies (Hindemith, *Concerto for Orchestra* I).
- R3 [AVOID] Soft dynamics in the high register: penetrating there and hard to play quietly.
- R4 [ALLOWED] Mute (sordina): penetrating, cutting in forte; in piano an echo / far-away effect
  (Mahler 1/IV opening).
- R5 [PREFER] Fast staccato / accented repeated notes — detached notes are more incisive than on the horn.
- R6 [AVOID] Long stretches of double/triple tonguing (doble/triple picado): very tiring (Verdi, *Aida*
  "Celeste Aida" fanfare figures).
- R7 [ALLOWED] Trills: a few lip trills, mostly valve trills.
- R8 [AVOID] Low-register trills with awkward valve changes (Fig. 18, written): Ab3–Bb3 (2+3 ↔ 1),
  C4–Db4 (open ↔ 1+2+3), B3–C#4 (2 ↔ 1+2+3).

**Auxiliary trumpets (scan p.17)**
- Small trumpet in D (convertible to Eb): sound tends to be shrill, especially high. D sounds M2 higher
  than written, Eb a m3 higher; written ≈F#3–C6 → sounding ≈G#3–D6 (D) and ≈A3–Eb6 (Eb) (top notes
  hard to read [?]).
- Bass trumpet (C or Bb): really a **valve trombone**, normally played by trombonists with a trombone
  mouthpiece. In C sounds an octave lower: written F#3–C6 → **F#2 (42) – C5 (72)**; in Bb sounds a M9
  (14 st) lower → **E2 (40) – Bb4 (70)**.

**Orchestral role / style facts**
- Baroque: fanfares, small brass groups. Classical: often **paired with timpani** for cadential
  reinforcement or harmonic filler in tutti (Beethoven, *Leonore* 2). 19th c.: prominent role, agile and
  brilliant (Mahler 5 opening).

**Agent applicability**
- Trumpet melody: concert ~C4–G5 (60–79). Fanfare / tutti punctuation: trumpets + timpani on cadences.
- Fast repeated 16ths: OK in short bursts; put rests between bursts in long cues.

## 6.4 Trombone (trombón) (scan p.19–24)

**Concepts**
- Slide (vara) changes tube length. Modern orchestra (since late 19th c.): tenor and bass trombones; large
  orchestras use **2 tenors + 1 bass** or **3 tenors**.
- Tenor trombone range (Fig. 24): **E2 (40) – Bb4 (70)**, extension C5–F5 (72–77) in parentheses.
  Notated in bass and tenor clef. Non-transposing.
- Seven slide positions (Fig. 25), each a semitone lower: position I gives the Bb harmonic series,
  position VII the E series (lowest normal note E2).
- Registers (Fig. 27, Adler): E2–~G2 "dark and rather nondescript"; ~A2–~F4 "very strong";
  ~G4–Bb4 "very intense".

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] Fast passages that force extreme slide jumps (e.g. alternating positions VII and I, Fig. 26).
- R2 [ALLOWED] Tone is homogeneous across the range, naturally brighter up high.
- R3 [ALLOWED] Very delicate pianissimo in the low register.
- R4 [ALLOWED] Low-register fortissimo: dramatic, crushing, metallic, with great crescendo power.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Middle register approaches horn timbre but more solid and hard.
- R6 [AVOID] Three trombones in unison against an orchestra you want heard: they dominate the whole mass
  (Rimsky-Korsakov, *Scheherazade* opening). *Why:* the 19th-c. string section grew to balance brass.
- R7 [AVOID] Pedal notes (Fig. 29: Bb1 down to E1, MIDI 34–28): possible but insecure, especially
  below G1 (31).

**Orchestral role / style facts**
- Classical: cadential support and harmonic filler (like trumpets). 19th c.: solid bass-line instrument,
  sometimes soloist (Schubert, *Unfinished* I: soft trombone chords; Puccini, *Tosca* I).

**Agent applicability**
- Brass chorale: trombones concert A2–F4; bass trombone takes the bass.
- For a soft dark low chord, trombones pp are fine; for a climax, trombone ff in A2–D4.
- Don't put 3 trombones in unison on the melody unless you want it to override everything.

## 6.5 Tuba (scan p.24–27)

**Concepts**
- Player chooses among tubas per score; the orchestral standard is the **12-foot F tuba**. Tubas are
  **non-transposing** (a Bb tuba exists, used in bands).
- The lowest four notes are pedal tones: good quality but hard to get.
- Usual range of F tuba (Fig. 33): lowest note ~D1 (26) [?] (four ledger lines below bass staff) to
  ~F4/G4 (65/67) [?].
- Registers (Fig. 34, Adler, approximate [?]): lowest notes–~Db2 "deep and heavy"; ~D2–~D3 "very strong";
  ~E3–top "getting weaker but quite intense".

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Very low notes only in slow motion; there they work at any dynamic, ff to pp.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Middle register is more agile than expected, but the player needs frequent breaths.
- R3 [AVOID] Top register: thins, loses character, can be shrill and hard to control.
- R4 [PREFER] Most beautiful tuba sound (Piston): legato, piano phrases in the middle register.
- Example: Mussorgsky/Ravel, *Pictures*, "Bydlo": solo usually on a small (tenor) Bb tuba; the F tuba
  suits the rest of the work, but the C#–G# leap of the solo is very hard on it.

**Orchestral role / style facts**
- Joined the orchestra in the second half of the 19th c. with the brass expansion; central to the
  late-Romantic bass (Wagner, Mahler, R. Strauss, early Schoenberg). Wagner, *Faust* overture shows typical
  low writing.

**Agent applicability**
- Tuba bass under brass: concert ~D2–D3 is the strong zone; below that keep notes long and slow.
- Lyrical tuba solo: middle register, legato, soft dynamics.

## Cross-brass summary for agents (inferred)
- Penetration ranking for attacks: trumpet > trombone > horn (horn is the blending member).
- Endurance: horn top register and trumpet multiple tonguing need rests; tuba needs breaths.
- Standard section (19th c. onward): 4 horns, 2–3 trumpets, 3 trombones (2 tenor + bass or 3 tenor), 1 tuba.
