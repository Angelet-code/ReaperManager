# Adler, El estudio de la orquestación — scans 526–547 (printed 527–545)

Range content: the last pages of ch.14, which is a chapter on *using* the percussion section (timpani, untuned
percussion, keyboard percussion, keyboards) inside the orchestra. It is mostly full-score examples with short
commentary, in three functional sections: (a) end of an example on percussion at a climax/ending (Brahms),
(b) "Creating a dramatic beginning" (Creación de un principio dramático), (c) "Colouring notes and passages"
(Coloración de notas y pasajes). Ch.14 ends at printed p.544 with no closing study list in this range; scan 544
is the title page of Part II "Orquestación"; scan 546 is the cover of the Spanish Volume II. The horn section
(printed p.312–325) is not in this range.

## Percussion at a climax / final cadence — Example 14-23 end (scan p.526–528, printed p.527–529)

Section title not in range (starts on earlier pages) [?]. Only the example's end is here.

**Examples**
- Brahms, *Symphony No. 2*, IV, m.392–end (D major, Allegro molto, CD-5/18). Shows how timpani are held back and
  then added in stages to drive the coda: (1) timpani play only a few quarter notes at m.392 under the tutti;
  (2) a timpani roll (redoble) enters *f* around m.404 under sustained winds/brass; (3) from m.408 timpani
  join trumpets and horns in a repeated-note rhythmic figure (repeated eighths/sixteenths on tonic and dominant,
  D and A), the strings and bassoons running in eighths; (4) another roll under the long high chord m.413–416;
  (5) on the final *ff* page timpani alternate roll and detached quarter notes, punctuating the staccato
  tutti chords, and roll into the final fermata chord. Trombones and tuba are saved and enter late with sustained
  *ff* chords (m.424ff) for the last surge.

**Rules**
- R1 PREFER (inferred from the example) Hold timpani back in a long climax: enter first with isolated strokes,
  then a roll under sustained chords, then rhythmic doubling of the brass figure, then a roll into the last
  chord. Each stage adds intensity without changing the notes of the rest of the orchestra.
- R2 PREFER (inferred) In a Classical/Romantic tonal climax, keep timpani on tonic and dominant (here D and A)
  and double the rhythm of trumpets/horns rather than giving them an independent rhythm. `[era: 19th-c. two-drum
  timpani]`
- R3 PREFER (inferred) Save trombones for the very last stage of a long build (Brahms enters them only in the
  closing bars) so the final surge has a new colour and weight.

**Agent applicability**
- For an epic ending: timpani track = sparse hits → roll (CC1 crescendo on a roll articulation) → eighth-note
  doubling of the brass rhythm on tonic/dominant → roll into the final chord; trombones only in the last 4–8
  bars.

## Creating a dramatic beginning (Creación de un principio dramático) (scan p.528–534, printed p.529–535)

**Concepts**
- "Ear-opener" (destapa oídos): a percussion sound at the very start of a piece or movement used as a
  dramatic attention-getter.

**Rules**
- R4 ALLOWED A percussion sound alone can open a piece or movement; a dramatic moment may sit right at the
  start, not only at a climax.
- R5 ALLOWED Percussion need not be loud to be dramatic: a soft solo timpani motif emerging from silence creates
  a mysterious atmosphere (Beethoven, Violin Concerto). *Why:* novelty and contrast; Adler notes that few or no
  important works before it had used the effect, and contemporary critics remarked on the sound.
  `[era: early-19th-c. novelty; now a standard device]`
- R6 PREFER Use a cymbal crash (golpe de platos) to change the atmosphere abruptly after a slow or soft
  movement (Tchaikovsky 4, finale opening). *Why:* it is "infallible" for a sudden change of mood.
- R7 MUST Distinguish let-ring and choked cymbal strokes in the notation: write a tie/ "l.v." (laissez vibrer)
  for strokes that should ring, and a staccato dot on strokes that must be damped immediately. *Why:* in
  Tchaikovsky 4 both kinds are written as identical eighth notes and only performance tradition tells the
  player that m.1 and m.5 ring while m.4 is choked so it does not invade the rests on beats 3–4.
- R8 ALLOWED Timpani can rise out of a crescendo to dominate the whole orchestra and then become an eighth-note
  ostinato under the exposition of the main theme (Shostakovich 5, finale).
- R9 ALLOWED Antiphonal (spatial) percussion: two snare drums placed left and right, the first playing
  loud, the second answering as a soft echo, then both combining in a crescendo to *forte* (Rossini, *La Gazza
  Ladra*). `[era: performance tradition said to come from the first performances (1817); not always in printed
  parts but still common practice]`

**Examples**
- Rossini, *La Gazza Ladra*, Overture, m.1–12 (E major, Maestoso marziale, CD-5/19). Snare drum solo: m.1 a
  roll *f*, m.2 the same *p* (echo, second drummer), m.3 roll *mf* crescendo, then tutti *ff* m.4 with a
  dotted march rhythm. In the march, timpani, triangle and bass drum all double the dotted rhythm of the brass
  (bass drum on every quarter); snare adds triplet-figure fill bars; piccolo/flute/violins carry the melody with
  trills. Natural horns in E and G and trumpet in A. `[era: natural brass with crooks, early 19th c.]`
- Beethoven, *Violin Concerto*, I, m.1–10 (D major, Allegro ma non troppo; CD track not shown). Timpani alone *p*:
  four repeated quarter notes on D (the motto) in m.1; woodwind chorale *p dolce* answers; the timpani motto
  returns in m.5 and m.9 (*p*); in m.10 the same repeated-note motive passes to violin 1 on D# (*p*). Shows a
  soft timpani motif that is thematic, not merely rhythmic support.
- Tchaikovsky, *Symphony No. 4*, IV, m.1–8 (F major, Allegro con fuoco, CD-5/21). Tutti *ff*: cymbals + bass
  drum and timpani hit beat 1 of m.1 (one eighth note) and m.5; flutes/piccolo/violins run 16th-note
  descending scales doubled in thirds/sixths by clarinets and bassoons; brass sustain a long chord then stop;
  the cymbal stroke in m.4 cuts off the first scale (choked), the one in m.5 restarts the repeat (ringing).
- Shostakovich, *Symphony No. 5*, IV, at rehearsal 97 (D minor, Allegro non troppo, quarter = 88,
  CD-5/22). Woodwinds and brass hold a trilled/sustained chord with *f* < *fff* into a cut-off; timpani roll
  *p* < *ff* under it, then immediately an eighth-note ostinato alternating two pitches (tonic–dominant, A and
  D) that continues alone, then under trumpets 1–3 and trombones stating the march theme *ff marc.* in octaves
  from m.3.

**Agent applicability**
- Dramatic soft opening: solo timpani, 4 repeated quarters on the tonic, velocity ~40–50, then a woodwind
  answer; reuse the repeated-note motif later in the strings.
- Big opening after a soft movement: one tutti hit with cymbal + bass drum + timpani on beat 1; in samples use
  a long-ring crash for "l.v." strokes and a choke articulation (or a short note with fast release) where the
  next beats must be silent.
- Martial opening: snare roll *f* (one side, pan L), echo *p* (pan R), roll crescendo, then tutti; triangle,
  bass drum and timpani double the brass dotted rhythm.
- Finale opening: timpani roll crescendo under a held tutti, then tonic–dominant eighth ostinato carrying into
  the brass theme.

## Colouring notes and passages (Coloración de notas y pasajes) (scan p.535–543, printed p.536–544)

**Concepts**
- Colouring (coloración): percussion added to specific notes or rhythms played by other instruments, to tint
  them rather than to carry material of its own.
- Antique cymbals (crótalos, platos antiguos): small tuned cymbals, a bright, bell-like pitched ring.
- Chinese/temple blocks (cajas chinas): Adler uses the term for the wood block (W. Bl.) part in Bernstein.

**Rules**
- R10 ALLOWED A whole section can be coloured by a few percussion instruments: timpani, bass drum and tam-tam
  added to a low wind/string ostinato produce the effect of a slow, heavy procession (cortejo) (Stravinsky,
  Rite of Spring).
- R11 PREFER (inferred from the Stravinsky example) When percussion colour a procession/ostinato, give the
  timpani steady staccato eighths with hard sticks (bacchette dure) and give bass drum and tam-tam sparse,
  irregularly placed strokes that do not line up with each other or with the barline, so the texture breathes
  instead of becoming a march.
- R12 PREFER Double a sustained pedal note with a soft pitched metal percussion colour to make an ending
  ethereal: antique cymbals doubling the 3rd horn's pedal E become the most prominent colour of Debussy's
  ending. *Why:* the crotales add a halo over a slightly dissonant cadential figure.
- R13 ALLOWED Use keyboard instruments (celesta, piano) and harp to mark every chord change in high sustained
  strings with one short accented attack each (Bassett). *Why:* the attack articulates the moment of change in
  an otherwise smooth string texture.
- R14 ALLOWED Percussion can become part of the thematic material, not just colour: in Bernstein's Danzón the
  timpani reinforce the strings' pizzicato theme, and a short wood-block figure becomes integral to the theme,
  returning with the pizzicato every time the theme returns.
- R15 PREFER (Bernstein, ex.14-32) When a percussion figure has been tied to a theme, expand it at the end of
  the section into a solo over sparse accompaniment to create a satisfying conclusion; finish with a tutti
  chord and timpani *sfz*.

**Examples**
- Stravinsky, *The Rite of Spring*, Part I, "Procession of the Sage" (El Cortejo del Sabio), from the bar the
  book labels m.114 (Pesante, 4/4, then 6/4, CD-5/23). Bassoons 1–3 and contrabassoons 1–2 in a low staccato
  ostinato; horns 1–4 sustained a2, horns 5–6 short accented chords on the off-beat; tenor and bass tubas a2 in
  long notes; timpani continuous staccato eighths with hard sticks (*mf*, sempre stacc.); bass drum *sempre
  marc.*; three solo cellos and three solo basses in staccato figures; from m.5 tam-tam *mf sempre marc.* and
  divided violas/cellos/basses join; from m.13 (6/4, full orchestra *ff/fff*) bass drum and tam-tam play
  duplet halves against the 6/4, a guiro scrapes with up/down strokes, violins *sul ponticello*, piccolo
  trumpet, four trumpets, trombones and tubas pile in.
- Debussy, *Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune*, m.106 to the end (E major, 12/8, "Très lent et très retenu
  jusqu'à la fin", CD-5/24). Muted horns (sourdines) *ppp*, two harps, two solo violins, divided violins,
  violas, solo cello *ppp*; antique cymbals enter *ppp* on the pedal E with the 3rd horn and ring over the last
  bars; the piece ends with divided cello and bass pizzicato *ppp*.
- Leslie Bassett, *Variations for Orchestra*, from 1 bar before to 4 bars after rehearsal PP (4/4,
  quarter = 62, CD-5/25). Violins divided in high register (8va) hold *pp* chords; at each new chord, a single
  short marcato (^) note in celesta, harp or piano (*mp*), mostly high register, marks the change; violas,
  cellos and divided basses add *pizz. preciso* chords and short lines that grow from *p/pp* to *mf* and turn
  *unis.*/arco.
- Bernstein, *Fancy Free*, "Danzón", m.737–743 (2-sharp key signature, "Strong, moderate quarters", quarter = 120,
  meter changes C–2/4–C, CD-5/26). String pizzicato theme *f*, accented quarters; timpani double the
  pizzicato rhythm *f*; wood block *mf* adds a short syncopated figure; bassoons and clarinets a2 in staccato
  syncopations; piano *f brittle* 8va and flutes/oboe in accented 16th figures.
- Bernstein, *Fancy Free*, "Danzón", m.820–830 (G major, quarter = 120, C then 3/4, CD-5/27). The wood block,
  so far sparing, now plays a solo of eighth-note patterns *p*; strings pizzicato *pp* single notes; flutes,
  oboe, clarinet, bassoon staccato chords *p/pp/ppp*; a sudden tutti chord *f/ff* arco with accented brass and
  timpani *sfz* closes the section.

**Agent applicability**
- Procession/ritual cue: low bassoon/contrabassoon (or cello/bass) staccato ostinato + timpani staccato eighths
  (hard-stick patch, velocity steady ~80) + bass drum and tam-tam on irregular beats, not every bar.
- Ethereal ending: put a crotale (antique cymbal) note on the sustained pedal pitch *ppp* (velocity ~25–35),
  doubled by a muted horn; let it ring over the final bars.
- Sparkle on chord changes: on each new string chord, one short accented celesta/harp/piano note in the high
  register at the chord onset (velocity ~70), nothing in between.
- Latin/dance pizzicato theme: double the pizzicato bass rhythm with timpani; give a wood block a fixed short
  figure that returns with the theme; at the section end let the wood block play solo, then a tutti hit.
