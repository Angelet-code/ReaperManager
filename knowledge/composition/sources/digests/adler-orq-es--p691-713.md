# Adler, El estudio de la orquestación — scans 691–713 (printed p.690–712)

Content: three complete or partial "before/after" pairs (original keyboard/chamber version vs. orchestral
version) plus Adler's commentary on the Ravel and Stravinsky ones. Most pages are full scores; rules below
for Bizet are read from the score (the prose about it is in the previous range) and are marked (inferred).

## 17 (cont.) Bizet, *Jeux d'enfants*, "La Toupie" (The Top) — Ex. 17-11 (scan p.691–700, printed p.690–699)

Ex. 17-11a: piano four hands (secondo/primo), Allegro vivo, quarter = 152, 2/4, complete (approx. 74 bars).
Ex. 17-11b: Bizet's own orchestral version (CD-5 track 72). Orchestra: 2 Fl, 2 Ob, 2 Cl in A, 2 Bsn,
Hn 1-2 in F, Hn 3-4 in C, 2 Tpt in A, Timp, strings.

**Concepts**
- Piano four hands (piano a cuatro manos): *secondo* = lower player (bass + accompaniment figure), *primo*
  = upper player (melody + its chords). The transcriber redistributes four hands' worth of layers.
- Ostinato "motor" figure: the secondo's continuous slurred sixteenths (the spinning top's hum).

**Rules** (all read from the score, (inferred) as rules)
- R1 [PREFER] Give a continuous keyboard sixteenth-note ostinato to one mid-register string section that can
  sustain it without fatigue: Bizet puts it in the violas almost throughout (slurred groups, ff → dim. → pp),
  with cellos joining the sixteenths in louder/recap passages. (inferred)
- R2 [PREFER] Give a light staccato eighth-note melody to two flutes in parallel thirds/sixths, p, with the bass
  as cello eighths on the beat (later pizz.) and the keyboard's off-beat chords as horn (Hn 3-4) or clarinet
  after-beats. Melody, bass and after-beat each get a different family. (inferred)
- R3 [PREFER] Mark the opening/closing ff "hits" as one staccato tutti eighth chord (all winds, brass, timp,
  strings), with the strings immediately falling into the sixteenth figure fp/dim.: the hit gives the attack,
  the strings keep the motion. (inferred)
- R4 [ALLOWED] Answering phrases in the woodwind choir (Ob/Cl/Bsn chords, p/pp, staccato) alternate with the
  flute melody; violins enter pizz. (Vln 2 div.) to reinforce the melody before taking it arco. (inferred)
- R5 [PREFER] A held, fading keyboard chord (ppp, tied over 3 bars) becomes a full sustained wind + brass +
  arco-string chord ppp with a timpani roll ppp: the orchestra realises the piano's decay as a soft sustained
  pad. (inferred)
- R6 [PREFER] Distribute a keyboard arpeggio/run (triplet eighths) as a relay across sections in register order:
  Vc (mf, dim.) → Vla (pp) → Vln 2 (pp) → Vln 1 (pp), each taking one segment. (inferred)
- R7 [PREFER] On the repeat of a section, re-colour rather than copy: in the reprise the same triplet relay goes
  to clarinets + bassoons instead of strings, a chromatic descending line goes to Cl a2 + Bsn a2 (f, dim.
  molto), and the melody is doubled by flutes + violins with trumpets taking the after-beats while Hn 3-4 hold
  a long pedal. (inferred)
- R8 [ALLOWED] [era: natural horns/trumpets, 19th c.] Bizet scores horns in two different crooks (F and C) and
  trumpets in A so that the available open notes cover the harmony; with valved horns this split is not needed,
  but the habit of giving brass simple after-beats and pedals follows from it. (inferred)

**Agent applicability**
- When arranging piano music, map layers first: ostinato → violas (or Vla+Vc), melody → flute pair,
  bass → Vc/DB pizz eighths, after-beat chords → horns or clarinets.
- On every return of a section, change at least one layer's family (strings ↔ clarinets/bassoons).
- Render a held piano chord as a ppp sustained tutti + timp roll, with a single moving line (Vc) inside it.

## 17 (cont.) Ravel, *Ma Mère l'Oye*, "Petit Poucet" (Tom Thumb / Pulgarcito) — Ex. 17-12 + commentary (scan p.700–709, printed p.699–708)

Ex. 17-12a: piano four hands, Très modéré, quarter = 66, constantly changing meter (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4), C minor,
complete (80 bars). Ex. 17-12b: Ravel's orchestral version (CD-5 track 73): Picc, Fl, Ob, Eng Hn, Cl in Bb,
Bsn, 2 Hn in F, strings with solo violins 1-3 and solo cello.

**Concepts**
- Motor unit (unidad motora): the rhythmic value that drives both melody and accompaniment (here eighths).
- Foreground / background (primer plano / plano de fondo) re-coloured at every entry of the melody.
- Pedal-resonance imitation: an orchestral sustained note that recreates the piano's damper-pedal halo.

**Rules** (from Adler's text unless marked)
- R1 [PREFER] When melody and accompaniment move in the same note value, separate them by strong timbral
  contrast: muted violins (sourdines, pp, stepwise eighths in two parallel lines) as background vs. solo oboe
  (pp expressif) as melody. *Why:* the listener cannot separate them by rhythm, only by colour.
- R2 [MUST] In a transcription, notate every articulation, note length and note ending in detail; Ravel
  specifies even the smallest ones. (For MIDI: set exact note-off lengths and phrase ends per part.)
- R3 [PREFER] Recreate a keyboard's long resonance (a continuous slur over the secondo) by overlapping
  instrumental backgrounds: at bar 12 the violin lines overlap the entering low-string lines instead of
  stopping before them.
- R4 [PREFER] A slurred eighth-note pattern in violas + cellos (muted) makes a light, regularly articulated
  string accompaniment that supports a freer, more imaginative solo phrasing above it.
- R5 [ALLOWED] When the melody passes to a new instrument (oboe → English horn, darker), give it a new
  articulation (new slurring) too.
- R6 [PREFER] Imitate the piano pedal with a held low wind note lightly accented by a DB pizz. at its onset:
  clarinet held concert Eb3 (MIDI 51; written F3 for Bb clarinet, p, hairpin dim.) + DB pizz. p.
- R7 [PREFER] Change foreground and background colour at each new entry of the melody; sometimes overlap the new
  background with the old one, sometimes give it a fresh articulation.
- R8 [ALLOWED] A climax can be built on a thin harmonic texture made mostly of octave doublings, provided most
  instruments sit in an intense register: Fl, Ob, Eng Hn, Cl and violins "f très expressif" (bars ~27–37) sound
  full because of register, not chord density. Follow with a diminuendo as the lines descend.
- R9 [ALLOWED] After the climax, return to the earlier theme in its earlier colour but transposed: Eng Hn theme
  a fifth lower than its first statement (bar 40).
- R10 [ALLOWED] Go beyond literal transcription when the piano idea can grow into an orchestral effect
  (bars 51–54): over a D pedal (DB arco with harmonic [?], Cl at its lowest concert D3 = MIDI 50, Hn 1 concert
  D4 = MIDI 62, pp), solo Bsn plays the melody "expressif" in parallel with muted violas.
- R11 [ALLOWED] Expand a keyboard ornament into a colour effect: the primo's grace note G#7–A7 [? register as
  given by the source, MIDI 104–105] becomes a chromatic glissando of string harmonics F#7→A7 [?] in the solo
  first violin. Ravel does this in many of his piano-to-orchestra transcriptions.
- R12 [ALLOWED] Mystery layer: glissando sul tasto (sur la touche) of the remaining first violins and then of
  the cellos, trills of solo violins 2 and 3 (sans sourdine, p), and a sul tasto tremolo in second violins (pp).
  The solo-violin trills imitate a piccolo motive (p, staccato grace figure) that is not in the piano part.
- R13 [PREFER] Keep one constant element through an unusual interlude (here Bsn solo + viola accompaniment);
  use it to lead back to the opening, more typical orchestration ("jeu ord." at bar 55).
- R14 [ALLOWED] Refer back to a special interlude once near the end: nine bars before the end the cellos play a
  harmonic that leads into a chord of similar colouring (divided cellos, pp).
- R15 [ALLOWED] Contrast colours in succession: solo cello + piccolo (hollow piccolo tone; score shows the
  pairing from bar 61) followed by a very quiet solo flute line descending from its rich but breathy middle
  register into its low register.
- R16 [PREFER] Close with exactly the opening colours (muted violins in eighths + oboe, "Un peu retenu", ppp) for
  the last five bars, supported by a natural-harmonic chord in 4 solo violas (pp, fermata).

**Examples** (score observations)
- Opening: Vln 1 + Vln 2 muted, pp, two slurred stepwise eighth lines; oboe solo enters bar 4, pp expressif.
- Bar 12–21: Eng Hn melody p expressif; muted Vla + Vc slurred eighths; Bb Cl held low note p with DB pizz.
- Bars 22–27: flute solo pp, then clarinet solo pp; 2 muted horns hold pp; violins pp eighths.
- Bars 61–68: piccolo melody; solo cello (tenor clef) in dialogue; Vln 1 div. muted p; bassoon chords p;
  2 horns pp; DB pizz. accents pp.

**Agent applicability**
- For a piano piece with uniform eighths everywhere, assign background to muted strings (CON SORD articulation /
  softer, darker patch) and melody to one solo woodwind; switch the solo instrument at each melodic entry.
- Fake the piano's sustain pedal with a long low clarinet or horn note started together with a pizz. bass note.
- Thin octave-doubled climaxes work if the doubled instruments are in their strong upper-middle registers.
- For a "magical" interlude: pedal + solo bassoon + muted violas, plus sparse harmonic glissandi, sul tasto
  tremolo and solo-violin trills (high, p); then return to the opening colours literally for the coda.

## 17 (cont.) Transcription from small chamber group to orchestra (transcripción de pequeño grupo de cámara a orquesta): Stravinsky, *Pulcinella* — Ex. 17-13 (scan p.709–713, printed p.708–712)

Source: Trio Sonata in G major, 1st mvt., attributed to Pergolesi by the 19th-c. musicologist Hugo Riemann (whose
edition and continuo realisation, with his own dynamics, Stravinsky used); actually by Domenico Gallo.
Ex. 17-13a: 2 violins, cello + Riemann's piano continuo realisation, Moderato, bars 1–15.
Ex. 17-13b: Stravinsky, *Pulcinella* Sinfonia, bars 1–15 (CD-5 track 74), Allegro moderato, quarter = 90:
2 Ob, 2 Bsn, 2 Hn in F, solo string quintet (Vln 1, Vln 2, Vla, Vc, DB soli) + ripieno string section.

**Concepts**
- Concertino vs. ripieno (concertino o ripieno, concerto grosso): Stravinsky's solo quintet vs. orchestral string
  quintet — the late-Baroque concerto grosso layout. [era: late Baroque concerto grosso, reinterpreted 1920]
- Recomposition (re-writing borrowed material so it sounds like the arranger): Stravinsky first studied the
  borrowed composer's whole output and techniques, then transcribed.

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] Rebar so one meter (4/4) holds most of the time; fewer meter changes means fewer ensemble problems.
  (Some 2/4 and 3/4 bars still occur.)
- R2 [ALLOWED] Reinterpret ornaments for the new medium: a mordent at the end of trio bar 4 becomes a double
  grace note in orchestral bar 5.
- R3 [ALLOWED] Do not inherit an editor's dynamics: Stravinsky's dynamics differ from Riemann's 19th-c. ones (he
  ignored or changed them). Start the first phrase forte (tutti).
- R4 [PREFER] Orchestrate a dynamic contrast between phrases by changing texture density: phrase 2 (bars 5–6)
  is lighter than phrase 1.
- R5 [PREFER] Then remove woodwinds, horns and double basses to change colour, so that the solo oboe + solo
  bassoon entry at bar 7 sounds fresh. (Score: under it, solo strings hold pp whole notes, Vc/DB give p
  continuo-like repeated eighths.)
- R6 [PREFER] Write a melodic counterpoint in an inner/bass wind (bassoon, bars 7–10, with trills) that
  reinforces the important harmony notes given by the original figured bass.
- R7 [ALLOWED] Realise the continuo harmony sometimes more simply, sometimes more contrapuntally than the
  editor's uniform realisation, but never change the composer's harmonic intentions.
- R8 [PREFER] Make colour change the main tool: change it smoothly, never break a phrase unless necessary,
  always preserve the form. *Why:* that is why the melodies project so cleanly.

**Examples** (score observations, bars 1–6 tutti f)
- Ob 1 plays a plainer (unornamented) version of the Vln 1 melody, Ob 2 the second violin line; Bsn 1-2 take
  the bass and a tenor line; Hn 1-2 fill harmony; solo quintet and ripieno strings double each other (Vln 2
  ripieno "non div." double stops). Bar 13: oboes soli in thirds, bassoons in parallel; Vc/DB soli sf staccato
  upbeat run.

**Agent applicability**
- To orchestrate a Baroque trio sonata: concertino (solo strings) vs. ripieno (section strings); winds double
  outer voices in tutti phrases only, drop out for contrast; a solo oboe + bassoon pair takes the lyrical phrase.
- Keep 4/4 where possible when building the MIDI bar grid; take the harmony from the figured bass, not from a
  later editor's realisation.

## Cross-example rules (inferred from all three)
- R1 [PREFER] Each structural layer (melody, ostinato/motor, bass, harmony fill) gets its own family or articulation.
- R2 [PREFER] Reprises are re-coloured, but the final bars often return literally to the opening colour.
- R3 [ALLOWED] Orchestral resources (sustain, harmonics, glissandi, trills, mutes) can replace or enlarge keyboard
  devices (pedal, grace notes, decay).
