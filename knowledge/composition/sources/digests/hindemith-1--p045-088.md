# Hindemith, Armonía tradicional I — scans 045–088

Notation used by the book (and kept here): Roman numerals with figured-bass subscripts, e.g. I6 = first
inversion, I6/4 = second inversion, V6/5 V4/3 V2 = V7 inversions. Chapter VI (before this range) adds
dominant-family symbols: V9 (dominant ninth), V7/6/5 and V10/4/2 (its inversions), V11/6/5 (dominant with
11th) and V13/7 (dominant seventh with 13th). "+" over a note marks a non-chord tone. "mal" = wrong.

## VI Chords derived from V7 — tail (scan p.45, printed p.38)

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] Progressions that use V13/7 (dominant seventh + 13th, in C: G–B–F–E) frequently produce
  consecutive octaves into the next chord; check them explicitly. *Why:* the 13th and the 7th move to
  chord tones that other voices may also reach in parallel. (Example in C major, V13/7 → I, shown with
  bracketed parallel octaves; exact voicing only partly legible [?].)

**Exceptions / conditions**
- Context from p.44 (outside range, for linkage): hidden 5ths/8ves are now allowed between two inner
  voices or soprano + inner voice if disguised by strong contrary motion in another voice; hidden octaves
  reached from a 7th or 9th stay forbidden; augmented/diminished melodic steps are now allowed when they
  improve fluency (esp. in minor).

**Examples**
- Ex. 38 (end): melodies in Eb minor and C# minor harmonized with I, IV6, V, V2, V6/5, V9, V11/6/5, V13/7.

**Agent applicability**
- After writing any V13/7→I, run a parallel-octave check between all voice pairs.

## VII Non-chord tones (Notas extrañas al acorde) (scan p.46–54, printed p.39–47)

**Concepts**
- Neighbor tone (bordadura): note a 2nd above or below a chord tone, placed between that tone and its
  repetition, on a weaker metric position than the chord tone. E.g. C chord, soprano C5–B4/D5–C5.
- Passing tone(s) (notas de paso): one or more stepwise notes filling the space between two *different*
  chord tones, on a weaker metric position than those chord tones. E.g. C chord, E5–D5–C5 or C5–D5–E5.
- Suspension (retardo): a note a 2nd away from a chord tone that it delays. *Prepared* (preparado): the
  same voice holds it as a chord tone of the previous chord. *Resolved* (resuelto): moves by step to the
  real chord tone. It sits on a stronger metric position than both preparation and resolution.
- Anticipation (anticipación): a chord tone sounded early, in the immediately preceding chord, on a weak
  metric position (e.g. G chord, soprano B4 then C5 as an off-beat eighth, then C chord with C5).
- Appoggiatura (apoyatura): a suspension without preparation; every suspension rule applies except the
  preparation.
- Escape tone (escapatoria): leaves a chord tone by step and leaps to a chord tone of the next chord;
  sits on a weaker metric position than its resolution.
- Escape tone reached by leap (escapatoria alcanzada por salto): approached by leap from the previous
  chord tone, then moves by step to a chord tone (the "incomplete neighbor / leaped-to" type).
- Free notes (notas libres): exceptional non-chord tones that fit none of the above categories (example:
  a chromatic leaping note in the soprano over a C major → V motion; detail [?]).

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Neighbor and passing tones fall on a weaker metric position than the chord tones they
  connect.
- R2 [MUST] Suspension = prepared in the same voice as a chord tone, held into a stronger beat, resolved
  by step to the chord tone.
- R3 [PREFER] Downward-resolving suspensions; upward-resolving suspensions are less frequent (example in
  C: B4 held into the C chord, resolving up to C5).
- R4 [ALLOWED] When the suspension resolves to the *third* of a **minor** chord, that third may be
  doubled.
- R5 [AVOID] Doubling the third of a **major** chord when a suspension resolves to it (can sound
  disturbing).
- R6 [NEVER] Double the third when it is the leading tone (third of the dominant chord) and a suspension
  resolves to it. (Example "mal": C5 suspended over G chord → B4 while B is already in a lower voice.)
- R7 [ALLOWED] Insert other notes between a suspension and its resolution (ornamented resolution: e.g.
  C5 held, then A4 / B4 decoration, then resolution).
- R8 [NEVER] Let a suspension "hide" consecutive octaves/fifths: if the voice pair forms parallel 8ves/5ths
  when the suspension is replaced by its resolution note, it is still wrong (examples "mal": bass G→C
  with soprano D5 suspended→C5 against an octave/fifth in the previous chord). (interpretation of the
  examples)
- R9 [ALLOWED] Such suspension-delayed octaves/fifths only in slow tempo (example "sólo es posible en
  tiempo lento", where the delay is long enough to separate them).
- R10 [ALLOWED] Consecutive fifths between a non-chord tone and a chord tone may be used freely.
- R11 [NEVER] Consecutive octaves between a non-chord tone and a chord tone — avoid strictly.
- R12 [NEVER] Consecutive fifths between two non-chord tones of the same kind (two suspensions, two
  passing tones, etc.) — as bad as fifths between chord tones. (Example in D minor/F: an allowed vs. a
  forbidden figure over V→i; noteheads partly ambiguous [?].)
- R13 [ALLOWED] Non-chord tones may cause doublings normally avoided — the leading tone, the 7th of a
  seventh chord, the 9th or 7th of V9, the 13th (=6) or 7th of V13/7. These are harmless because the "+"
  notes are transitory.
- R14 [PREFER] When harmonizing an unfigured melody, give priority to a perfect bass line: write the whole
  bass first, while thinking of the chords the melody implies.

**Exceptions / conditions**
- Complex chords (V9, sometimes even V7) can often be read as simpler chords plus non-chord tones; the
  analysis choice is free.
- R9 is the exception to R8.

**Examples**
- Suspension figures in C: soprano C5 tied over G chord → B4; D5 tied over C chord → C5; in another voice
  a 9–8 over bass.
- Ex. 39 melodies (C major, A minor, G, E minor, D, B minor, A, F# minor, E, C# minor, B, G# minor, Gb,
  Eb minor, Db, Bb minor, Ab, F minor, Eb, C minor, Bb, G minor, F, D minor) with Roman numerals under
  long harmonic spans (I——IV——I6——V6/5) and "+" on passing/neighbor tones: one harmony per beat or per
  bar, melody ornamented by NCTs.

**Agent applicability**
- Generate melodies over a slower harmonic rhythm by inserting passing/neighbor tones on weak
  subdivisions; mark them as NCT so voice-leading checks treat them correctly.
- Suspension generator: tie the note from the previous chord across the strong beat, resolve down a step
  on the weak part; never resolve onto a leading tone already present in another voice.
- Parallel-check policy: 5ths NCT↔chord tone = OK; 8ves NCT↔chord tone = fail; 5ths NCT↔NCT of same type
  = fail; check "underlying" voice-leading with suspensions replaced by their resolutions (fail unless
  tempo is slow).
- Bass-first workflow for harmonizing a given melody.

## VIII The 6/4 chord; the II6/5 chord (Acorde de 6/4; acorde de II 6/5) (scan p.55–59, printed p.48–52)

**Concepts**
- 6/4 chord (acorde de cuarta y sexta): second inversion, fifth in the bass; figured 6/4. E.g. C: I6/4 =
  G–C–E; D minor: IV6/4 = D–G–Bb.
- II6/5: subdominant chord with added sixth (acorde de subdominante con sexta agregada). C major: F–A–C–D;
  C minor: F–Ab–C–D. The characteristic interval is the 2nd/9th C–D.

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] In a 6/4 chord double the fifth (the bass note); less often the root or third.
- R2 [PREFER] Use I6/4 (a) before the dominant in a final cadence — it acts as V with two appoggiaturas
  (C: bass G, upper C5/E5 → B4/D5); or (b) as a passing chord or double-neighbor chord (same function as
  passing/neighbor tones).
- R3 [PREFER] IV6/4 and V6/4 are mainly chords produced by neighbor, passing or anticipation motion (e.g.
  pedal C3: I – IV6/4 – I, upper voices G4–C5–E5 → A4–C5–F5 → back).
- R4 [PREFER] II6/5 especially in endings, before I6/4, V or V7.
- R5 [NEVER] Omit the notes forming the characteristic 2nd/9th of II6/5 (in C: C and D).
- R6 [ALLOWED] Omit the third of II6/5 (A / Ab, the major/minor-defining note); then the doubling is free
  (examples: F–C–D with F or C doubled).
- R7 [NEVER] In an incomplete II6/5 used as neighbor chord to the following dominant, double the note
  that acts as appoggiatura/suspension resolving to the dominant's third (C: the C that goes to B).
  (Example "mal": C doubled in II6/5 → V.)

**Exceptions / conditions**
- Incomplete II6/5, like I6/4, often behaves as a neighbor chord to V rather than an independent
  harmony.

**Examples** (Ex. 41 progressions to play — reusable templates)
- A: I6/4–V7–I · E min: V–I6/4–IV · G: I–IV6/4–I · C min: IV–I6/4–V–I · Eb: IV–V6/4–I ·
  C# min: I6–II6/5–I6/4–V7–I · F#: II6/5–V7–I · Eb min: I6–V6/4–I–II6/5–I6/4–V7–I.
- Ex. 42/43: figured basses and melodies using 6/4, II6/5, V13/7, V11/6/5.
- Ex. 42 instruction: a melody over figured bass must be a fluent line, not crawl note to note; write the
  whole melody before the inner voices.

**Agent applicability**
- Cadence template (4-part, C major): II6/5 (F2 bass, A3 C4 D4/…) → I6/4 (G2, G3 C4 E4) → V7 (G2, G3 B3
  F4) → I; double the bass in the 6/4.
- Never write a root-position-like 6/4 on a strong beat except the cadential I6/4 before V.
- Compose the top line as a melody first, then fill inner voices.

## IX Chords on II, III, VI, VII (Acordes sobre II, III, VI, VII) (scan p.60–66, printed p.53–59)

**Concepts**
- Major key: II, III, VI minor; VII diminished. Minor key (harmonic): VI major; II and VII diminished;
  III augmented (C min: Eb–G–B).
- Deceptive cadence (cadencia rota): VI in place of I at an ending aimed at I.
- Phrygian cadence (cadencia frigia, semi-cadencia): V as the conclusion of a phrase, usually after IV6 in
  minor.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Treat major/minor triads on II, III, VI (and inversions) exactly like I, IV, V.
- R2 [PREFER] Use the diminished triad in inversion, especially first inversion (sixth chord).
- R3 [PREFER] In diminished triads (all forms) double the note that is *not* part of the dim 5th / aug 4th.
- R4 [PREFER] Resolve the dim 5th inward / aug 4th outward (the most satisfactory treatment).
- R5 [NEVER] Double the leading tone contained in the augmented triad (III of minor).
- R6 [ALLOWED] When soprano and/or bass constrain the texture strongly (esp. with the augmented chord),
  some voice-leading deficiency (hidden 5ths/8ves) is unavoidable; accept it in favor of melodic line,
  bass structure or root progression. Hidden 5ths/8ves are often the least bad way out.
- R7 [AVOID] Hidden 5ths/8ves in the outer voices (soprano–bass) even then.
- R8 [ALLOWED] Doubling the leading tone (sensible) when: (a) it is not the third of a dominant chord;
  (b) it is the third of a dominant chord that goes to a chord other than tonic (II, III, IV, VI) — then
  the two leading tones must not move in the same direction; (c) doubling gives better voice leading
  (almost always with contrary motion between the two voices holding it).
- R9 [PREFER] Use VI for I in final approaches to make a deceptive cadence; use IV6–V (minor) for a
  Phrygian half cadence.
- R10 [ALLOWED] From Ex. 46 on, strict vocal-range rules are relaxed; still 4 parts on paper. At the
  piano: fuller chords (more doublings, complete ninth chords) or reduced 3-part harmony are fine.

**Examples**
- Ex. 44 Roman-numeral rhythms, e.g. E major: I III | IV II III | I IV6 V6 | III6 VI | IV I6/4 VI | V6/5
  I II6 | I6/4 V7 | I. G major 6/8: I II | III VI | V6/5 V7 V13/7 | VI V6 | IV6 III6/4 | … | II6/5 V13/7 | I.
- Ex. 46 melody (G): I VI III I V6 IV6 I6/4 V | IV VII6/4 III VI6/4 II V6/4 VI6 IV6/4 | V6/5 V7 I III II
  VI V7 I.
- Ex. 46 (Eb): …IV6–III6–II6–III–VI II6–II–V7; shows step-wise root sequences using all degrees.

**Agent applicability**
- Diatonic chord pool per key; in minor treat III+ carefully (don't double the leading tone).
- Diminished VII/II: prefer first inversion, double the third (bass) — e.g. C: vii°6 = D3 bass, D doubled.
- Deceptive cadence: V(7)→VI, with leading tone up to tonic and the third of VI doubled (inferred from
  standard practice; not stated here).
- Phrygian half cadence in minor: iv6 → V (A min: F3 bass → E3).

## X Seventh chords on I, II, III, IV, VI, VII (Acordes de séptima secundarios) (scan p.67–79, printed p.60–72)

**Concepts**
- Secondary seventh chords (acordes de séptima secundarios). Major (C): I7 C–E–G–B, II7 D–F–A–C, III7
  E–G–B–D, IV7 F–A–C–E, VI7 A–C–E–G, VII7 B–D–F–A. Minor (harmonic, A min): I7 A–C–E–G#, II7 B–D–F–A,
  III7 C–E–G#–B, IV7 D–F–A–C, VI7 F–A–C–E, VII7 G#–B–D–F (dim 7th). (Note names computed from the
  printed chord tables.)
- Used in all inversions (6/5, 4/3, 2) and positions.
- Figured-bass line: a horizontal line under 2+ bass notes = the first chord (triad or figured chord)
  is held to the end of the line; the bass meanwhile has non-chord tones or arpeggiates.

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] I7 and III7 in minor (rarely used). VII7 in minor (dim 7th) is very common.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Read many secondary sevenths as triads/V7 plus non-chord tones instead of independent
  chords.
- R3 [PREFER] Prepare the seventh: it appears in the same voice as a chord tone of the preceding chord
  (softens the harshness).
- R4 [PREFER] For VII7 (major and minor) and II7 (minor), in all inversions, resolve the dim 5th / aug 4th.
- R5 [PREFER] Use secondary sevenths complete (4 notes).
- R6 [ALLOWED] Omit a note for better voice leading where full harmony isn't required: (a) chords without
  aug/dim intervals (major: I7, II7, III7, IV7, VI7; minor: IV7, VI7) and VII7 in minor → omit third or
  fifth; (b) the others (major VII7; minor I7, II7, III7) → omit any note that is not part of the
  dim/aug interval and is not the seventh.
- R7 [MUST] When a note is omitted, double the root and put the seventh in the top voice.
- R8 [NEVER] Double the leading tone, even when it is the root (VII7).
- R9 [ALLOWED] Consecutive fifths in progressions that include secondary seventh chords (triad/V-chord ↔
  secondary 7th, or 7th ↔ 7th) when they can't be avoided or avoiding them would force clumsy voice
  leading. *Why:* the seventh chord's stronger effect absorbs the listener's attention.
- R10 [NEVER] Consecutive fifths between soprano and bass, even in R9 contexts.
- R11 [NEVER] Consecutive fifths in progressions made only of simple chords (triads and dominant
  chords).
- R12 [ALLOWED] Non-chord tones in inner voices at suitable moments; don't overload the texture.
- R13 [MUST] Figured-bass accidentals in this book are #, b, natural, bb (not slashed figures) — read
  them literally.

**Exceptions / conditions**
- R9 is an explicit exception to the general parallel-fifth ban; R10 and R11 bound it.
- Ex. 51 (Ab, "Muy movido") marks "(Quintas consecutivas)" in a chain of 2–7–6/5 figures: a demonstration
  of tolerated fifths within a seventh-chord sequence.

**Examples**
- Ex. 48 progressions to play (templates): I7–IV, I7–VI, I6/5–IV, I6/5–VI6, I4/3–IV7, I4/3–II6/5,
  I2–VII6, I2–V11/6/5; II7–V, II7–I6, II6/5–I6, II6/5–VI6, II4/3–V7, II4/3–I6, II2–III7, II2–V7/6/5;
  III7–VI, III7–VI6, III6/5–II6, III6/5–IV7, III4/3–II7, III4/3–VI6, III2–VI7, III2–VI6; IV7–II, IV7–V2,
  IV6/5–V, IV6/5–V7, IV4/3–VII7, IV4/3–V6/5, IV2–II2, IV2–VII6/5; VI7–V7, VI7–IV, VI6/5–V6/5, VI6/5–VII7,
  VI4/3–V6, VI4/3–II?, VI2–V13/7, VI2–II7; VII7–I, VII–I7, VII6/5–VII6, VII6/5–VI6, VII4/3–I6, VII4/3–VI6,
  VII2–V9, VII2–I6/4 (various keys).
- Ex. 50 (D): I I6/5 IV7 V2 I6 II7 V III7 | VI I4/3 VII4/3 III7 I6 II6/5 V4/3 VI V7 I — a sequence-rich
  diatonic-seventh progression.
- Ex. 50 (E): I I2 VI7 VI2 IV II7 III IV I7 IV7 VI VI2 … — chains of sevenths in inversion.

**Agent applicability**
- Seventh-chord sequences (e.g. circle of fifths I7–IV7–VII7–III7–VI7–II7–V7–I) are idiomatic; prepare
  each 7th by common tone and resolve it down a step.
- Relax the parallel-fifth checker inside seventh-chord progressions, except between soprano and bass.
- When thinning a 7th chord to 3 notes: keep root (doubled), 7th on top; drop 5th (or 3rd where allowed).

## XI Simple alteration (Alteración simple) (scan p.80–88, printed p.73–81)

**Concepts**
- Other minor scales give extra in-key chords. Ascending melodic minor (A min): II B–D–F# (minor), IV
  D–F#–A (major), VI F#–A–C (diminished). Descending melodic = natural minor: III C–E–G (major), V E–G–B
  (minor), VII G–B–D (major).
- Seventh chords can be built on both melodic-minor forms too (table of 9 chords in A minor; individual
  spellings [?] beyond the triads above).
- Chromatic alteration (alteración cromática): raising/lowering scale notes by accidentals. Most frequent
  results — major (C): IV F–Ab–C (minor subdominant), VI Ab–C–Eb, II Db–F–Ab; minor (A): II Bb–D–F,
  VI F#–A–C#.
- Neapolitan sixth (sexta napolitana): altered II (C: Db–F–Ab; A min: Bb–D–F) used mainly in first
  inversion (C: F bass).
- Most frequent altered seventh chords (C major): D–F–Ab–C (half-diminished II7) and B–D–F–Ab (diminished
  VII7).
- Picardy third (tercera de Picardía): major tonic ending a minor piece.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Secondary sevenths produced by simple alteration follow the same consecutive-fifth policy as
  unaltered secondary sevenths (Ch. X R9–R11).
- R2 [PREFER] Use altered VI in a deceptive-cadence variant: I – V7 – VI(altered) (C: V7 → Ab–C–Eb).
- R3 [PREFER] Use altered II mainly as Neapolitan sixth.
- R4 [ALLOWED] In Nap. II6 → V, accept (recommended here) (a) a diminished third in one voice — root of
  the Neapolitan to the leading tone (C: Db→B; A min: Bb→G#), and (b) a false relation (cross relation)
  between the lowered and natural degree. Both are normally avoided elsewhere.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Mix modes: minor-derived chords in major (e.g. minor subdominant) and vice versa; even the
  tonic chords may be exchanged when no precise major/minor feeling is wanted — more in the middle of the
  harmonic development than at beginning or end.
- R6 [ALLOWED] End a minor piece/section on a major tonic (Picardy third); the reverse (major piece ending
  minor) is less frequent.
- R7 [PREFER] (notation) From here on the book uses figured bass rather than Roman numerals, because
  altered chords can't be named unambiguously with Roman numerals; Roman numerals are kept for a few cases
  only.
- R8 [MUST] (reading figures) Two successive figures (e.g. 8 7) may describe the motion of one voice over a
  single bass note, not two chords.

**Examples**
- Ex. 53 (C, "Moderadamente rápido"): bass with b, 6, b5, b7/5 figures — chords with Ab and Eb inside C
  major (minor-mode mixture).
- Ex. 53 (Eb, "Movido"): bass Eb–Cb (b5), Eb–C (6), …: lowered VI and minor subdominant colours in major.
- Ex. 54 (E minor, "Moderado"): bass F natural marked "(Nap. II)" → bass with # (V): here the Neapolitan
  (F–A–C) stands on its root F rather than in first inversion, then resolves to V (B–D#–F#).
- Ex. 55 (Ch. XII, preview p.89, outside range): fa#: nap. II6 – I shown with secondary dominant.

**Agent applicability**
- Mode-mixture palette for major keys: iv (F–Ab–C), bVI (Ab–C–Eb), bII6 (Neapolitan, F bass), ii ø7
  (D–F–Ab–C), vii°7 (B–D–F–Ab). For minor: bII6 (Bb–D–F over D), raised VI (F#–A–C#), melodic-minor IV
  major (D–F#–A), natural-minor v and VII.
- Neapolitan cadence: bII6 → (I6/4) → V → I; let one voice go b2 → leading tone (dim 3rd) — don't "fix"
  it; cross relation is acceptable here.
- Deceptive cadence variant: V7 → bVI in major for a darker surprise.
- Ending a minor progression: optionally make the final tonic major (Picardy).
