Method note (preface, scans 2–6, only the usable facts): the course is strictly technical chord connection in
4 parts; rules are deliberately minimal and are **relaxed step by step** in later chapters (a rule stated
early is often loosened later — later sections below link back). No literature examples are given. Style
target is roughly 1600–1900 common practice; it is not a style course.

## I Introducción (scan p.8–10, printed p.1–3)

**Concepts**
- Four voices (cuatro voces): Soprano, Alto (Contralto), Tenor, Bass (Bajo). Written on two staves: S+A on the
  treble staff, T+B on the bass staff.
- Triad / perfect chord (tríada, acorde perfecto): major or minor. Tones named root (fundamental), third
  (tercera), fifth (quinta).
- Chord naming: by root. Upper-case letter = major (C = C major triad), lower-case = minor (a = A minor triad).
  Same convention for keys: "Mi" = E major, "mi" = E minor.
- Close position (posición cerrada): no chord tone could be inserted between S–A or between A–T.
- Open position (posición abierta): a chord tone could be inserted between S–A or A–T.
- Position by soprano (posición de octava / de quinta / de tercera): named by which chord member the soprano
  holds (octave = root on top, fifth, third). Each can be close or open.
- Scale degrees: Roman numerals I–VII denote degrees and the triads built on them.
  Names: I tonic (tónica), V dominant (dominante), IV subdominant (subdominante), VII leading tone (sensible).

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Voice ranges (whole notes = normal range; small notes in the scan = extreme extension, partly
  hard to read):
  - Soprano C4–G5 (MIDI 60–79); extension ~A3/B3 [?] down, A5 (81) up.
  - Alto G3–D5 (55–74); extension ~F3 (53) [?] down, E5 (76) up.
  - Tenor C3–G4 (48–67); extension A4 (69) up.
  - Bass E2–C4 (40–60); extension ~D2 (38) [?] down, D4 (62) up.
- R2 [MUST] In a triad in 4 parts exactly one tone is doubled.
- R3 [PREFER] Double the root; doubling the fifth is ALLOWED. [NEVER] double the third (relaxed later, see Ch.IV R12).
- R4 [NEVER] Cross voices; keep the order S > A > T > B (one exception, Ch.V R10).
- R5 [MUST] Spacing: S–A ≤ octave (12 semitones), A–T ≤ octave; T–B any distance (relaxed for V9/V13, Ch.VI R5).
- R6 [MUST] Diatonic triads use only scale tones; in minor use the **harmonic minor** scale.
- R7 Triad qualities — Major key: I, IV, V major; II, III, VI minor; VII diminished.
  Minor key (harmonic): I, IV minor; V, VI major; II, VII diminished; III augmented.
- R8 The dominant triad is major in both modes; its third is the leading tone.

**Examples**
- The scan shows a C triad in octave, fifth and third positions, each in close (c) and open (a) spacing
  (exact octave placement of each voice not reliably legible).

**Agent applicability**
- Clamp each generated voice to R1 ranges; treat the extension notes as rare peaks only.
- Voicing check per chord: sorted pitches S>A>T>B, S–A ≤ 12, A–T ≤ 12, T–B unconstrained.
- In minor keys raise the 7th degree for V and VII chords (harmonic minor).

## II Tríadas de las armonías principales (scan p.11–18, printed p.4–11)

**Concepts**
- Principal harmonies: I, IV, V (all in root position in this chapter).
- Motion types: direct/similar (movimiento directo, two or more voices same direction), contrary
  (movimiento contrario), oblique (movimiento oblicuo, one voice holds while another moves).
- Hidden octaves/fifths (octavas/quintas ocultas): two voices moving in the same direction into an octave/fifth
  from a smaller interval.
- Bajete: unfigured bass (bajo sin indicación de grados); a chord must be built on every bass note.

**Rules — simplest form of I–V, V–I, I–IV, IV–I (root doubled in both chords)**
- R1 [MUST] Procedure: (a) write the bass move; (b) complete the first chord; (c) hold the common tone in the
  same voice; (d) move the two remaining upper voices by step to the nearest tones of the second chord.

**Rules — more complex form of I–V and I–IV (fifth doubled in first chord, any allowed doubling in second)**
- R2 [MUST] If two tones could be held, hold one and move the other by the shortest path (leap of a major/minor
  third) to the nearest tone of the next chord.
- R3 [MUST] If no tone can be held, move each of the three upper voices by the shortest path (step, or leap
  ≤ perfect fourth, 5 semitones) to the nearest tone.

**Voice-leading prohibitions**
- R4 [NEVER] Parallel (consecutive) octaves or unisons between any two voices.
- R5 [NEVER] Both voices leaping in the same direction into an octave (shown as a forbidden case alongside R4).
- R6 [NEVER] Parallel (consecutive) fifths. (Contrary-motion fifths: see R18.)
- R7 [NEVER] All four voices leaping in similar motion.
- R8 [AVOID] Three voices leaping in the same direction; if it happens, keep the fourth voice still or move it in
  contrary motion. *Why:* the accumulated leaps create excessive forward drive.
- R9 [ALLOWED] Simultaneous leaps of three voices without limit when they are only a change of position of the
  same chord, provided the fourth voice holds or moves contrary.

**Rules — IV–V and V–IV**
- R10 [MUST] Simplest form: root doubled in both chords; move the three upper voices in **contrary motion to the
  bass** by the shortest path. (This fully applies only to the simplest form.)
- R11 [NEVER] Two voices ascending into an octave from a smaller interval (hidden octaves).
- R12 [NEVER] The two outer voices ascending into a fifth from a smaller interval (hidden fifths).
  (Both relaxed later: Ch.V R6, Ch.VI R7–R9.)
- R13 More complex IV–V: fifth doubled in first chord; sometimes only possible by omitting the fifth of the
  second chord and tripling its root. Move each upper voice in any direction by the shortest path (no leaps
  > fourth).
- R14 [NEVER] Melodic leaps of an augmented or diminished interval in any voice (relaxed Ch.IV R11, Ch.VI R10).
- R15 [AVOID] Leaps larger than a fifth (7 semitones) in the three upper voices (relaxed Ch.III R7).
- R16 [ALLOWED] From here on the fifth may be omitted from any major or minor chord (root then tripled or
  third doubled per later rules).
- R17 [MUST] When the same harmony is repeated, change its position (voicing).
- R18 [ALLOWED] Fifths by contrary motion that arise from melodic passages: Hindemith states there is no reason to
  avoid them, though many textbooks forbid them (footnote, Ex.14).

**Examples**
- E major I–V: B E3→B2; T B3 held (tie); A E4→D#4; S G#4→F#4 (root doubled, common tone held, others step).
- Exercises: chord-symbol-and-rhythm drills in many keys (incl. remote ones: C#, G#m, Gb, D#m), basses with
  degree numbers, bajetes, melodies with and without degree numbers. In bajetes, an accidental placed
  under a bass note refers to its **third** (e.g. # under E in A minor = G#, the leading tone of V).

**Agent applicability**
- Default I/IV/V connection: keep common tone, step the rest, root doubled.
- IV↔V (no common tone): upper three voices all move opposite to the bass.
- Run a parallel-5ths/8ves/unisons check on every voice pair between consecutive chords; also flag outer-voice
  similar motion into a P5/P8 (ascending) as hidden.
- Never all four voices leaping the same way; if 3 leap together, hold or counter-move the 4th.

## III Acordes de sexta (scan p.19–24, printed p.12–17)

**Concepts**
- Sixth chord (acorde de sexta): first inversion of a major/minor triad, third in the bass. Figured bass "6";
  a bass note without figure = root-position chord. Symbol in degree analysis: I6, IV6, V6.
- Caesura (cesura): a clearly perceptible phrase break in melody or figured bass.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] In sixth chords double root or fifth; not the third (for now — relaxed Ch.IV R12).
- R2 [MUST] Soprano in octave or fifth position (not third position, since the bass has the third).
- R3 [ALLOWED] Omit the fifth and triple the root.
- R4 [MUST] Connections between consecutive sixth chords or sixth↔root chords follow the same principles as
  root-position chords: (a) hold the common tone; (b) move each voice by the smallest possible interval.
- R5 [ALLOWED] When the harmony does not change, hidden octaves in the three upper voices that only outline the
  chord (broken chord, acorde roto) are fine. [AVOID] the same leaping pattern when the harmony changes.
- R6 [ALLOWED] Bass may leap an octave or a sixth; bass may leap by augmented/diminished interval **to** the
  leading tone.
- R7 [ALLOWED] From Ex.20 on: no longer required to take the shortest path or hold common tones; leaps of an octave
  or sixth also allowed in upper voices.
- R8 [ALLOWED] After a clearly perceptible caesura in melody or figured bass, all four voices may leap in the same
  direction (exception to Ch.II R7).
- R9 [ALLOWED] Bass and tenor in unison on a chord (footnote in Ex.20, on a V).

**Examples**
- C major, OK: I (B C3, T C4, A G4, S E5) → I6 (B E3, T C4, A C5, S G5): upper voices leap within the same harmony.
  Wrong: same upper-voice leaps from I into V6 (bass B2).
- Exercises: degree-and-rhythm drills mixing I, I6, IV, IV6, V, V6 in 2/4, 3/4, 6/8, 2/2; figured basses with 6
  and accidentals; melodies to harmonise.

**Agent applicability**
- For I6/IV6/V6 voicings, double bass-note's chord root or fifth, not the bass note (until 3rd-doubling rule).
- Repeated chord = good place for upper-voice leaps/re-voicing; across chord changes keep motion tighter.
- Allow bass octave/sixth leaps freely; all-voice leaps only after a phrase break.

## IV El acorde de séptima de dominante (scan p.25–31, printed p.18–24)

**Concepts**
- Dominant seventh (acorde de séptima de dominante, V7): dominant triad + the seventh above its root.
  C major / C minor: G–B–D–F.
- Positions (by soprano): third, fifth, seventh. Not octave position (unless fifth omitted, see R6).
- Distinctive interval: the diminished fifth B–F (6 semitones) or, in positions other than seventh position,
  its inversion the augmented fourth F–B (tritone, tritono).
- Figured bass: "7" under a note = root-position seventh chord.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] The dim 5th and aug 4th of V7 require resolution.
- R2 [MUST] Diminished fifth (leading tone below, 7th above) resolves **inward**: B4–F5 → C5–E5 (C minor: C5–Eb5).
- R3 [MUST] Augmented fourth (7th below, leading tone above) resolves **outward**: F4–B4 → E4–C5 (minor: Eb4–C5).
- R4 [MUST] In both cases the leading tone rises to the tonic (and the 7th falls by step).
- R5 [MUST] V7–I procedure: (a) write the bass; (b) complete V7; (c) resolve the tritone; (d) move other voices to
  the next chord by the shortest path.
- R6 [ALLOWED] Omit the fifth of V7; then octave position becomes possible.
- R7 [MUST] Complete V7 (with fifth) → I without fifth. V7 without fifth → complete I.
- R8 V7 (with or without fifth) → IV: the tritone cannot be resolved; hold the common tone to connect as directly as
  possible.
- R9 [ALLOWED] "V 8–7": V with octave on one beat, the octave falling to the 7th on the next beat.
- R10 Many IV–V7 (and V7–IV) connections are only satisfactory using an augmented or diminished melodic interval.
- R11 [ALLOWED] From here on: approach the leading tone (in any voice, not only bass) by an augmented or diminished
  interval. Leaving the leading tone by such an interval is possible but less common.
- R12 [ALLOWED] From here on the third may be doubled in root-position and sixth chords. Particularly useful in
  (a) sixth chords, (b) the resolution of V7.
- R13 [AVOID] Doubling the third when it is the leading tone of a dominant chord. *Why:* risk of parallel octaves.
- R14 [NEVER] Double the seventh of V7.
- R15 [MUST] Unless otherwise indicated, the last chord is the tonic, in root position, never inverted.

**Examples**
- Third doubled in I6 (C): E3–E4–G4–C5; E3–C4–G4–E5; E3–C4–E4–G4.
- V7→I with doubled third (C): B G3, T B3, A F4, S D5 → B C3, T C4, A E4, S E5
  (B3→C4 up, F4→E4 down, D5→E5; result: root doubled, third doubled, fifth omitted).
- Exercises: V7–I with/without fifth, I–V7 in many keys; degree drills adding V7 and V8–7; figured basses with 7.

**Agent applicability**
- When placing V7: track leading tone (→ up a semitone to tonic) and 7th (→ down a step). Tritone: dim5 contracts to a 3rd, aug4 expands to a 6th.
- Complete V7 resolves to incomplete I (tripled root or doubled third); incomplete V7 to complete I.
- Never double the leading tone or the chordal 7th in a dominant.
- End phrases/pieces on root-position tonic.

## V Inversiones del acorde de V7 (scan p.32–40, printed p.25–33)

**Concepts**
- Three inversions, each in three possible soprano positions:
  - V6/5 (figured 6/5): third (leading tone) in the bass; third position impossible. C: B in bass.
  - V4/3 (figured 4/3): fifth in the bass; fifth position impossible. C: D in bass.
  - V2 (figured 2, also 4/2): seventh in the bass; seventh position impossible. C: F in bass.
- Open fifth sonority (No.1): root + fifth, no third, any position/doubling. Bare unison/octave root (No.2): all
  voices on the root.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Omit the fifth in V6/5 and V2; then double the root (G in C).
- R2 [MUST] V6/5, V4/3, V2 → I: same procedure as V7–I (resolve tritone, shortest path).
- R3 Normal resolutions: V6/5 → I (root position); V4/3 → I or I6; V2 → I6 (the bass 7th falls to the 3rd).
- R4 V6/5 (V4/3, V2) → IV: as V7–IV, the tritone has no normal resolution.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Even when the tritone cannot be normally resolved, following the given rules yields acceptable
  connections.
- R6 [ALLOWED] In the outer voices from now on: hidden octaves (similar motion), and octaves by contrary motion.
  Octaves by contrary motion only at the end of an exercise or of a clearly articulated section.
- R7 [ALLOWED] Open-fifth chord (No.1) anywhere, all positions/doublings. *Caution:* hollow sound.
- R8 [ALLOWED] Bare root chord (No.2) only as the final chord.
- R9 [NEVER] Use either No.1 or No.2 immediately after a chord containing the dim 5th/aug 4th (V7, V6/5, V4/3, V2…).
- R10 [ALLOWED] In close-position V7, the bass may lie above the tenor when unavoidable. It looks like V2, but
  the bass line justifies hearing full V7 function. Outside this case avoid voice crossing.
- R11 [NEVER] End an exercise on a seventh chord (or similar combination) or its inversions.
- R12 Figured bass: an accidental placed before a figure alters the interval that figure indicates, counted from
  the bass (e.g. #6, #4/2, 7 with # beneath = raised third). A lone accidental still = the third (Ch.II).

**Examples**
- Allowed outer-voice octaves (C): hidden: S G4→C5 with B rising to C3; contrary: S G4→C5 vs B C4→C3, and
  S C5→G4 vs B C3→G3 [approx. pitches].
- Bass above tenor (Eb major) [approx]: IV (B Ab3, T Ab3, A C4, S Ab4) → V7 with B Bb3 above T Ab3 → I (B Eb3,
  T G3, upper Eb4).
- Exercises: V2/V6/5/V4/3 inside I–IV–V drills (e.g. "I V2 I6 V6/5 I V6 I" in A major 3/4); figured basses with
  6/5, 4/3, 2, 7, accidentals; melodies with/without degrees.

**Agent applicability**
- Bass-line choice (inferred from R3): stepwise basses through V inversions, e.g. I–V4/3–I6 (C3–D3–E3),
  V6/5–I (B2–C3), IV–V2–I6 (F–F–E: the bass 7th descends by step to the third of I).
- V6/5 puts the leading tone in the bass → next bass note must be the tonic.
- Don't follow any dominant-seventh-family chord with a bare 5th or unison sonority.
- Final chord: root-position tonic triad (bare unison only as last chord).

## VI Acordes derivados del V7 — V9, V13/7 (scan p.41–44, printed p.34–37; chapter continues)

**Concepts**
- Dominant ninth (acorde de novena de dominante, V9): V7 + ninth above the root. C major: G–B–D–F–A;
  C minor: G–B–D–F–Ab.
- V9 inversions used: V7/6/5 (third in bass) and V10/4/2 (seventh in bass). Figures: 9; 7/6/5; 10/4/2.
- Dominant seventh with sixth (V13/7): V7 with the **sixth (13th) replacing the fifth**. C: G–B–F–E (minor: Eb).
  Inversions: V11/6/5 (third in bass), V7/4/2 (seventh in bass). Figures: 13/7 (or 7 with 13); 11/6/5; 7/4/2.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] V9 in 4 parts: (a) omit the fifth; (b) ninth in the top voice; (c) the two upper voices must not form a
  second.
- R2 [PREFER] In V13/7 the sixth (13th) is almost always in the top voice.
- R3 [MUST] In connections, treat V9 and V13/7 and their inversions like V7 and its corresponding inversions
  (V10/4/2 and V7/4/2 resolve to I6, like V2).
- R4 [ALLOWED] The characteristic tone (9 of V9; 6/13 of V13/7) may be approached by a diminished or augmented
  interval.
- R5 [ALLOWED] In V7/6/5 and V10/4/2 the two upper voices may be a ninth apart (14 semitones) — exception to the
  S–A ≤ octave rule (Ch.I R5).
- R6 [MUST] Choose chord positions carefully to avoid unnecessary leaps in all voices.
- R7 [ALLOWED] Hidden octaves and fifths (Ch.II R11–R12) between two inner voices, or between soprano and an
  inner voice.
- R8 [PREFER] Even then make them less evident with strong contrary motion in at least one other voice.
- R9 [NEVER] In any voices: octaves reached by two voices moving from a seventh or ninth into an octave (e.g.
  C4/B4 → E4/E5), including similar-motion and comparable cases shown.
- R10 [ALLOWED] From now on, augmented/diminished melodic intervals may approach any tone (not only 9 of V9,
  6 of V13/7 and the leading tone) when it gives more fluent voice leading (especially in minor) or avoids
  worse errors.

**Examples** (C major)
- V9 root position: B G2, T F3, A B3, S A4 (fifth omitted, 9th on top); also G3–B3–F4–A4.
- V7/6/5 (V9 1st inv.): B B2, T G3, A F4, S A4; ninth-apart variant: B2–F3–G3–A4 (S–A = A4/G3).
- V10/4/2 (V9 3rd inv.): F3–G3–B3–A4; ninth-apart variant: F2–B2–G3–A4.
- V13/7: G3–F4–B4–E5 (minor: Eb5) [approx]; sixth on top.
- Exercises: degree drills with V9, V13/7, V10/4/2, V11/6/5, V7/4/2 among I/IV/V chords; partially figured basses
  (9, 13/7, 11/6/5, 7/6/5, 10/4/2, 7/4/2); melodies with some degree indications.

**Agent applicability**
- To colour a dominant: add the 9th (major key: scale degree 6; minor: b6) in the top voice and drop the 5th;
  or replace the 5th with the 13th (degree 3 of the key) in the top voice. Resolve as plain V7.
- Keep S–A ≤ 12 semitones except that V9/V13 inversions may use a 14-semitone gap.
- Hidden 8ves/5ths: tolerate in inner pairs or soprano–inner, never from a 7th/9th into an 8ve.
