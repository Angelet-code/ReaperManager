## Foreword (scan s239R–s240R, printed p.vii–ix)

**Concepts**
- Orchestration (orquestación), in Piston's sense: writing music for orchestra with the principles of
  instrumental combination found in the scores of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven. He presents it as a
  common technique, still used in symphonic music of his own day as well as in the Classical and
  Romantic periods.
- Transcription (transcripción) for orchestra of music written for another medium is a "lesser" but
  legitimate art. It works only if the orchestrator thinks the composer's thoughts. Otherwise the result
  is a superficial display of craft.

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] Treat the orchestra as a single, flexible instrument that presents the music's form and
  content faithfully, not as a set of separate effects. *Why:* this is the book's stated end goal.
- R2 [MUST] Build in this order: know each instrument (capabilities, character, imagined sound), then
  learn the effects of combination (balance of tone, mixed tone colours, clarity of texture), then
  handle the whole orchestra.
- R3 [ALLOWED] The book's method excludes earlier practice based on improvisation, chance instrumental
  balance and a keyboard holding the texture together (basso continuo). `[era: pre-Classical/Baroque
  continuo practice is out of scope]`. For a Baroque idiom, an agent must take the continuo layer
  (harpsichord/organ realising the bass) from other sources. (inferred)
- R4 [PREFER] Draw conclusions about orchestral sound from the cumulative evidence of many
  hearings, held open to revision, not from one or two examples. *Why:* notation cannot fix dynamics,
  rhythm or tone colour exactly, and players, conductors, orchestras (string count, instruments,
  players' skill) and halls all change the result.
- R5 [AVOID] Using a single recording or broadcast as proof of how a scoring sounds. *Why:* in Piston's
  experience, commercial recordings add sounds not in the score, drop written notes, and can shift
  pitch, tempo and timbre through speed errors. `[era: 1950s 78/LP records and radio]`

**Agent applicability**
- An agent "listening by measurement" (render, then measure) faces the same problem as Piston's student:
  one render with one library is a single "performance". Judge a scoring choice across sections,
  patches or renders before turning it into a rule, and expect results to depend on the library (as
  with orchestra size and hall). (inferred)
- When orchestrating a piano or chamber piece (transcription), first establish what the composer means
  (line hierarchy, voicing, pedal resonance) and then choose instruments to serve it. Do not decorate.
