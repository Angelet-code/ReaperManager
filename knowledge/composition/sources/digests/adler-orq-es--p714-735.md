# Adler, El estudio de la orquestación — scans 714–735 (ch.17: transcription, band to orchestra)

Pitch convention: C4 = middle C = MIDI 60. This range is almost entirely full-score examples plus commentary; it
gives transcription principles and colour-substitution decisions, not ranges. Printed p.727 (scan p0728) carries
no visible page number, but the sequence 713–734 is continuous. Scan number = printed number + 1.

## 17 Schoenberg's orchestration of Brahms, Piano Quartet in G minor, Op.25 (scan p.714–716, printed p.713–715)

**Concepts**
- Principal voice (Hauptstimme, voz principal): the melodic line marked with the bracket "H┐" in Schoenberg's
  scores; secondary voice (Nebenstimme) is marked "N". Adler reads the passing of the Hauptstimme between
  sections as a colour device.
- get. / zus. (geteilt / zusammen): German score markings for divisi / unison, seen on Vln 2 and DB.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Before transcribing any work, know the original composer's orchestral and compositional style
  intimately; Adler calls this the most important precondition (Schoenberg with Brahms, Stravinsky with Pergolesi).
- R2 [PREFER] Keep the original harmony unchanged. Schoenberg altered nothing harmonically and aimed at a
  Brahms-like orchestral sound, with three exceptions: heavier trumpet use and xylophone in the finale, and
  clarinet prominence (incl. Eb clarinet) throughout.
- R3 [ALLOWED] Piano octaves can go to a clarinet choir: the opening piano octaves become Eb clarinet + Bb
  clarinet + bass clarinet in octaves, p (Ex. 17-14b m.1–4).
- R4 [PREFER] Close a phrase and prepare a contrasting string entry with a dark wind blend: bassoons + horns +
  two clarinets (the "typical Brahms combination") at m.4 hand over to the strings at m.5.
- R5 [AVOID] Doubling two string instruments of similar colour at the unison for a melody (e.g. Vln 1 + Vc):
  they "cancel each other out". *Fix:* double at the octave instead. That gives a richer sound and compensates
  for the similar colour (Brahms had cello + piano; Schoenberg wrote Vln 1 + Vc in octaves).
- R6 [PREFER] Hold back a colour so its later entry has more effect: the quartet's viola entry is given to the
  horn, so the following violin entry sounds more moving.
- R7 [PREFER] Exchange the principal voice between sections in step with the phrase segmentation. Brahms's
  theme falls into 2 bars + 1 bar + 2 bars and a beat, and each fragment changes section. This adds colour and
  underlines the fragmented presentation.
- R8 [PREFER] When enriching an original's soft passage (m.6–10), add instruments but keep its gentle texture
  and dynamics. Mark a new formal function (bridge/transition at m.11) with a wholly new texture.
- R9 [ALLOWED] Add a folk flavour with a standard orchestral instrument rather than the folk original:
  xylophone in the *Rondo alla zingarese* gives the "gypsy" colour instead of a cimbalom. *Why given:* the
  source notes Kodály brought the cimbalom into Hungarian pieces only a few years later.
- R10 Heavier solo trumpets, Eb clarinet and xylophone make the result sound more 20th-century than its
  19th-century origin. Use these deliberately or avoid them, depending on whether a period sound is wanted.
  `[era: 1937 orchestration of an 1861 work]`

**Examples**
- Ex. 17-14a Brahms, Piano Quartet Op.25/i m.1–10 (original): piano octave theme p espress., then Vc, Vla,
  Vln enter one after another with the piano.
- Ex. 17-14b Schoenberg orchestration (Allegro, quarter = 132): three clarinets in octaves (H) m.1–4; Ob 1,
  2 bassoons + contrabassoon and horns 3–4 answer p; Vln 1 + Vc in octaves take the H at m.5 (Vln 2/Vla
  accompany, DB divisi); horn 1 takes an H fragment at m.7; Vln 1 + Vc in octaves again at m.8.

**Agent applicability**
- Rendering a piano texture for orchestra: map piano octaves to a woodwind choir in octaves (cl family),
  never to two same-colour strings at the unison.
- Melody on Vln 1 + Vc: place them an octave apart (e.g. Vln 1 at D5, Vc at D4), not the same pitch.
- Make the melodic owner track change at phrase-fragment boundaries (each 1–2 bar fragment on a new section).

## 17 Transcribing band or wind-ensemble music for orchestra (Transcripción de banda o conjunto de viento para orquesta) (scan p.716–717, printed p.715–716)

**Concepts**
- Band (banda) vs. orchestra: the band has no fixed size; the orchestra plays exactly what is written
  (except the variable-size string sections). A part for 2 flutes in an orchestra is played by exactly two flutes.
- Band part doubling: in a normal band each of three clarinet parts may be played by up to 15 clarinettists.
  A band composer writes *solo* on the 1st clarinet part to get a single player.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] When transcribing band to orchestra, remember the band clarinet section produces a massive sound
  with no orchestral equivalent. Do not expect 2–3 orchestral clarinets to replace it.
- R2 Neither medium can sound like the other. Arguing which version is "better" is pointless; a band
  transcription is better than not hearing a work at all (Adler's example: Wagner, *Elsa's Procession*).
- R3 [MUST] Woodwinds and brass are treated the same way in both media: same techniques, registers, colours,
  strengths and weaknesses. Keep the instrumental sections balanced whatever the ensemble.
- R4 [NEVER] Lose musical content or override the composer's choices about the quality of each sound.
- R5 [MUST] Handle dynamics, colour assignment and the emotional feeling of the original or section with care.
- R6 [MUST] Preserve which parts are foreground (partes predominantes) and which are background (plano de fondo).
  This is often decisive when assigning a melody, harmony or contrapuntal background to an instrument or group.
- R7 Analysis checklist for comparing a composer's own band and orchestral versions: (1) how much of the band
  score survives, and what this shows about the composer's view of each medium; (2) were solos reassigned, and
  why; (3) what happened to band-only instruments (saxophones, euphonium, cornets…), and what role the strings
  took; (4) were notation changes needed.
- R8 [MUST] When strings are added, decide in detail how colour and foreground/background ideas are redistributed.

**Agent applicability**
- Converting a "band/wind" arrangement to an orchestral template: never map a unison clarinet tutti 1:1. Spread
  the line over clarinets + violins or flutes, or accept a lighter result on purpose.
- Keep a foreground/background tag per line, and check it survives the instrument swap (melody still on top
  in level and register).

## Milhaud, *Suite française*: band and orchestral versions (scan p.717–727, printed p.716–726)

**Rules**
- R1 Both versions were written for school ensembles. Milhaud minimised technical difficulty and used a narrower
  range per instrument. `[era: 1944, American school bands/orchestras]`
- R2 Both versions share a melodic, colourful Gallic flavour (reminiscent of French band music). The band
  version's heavy doubling gives a denser texture than the orchestral one.
- R3 [PREFER] Orchestral version = lighter, chamber-like: each woodwind part goes to one instrument where the
  band doubled heavily (Ex. 17-15).
- R4 [ALLOWED] Put a violin melody in a fairly weak register and double it an octave higher with one flute when
  the violin solo enters (m.29).
- R5 [PREFER] Alternate the doubling instruments (now brass, now woodwinds) for colour variety. The band version
  keeps one colour or similar colours through the whole passage.
- R6 [PREFER] For student or less agile violins, give the high octave of a fast violin melody to flutes so 1st
  violins never pass 3rd position (Ex. 17-16). *Why:* school wind players are often more skilled than string
  players. `[era: school-orchestra writing]` (Third position on the E string tops out around B5–C6 (MIDI 83–84);
  inferred, not given in the source.)
- R7 [PREFER] Reserve the whole brass section for the important entry (m.57 of the 5th movement). The band
  version keeps brass playing throughout.

**Examples**
- Ex. 17-15a Milhaud, *Suite française* ii, m.25–36, band (Andante, 6/8): all clarinets (Eb, Bb 1–3) in unison
  and octaves on the melody, the highest (Eb cl.) line also doubled by Fl 1–2 a2, mf; horns 1–4 a2 + bass
  saxophone play the countermelody in octaves; short off-beat chords for timpani, string bass, all trombones
  (open) and tuba; alto clar. + alto saxes a2 give the lead-in at m.25–26.
- Ex. 17-15b same, orchestra: Ob holds, Vln 2 arco takes the lead-in, Vc divisi sustain; at m.27 Vln 1 (mf)
  has the melody, Vln 2/Vla/Vc/DB the sustained countermelody (the band's horn line), short chords move to 2
  bassoons + 2 horns + timpani p; Vln 1 "Solo" at m.29 doubled 8va by Fl 1; clarinets take the line at m.31
  (Vln 1 Tutti).
- Ex. 17-16a Milhaud, *Suite française* v, m.1–11, band (Animé, quarter = 138): Fl a2 + Eb cl. + three Bb
  clarinets in 16th-note melody f; bassoons, trombones, tuba and pizz. string bass in short chords; horns enter
  m.2.
- Ex. 17-16b same, orchestra: Vln 1 melody with Vln 2 a 3rd/6th below [?], 2 C trumpets + 2 trombones doubling the
  opening with chords; Vla/Vc/DB block chords; woodwinds (Fl, Ob, Cl, Bsn) take over at m.3–4 and the violins
  return; tutti strings with Fl/Ob doubling from m.6.
- Ex. 17-17a v, m.50–57, band: piccolo + oboes on the melody, cornet 1 in 16ths, trombones/baritone/tuba
  harmony; at m.57 cornets + trumpets *soli* ff in accented 16ths over a snare-drum fermata.
- Ex. 17-17b same, orchestra m.51–60: flutes + oboes + Vln 1 melody, clarinets and violas in running 16ths, Vc
  divisi then unison; only horn 1 (mf) among brass until m.57, where 2 C trumpets + 2 trombones enter f in
  accented 16ths with a snare-drum roll (molto rallentando, fermata); m.58 a tempo tutti f, trumpets doubling
  Vln 2's figure, trombones/bassoons/low strings in chords.

**Agent applicability**
- To make a wind-band texture orchestral: thin each woodwind part to one player, move sustained countermelodies
  from horns to strings, and give short off-beat chords to bassoons + horns + timpani.
- Plan brass as a reserve: keep trumpets and trombones silent until the climactic entry, then bring them in f
  as a block.
- For a fast high violin line, let Vln 1 stay in the middle register and add flute(s) an octave above.

## Schoenberg, *Theme and Variations* Op.43a (band) / Op.43b (orchestra) (scan p.727–735, printed p.726–734)

**Concepts**
- Recomposition (recomposición) vs. direct transcription: Op.43b does not just put strings where the band has
  non-orchestral instruments (saxophones, euphonium…). It changes the timbres radically so that the two
  versions sound completely different.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Replace a solid band colour with a single clear solo: the band's oboes + clarinets in unison (f)
  on the opening theme become one trumpet (p) in the orchestra (Ex. 17-18). The dynamic change (f band → p
  orchestra) is even more striking.
- R2 [ALLOWED] Reassign the principal voice: in Var. I the orchestral oboe plays the band trumpet's line and
  becomes the Hauptstimme (m.22–23). In the band score a completely different part (flutes + clarinets) is
  marked as principal.
- R3 [PREFER] Muted upper strings + pizzicato cellos/basses give a much softer colour than band clarinets,
  euphonium, cornets, horns and saxophones (Var. I). Use them for delicacy instead of the band's vigour.
- R4 [PREFER] Thin the accompaniment under a solo in the orchestra: in Var. IV the band's clarinet, muted
  trumpets and euphoniums become solo muted strings plus tutti strings *col legno battuto* (m.106–113). Later,
  very soft solo strings replace the band's more penetrating muted cornets, trumpets and baritones (from m.113).
- R5 [MUST] Remember that a band "solo" section may be several players on one part, so band accompaniments are
  denser. Thin them when one orchestral player carries the solo.
- R6 Colour substitutions Schoenberg made in Var. IV (band → orchestra):
  - clarinet colour at the end of the flute solo (m.122) → oboe + English horn;
  - clarinet + alto saxophone → muted trombones;
  - flugelhorn theme → low flute + high bassoon, with the euphonium/baritone part → very soft clarinets (m.124–125);
  - final flugelhorn colour → violas (much softer);
  - band tubas/basses for the quiet close → muted low strings, which give a calm ending far more easily.
- R7 [ALLOWED] Invent idiomatic orchestral figures with no band equivalent: undulating Vln 2 figures *sul
  ponticello* (m.122–123).
- R8 [PREFER] Keep the trumpet muted and leave the oboe melody undoubled (m.129); the band doubles it. Undoubled =
  more delicate.
- R9 [PREFER] Exchange high and low registers between phrases (m.132–139): phrase 1 on flutes + clarinets with
  Vln 2 an octave below; phrase 2 on violins high (m.136–137) with woodwinds an octave below. More sophisticated
  and less penetrating than the band original.
- R10 [ALLOWED] Some bars can stay exact transcriptions (m.126–127 of Var. IV) inside a recomposed passage.

**Examples**
- Ex. 17-18a Op.43a m.1–6 (Poco allegro, quarter = 84), band: Ob 1–2 a2 + Cl 1–2 theme f (P = Hauptstimme);
  bassoons a2 + bass cl. + baritone sax bass line mf; trombones + horns chords; euphonium/tuba answers p;
  cymbal + bass drum on the downbeat of m.2 and m.4; flutes + saxophones enter m.5.
- Ex. 17-18b Op.43b m.1–6, orchestra: C trumpet 1 alone on the theme p; bass line on bassoons a2 + bass
  clarinet + tuba p; trombones 1–3 chords p; horns p; strings, timpani and percussion tacet.
- Ex. 17-19a Op.43a Var. I m.22–26, band: flutes and clarinets (triplets, p dolce) carry the principal voice,
  trumpets/flugelhorns pp, horns, euphonium pp, tuba, pizz. string bass.
- Ex. 17-19b Op.43b Var. I, orchestra: oboe 1 (pp) as principal; Vln 1 divided into 4 solos con sord. with
  triplet figures p dolce, then "Tutti con sord."; violas with triplets; Vc/DB pizz. then arco con sord. pp; C
  trumpet 1 muted p; English horn and bassoons in fragments; horns p chord.
- Ex. 17-20a Op.43a Var. IV (Tempo di Valzer, 3/4, metronome 60 on a note value that is hard to read, probably the dotted half [?]), band, m.106–~132: flute 1
  solo p over clarinets, muted trumpets and baritone, triangle/tambourine; muted cornets + trumpets p dolce from
  m.113; clarinet solo m.122; flugelhorns open p dolce m.124–125; glockenspiel and xylophone colour; Tutti
  clarinets m.131. The orchestral version (17-20b) lies beyond this range.

**Agent applicability**
- When porting a band piece, keep the melody and harmony but treat each band colour as a choice. A unison
  reed tutti can become one solo trumpet p, which changes the character entirely.
- Delicate accompaniment recipe: Vln 1 as 2–4 solo players con sord. (separate tracks or a solo-strings patch),
  violas con sord., Vc/DB pizz. pp, with one wind solo on top.
- Under a woodwind solo, use solo muted strings or col legno instead of muted brass for accompaniment.
- Register exchange: alternate phrases between high-strings-over-low-woods and high-woods-over-low-strings
  (octave doubling each time).
