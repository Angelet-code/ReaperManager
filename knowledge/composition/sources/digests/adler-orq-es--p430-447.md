## 12 Intro and traditional uses (scan p.430–431, printed p.431–432)

**Concepts**
- Percussion section (sección de percusión): the most versatile and colourful orchestral section; the number
  of available instruments is practically unlimited, and percussionists will even build new instruments.
- Turkish instruments (instrumentos turcos / música militar turca): snare drum (caja clara), triangle
  (triángulo), cymbals (platos), small gongs, plus Mediterranean castanets (castañuelas) and tambourine
  (pandereta) — first used in 17th–18th-c. opera.
- Kettledrums: Purcell first used cavalry drums orchestrally; they became modern timpani.

**Rules**
- R1 [ALLOWED] [era: Classical, c.1770–1825] Use triangle, cymbals, snare and bass drum to evoke a military /
  "Turkish" character (Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven). *Example:* Beethoven Sym. 9 finale, march variation.
- R2 [ALLOWED] [era: mid–late 19th c. onward] Triangle, cymbals, snare and bass drum are full timbral members
  of the standard symphony orchestra, no longer only military colour.
- R3 [ALLOWED] [era: nationalist 19th c.] Folk/"ethnic" colour instruments: castanets, tambourines, cimbalom.
- R4 [ALLOWED] [era: late 19th c. onward] Glockenspiel and xylophone join the enlarged symphony orchestra.
- R5 [ALLOWED] [era: 20th c.] Percussion as a self-sufficient ensemble (Varèse *Ionisation*, Antheil
  *Ballet mécanique*; percussion ensembles popular since the 1920s).
- R6 [ALLOWED] [era: late 20th c.] Mixing Asian/African/Latin-American percussion with Western instruments.

**Examples / repertoire pointers**
- Varèse, *Ionisation*; Antheil, *Ballet mécanique*: percussion ensemble as an orchestra in itself.
- Lou Harrison, Henry Brant, George Crumb: wide use of non-Western percussion.
- Takemitsu, Tan Dun, Bright Sheng, Chen Yi, Xiaogang Ye, Chinary Ung: Asian + Western percussion combined.
- Villa-Lobos, Ginastera, Saitta, Gandini: Latin-American percussion writing.
- Cantata *Schlage doch, gewünschte Stunde* (formerly attrib. Bach; per Herz by Melchior Hoffmann): a bell as
  funeral-knell symbol.

**Agent applicability**
- Period pastiche: Classical "Turkish" colour = bass drum + cymbals + triangle (+ snare), used only in
  march/military passages; Romantic and later = free timbral use.

## Number and distribution of players (scan p.432, printed p.433)

**Rules**
- R7 [MUST] Treat the timpanist as a separate member: the timpanist plays only timpani; never assign other
  percussion to the timpani player, even with time available.
- R8 [MUST] The other percussionists share many instruments; parts are assigned by the principal. Write all
  the music first, then decide the number of players (1–5).
- R9 [MUST] Check per player: (a) enough time to change instruments (e.g. from a mallet instrument to one
  played with sticks); (b) how many instruments one player can play at once (e.g. tam-tam with a beater in
  the right hand + suspended cymbal with a stick in the left).
- R10 [PREFER] Use as few players as possible; for special cases consult an experienced percussionist.

**Agent applicability**
- When generating a percussion layout, keep timpani on its own track/player; for other percussion, never
  give one "player" more than 2 simultaneous instruments, and leave gaps (≥1–2 bars at moderate tempo,
  inferred) for instrument or mallet changes.

**Tags:** #orchestration/percussion #layout

## Notation (scan p.432–433, printed p.433–434)

**Rules**
- R11 [MUST] Explain instrument symbols/abbreviations at the start of the score and parts; many players
  prefer written names to pictograms.
- R12 [MUST] Pitched percussion: 5-line staff in treble clef, or a grand staff (piano-style) for keyboard
  instruments.
- R13 [ALLOWED] Unpitched percussion: either a 5-line staff using lines OR spaces but not both (Ex. 12-1), or a
  1-line staff (Ex. 12-2). [PREFER] the 1-line staff in modern parts.
- R14 [MUST] Use the percussion clef (two vertical bars) for indefinite or approximate pitch; on a 5-line staff
  it is centred on the 3rd line.
- R15 [ALLOWED] *l.v.* (dejar vibrar, let vibrate) and/or a tie leading off a note = let ring for an indefinite
  time.

**Agent applicability**
- In MIDI, *l.v.* = do not cut the note: let the sample's natural decay run (long note-off or sustain pedal on
  sampled vibes/chimes).

**Tags:** #notation/percussion

## Mallets and sticks (mazas y baquetas) (scan p.433–434, printed p.434–435)

**Concepts**
- Three classes: (1) keyboard mallets (baquetas de láminas: felt, plastic, other heads); (2) beaters (mazas)
  for tam-tam and gongs; (3) drumsticks (baquetas de parche) for all drums.
- Standard pictograms exist for: metal, hard (wood/plastic), medium/normal (rubber), soft (lamb's wool / soft
  felt) mallets; wire brushes (escobillas); bass-drum beater; heavy beater (tam-tam); two hard mallets per
  hand; two soft (l.h.) + two hard (r.h.); two soft per hand.

**Rules**
- R16 [MUST] Explain mallet symbols in a notation guide at the start of score and parts.

**Tags:** #orchestration/percussion/mallets

## Categories (scan p.434–435, printed p.435–436)

**Concepts**
- Two main groups: definite pitch (altura determinada) vs indefinite pitch (altura indeterminada); each
  sub-divided by Hornbostel's classes: idiophones (idiófonos), membranophones (membranófonos), chordophones
  (cordófonos), aerophones (aerófonos).
- The border between definite and indefinite pitch is blurred: inharmonic partials and noise make the ear
  tolerate pitch deviations in pitched percussion.

**Rules**
- R17 [ALLOWED] [era: Classical–early Romantic, hand-tuned timpani] Timpani notes "outside the harmony"
  (Beethoven, Verdi) when there was no time to retune; a timpani note a 2nd away from the harmony is
  absorbed. Strauss (in his revision of Berlioz's *Treatise*) remarks on it; Adler does not advocate it today
  given precise modern tuning.
- R18 [PREFER] Treat indefinite-pitch percussion (high/medium/low) as blending with surrounding pitched
  instruments: they pick up consonant fundamentals of the chord; they are not mere "noise".
- R19 [MUST] Mallet idiophones come in different sizes/ranges: name the size you need.
- R20 [ALLOWED] [era: late 20th c.] Electronic amplification/distortion/synthesis of percussion (Larry Austin
  *Life Pulse Prelude*, Bolcom *Session IV*, Daugherty *Metropolis Symphony*, Davidovsky *Synchronisms No. 6*,
  Erb *Klangfarbenfunk* with electronic rock instruments).

**Agent applicability**
- Choose unpitched percussion register (low tom vs high wood block) to fit the chord's register/colour.

**Tags:** #orchestration/percussion #acoustics

## Pitched idiophones — keyboard (mallet) instruments (instrumentos de láminas) (scan p.436–441, printed p.437–442)

### Xylophone (Xil.; It. xilofono, Fr. xylophone, Ger. Xylophon)

**Concepts**
- Wooden bars in piano layout; now usually with resonators. Very short, dry, crisp, penetrating sound;
  notes do not sustain.

**Ranges** (Ex. 12-3; **written** treble clef, **sounds 1 octave higher**; converted)
- Model 1: written F3–C7 → sounding F4–C8 (MIDI 65–108).
- Model 2 (most common, "standard"): written C4–C7 → sounding C5–C8 (MIDI 72–108).
- Model 3: written C3–C7 → sounding C4–C8 (MIDI 60–108).
- If models 1/3 are unavailable, the player transposes missing notes by an octave.

**Rules**
- R21 [MUST] Sustain = roll (redoble) with two mallets; there is no real sustain.
- R22 [PREFER] Single notes and fast passages; doubling other instruments it adds a bright, dry edge like a
  hard *pizzicato*. Trills, arpeggios and glissandi are very effective.
- R23 [ALLOWED] Write at sounding pitch only if stated at the head of the score.
- R24 [PREFER] Normally 2 mallets (one per hand). Loud: ebonite, hard rubber, plastic, occasionally hard wood
  (hard rubber is less effective in the top register). Soft: yarn-headed mallets.
- R25 [PREFER] Specify mallets as "hard / medium / soft"; leave exact type and material to the player.
- R26 [MUST] Leave time for mallet changes (Ex. 12-4 footnote: hard mallets first time, soft on repeat →
  needs time to change).
- Brightest and most penetrating in the top register.

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-4 (Adler): quick 3/4 line with leaps, a roll on a held note, 8va top; hard vs soft mallet contrast.
- Pointer — passages for study: Gershwin *Porgy and Bess* opening; Holst *Planets* "Uranus"; Mahler Sym. 6/i;
  Saint-Saëns *Carnival* "Fossils" and *Danse macabre*; Stravinsky *Petrushka* Tableau I, Russian Dance
  (xylophone + piano).

### Marimba (Mar.; Ger. Marimbaphon)

**Concepts**
- Descendant of the xylophone; rosewood bars, thinner/longer/wider; resonators always. Same technique, sweeter
  and deeper sound. Written for extensively only since 1950; now gaining an orchestral place.
- **Sounds as written**; 1 or 2 staves, treble and bass clef.

**Ranges** (Ex. 12-5)
- Text: same four-octave span as the xylophone, an octave lower → C3–C7 (MIDI 48–96) (inferred from text);
  some marimbas go down to A2 (45) or even F2 (41). The figure shows A2–C7 (45–96).
- Bass marimba (marimba bajo, rare): C2 (36) – D5 [?] (74).

**Rules**
- R27 [PREFER] Exploit the low register: unique and very beautiful. Upper register sounds xylophone-like.
- R28 [PREFER] Mallets: yarn or soft rubber.
- R29 [ALLOWED] Most players use 2 mallets per hand (a few use 3) → wide rolled 4-note chords are easy and
  characteristic.

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-6 (Adler): f opening line, then rolled (tremolo) 2- to 4-note chords from pp to ff — typical
  4-mallet writing.

### Vibraphone (Vib.; It. vibrafono, Fr. vibraphone, Ger. Vibraphon)

**Concepts**
- American invention; metal bars, motor-driven rotating discs (hélices) on top of the resonator tubes give
  vibrato/tremolo — the only mallet instrument with vibrato. **Sounds as written.**
- Motor off: pure metallic sound like a struck tuning fork, limited duration. Motor on: note vibrates and lives
  much longer; motor speed adjustable slow ↔ fast.
- Damper pedal (pedal): pressed = notes ring; released = notes damped.

**Ranges** (Ex. 12-7, as written = sounding)
- Size 1: F3–F6 (MIDI 53–89).
- Size 2: C4–F6 (MIDI 60–89).
- Size 3 (rare — AVOID writing for it): F3–C7 [?] (53–96; top shown under 8va).

**Rules**
- R30 [MUST] State motor instructions (off / on / speed) in score and part.
- R31 [MUST] Indicate pedal intent: write *l.v.* on a note/chord and the ring duration.
- R32 [ALLOWED] Chords of 4 or even 6 notes; trills, glissandi and fast passages as on xylophone/marimba.
- R33 [PREFER] Characteristic sound = sustained, un-rolled notes and chords (no roll needed).
- R34 [PREFER] Mallets: cord, hard, medium, rubber; yarn for soft passages; metal brushes for glissando
  effects. Plastic and wood rarely/never.

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-8 (Adler): Scherzando line, motor on (medium) first time, off second; *l.v.* chords, rolled 4-note
  chord.
- Pointer: Zwilich Sym. 1/ii.

### Extended mallet techniques (vibraphone, xylophone, marimba)

- R35 [ALLOWED] Dead stroke (golpe sin resonancia): strike and leave the mallet on the bar → dull, damped
  staccato; useful when the instrument is soloist or with light accompaniment. Notated with x-headed notes
  (Ex. 12-9: marimba, soft mallets, very slow, 2-note chords per hand).
- R36 [ALLOWED] Bowed vibraphone (arco de chelo): cello/bass bow on the bar end → very mysterious sound; most
  effective with motor on and damper pedal down (Ex. 12-10: slow sustained single notes).

**Agent applicability**
- Sampled vibes: emulate motor with a vibrato/tremolo LFO setting or a "motor on" patch; dead stroke = very
  short note with damped sample.

### Glockenspiel (Glsp.; It. campanelli, Fr. jeu de timbres, Ger. Glockenspiel)

**Concepts**
- Two rows of hard steel bars in keyboard layout, portable case. Oldest mallet instrument; many 19th-c.
  passages. Only one size.

**Range** (Ex. 12-11): **written** G3 [?]–C6, **sounds 2 octaves higher** → G5–C8 (MIDI 79–108).
- Some composers (Schoenberg, *Five Pieces for Orchestra*) write at sounding pitch.

**Rules**
- R37 [PREFER] Normally 2 mallets; some players use 2 per hand.
- R38 [PREFER] The glockenspiel is the only instrument on which metal mallets should be used: loudest, most
  sonorous result. Wood, plastic or semi-hard rubber: more resonant, less dry; harder yarn: quite soft playing.
- R39 [ALLOWED] Cuts through an orchestral *tutti*, especially in its top register.
- R40 [ALLOWED] Sustains longer than xylophone or marimba; the player can lengthen decay and add slight vibrato
  by waving the hand near the bars.

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-12 (Adler): Moderato 3/4 figure with glissando; first time p, second f.
- Pointer: Wagner *Meistersinger* Act III sc. 5 Waltz (glockenspiel + flute + oboe).

### Chimes / tubular bells (campanófono, campanas tubulares; Chm.; It. campane, Fr. jeu de cloches, Ger. Glocken)

**Concepts**
- Chromed brass tubes hung in a frame, chromatic. Sets of 18; most US orchestras have 20. **Sound as written**
  (per Adler).
- Church-bell sound with the same "out-of-tune" quality. Damper pedal operated with the right foot.

**Range** (Ex. 12-13): C4–F5 (MIDI 60–77) for 18 tubes; F#5–G5 (78–79) added on 20-tube sets.

**Rules**
- R41 [ALLOWED] Effective both soft and loud.
- R42 [PREFER] Soft: yarn mallets or leather mallets covered with cloth. Loud: uncovered leather (now hammer-
  like).
- R43 [ALLOWED] Fast passages + damper pedal = many church bells ringing together.
- R44 [ALLOWED] Glissando possible — AVOID overusing it.
- R45 [PREFER] Max 2 simultaneous notes per player; for 4-note chords some composers use 2 players.

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-14 (Adler): slow (Lentamente) half/quarter-note melody in G major with Ped. markings.
- Pointers: Holst *Planets* "Saturn" ending; Mussorgsky–Ravel *Pictures*, "Great Gate of Kiev" ending;
  Puccini *Tosca* Acts I–II; Wagner *Parsifal* final scene.

**Agent applicability**
- Tubular bells patch: keep to 60–79, ≤2 notes at a time; use for bell-tolling climaxes and endings.

### Crotales (Crot.; It. crotali, Ger. Zimbeln, Fr. cymbales antiques; "antique cymbals")

**Concepts**
- Small tuned discs, 7.6–12.7 cm diameter; mounted keyboard-style on a board or hung on leather straps. Thicker
  than hand cymbals and tuned chromatically. Not to be confused with hand cymbals (indefinite pitch).
- Struck with metal mallet ≈ glockenspiel but more diffuse/less penetrating when two discs are clashed.

**Ranges** (Ex. 12-15; **written**, **sound 2 octaves higher**)
- Common set: written C5–C6 → sounding C7–C8 (MIDI 96–108).
- Lower set (rare): written C4–B4 → sounding C6–B6 (MIDI 84–95).

**Rules**
- R46 [PREFER] Metal mallet for best result; wood and plastic possible but less resonant; plastic and
  semi-hard rubber for soft effects.
- R47 [ALLOWED] Player may wave a hand over the disc to sustain, or gently shake hand-held discs after striking.
- R48 [ALLOWED] Bowed crotales (cello/bass bow): disturbing but penetrating sustained sound (Ex. 12-17).

**Examples**
- Ex. 12-16 (Adler): slow chromatic line with fermata; Ex. 12-17: bowed long notes.

### Steel drums (steel drum, tambores de acero)

**Concepts**
- Not a drum: the top of an oil barrel, heated, made concave, sectioned and hammer-tuned, each section a note.
  Sound like a metallic, sonorous marimba; played with soft mallets. Caribbean origin; played in steel bands
  (one melody, others harmony).

**Rules**
- R49 [PREFER] Use mainly to evoke a Caribbean popular atmosphere (few composers use it otherwise).
- Ex. 12-18 (Adler): Alegremente, 2- and 3-note chords in syncopated calypso-like rhythm with meter changes
  3/8–4/8–6/8.

## Shaken or rubbed instruments (scan p.442–444, printed p.443–445)

**Concepts**
- Musical saw, flexatone and glass harmonica have an "electronic" sound; used since WWII by Crumb, Schwantner,
  Mayuzumi, Kagel, Haubenstock-Ramati.

### Musical saw (serrucho; It. sega cantante, Fr. lame musicale, Ger. singende Säge)
- Precision-tuned toothless steel blade with wooden handle, held between the knees; left hand bends the tip
  while a violin bow rubs the edge. No fingering: pitch by bending.
- Range approx. (Ex. 12-19, sounds as written): C5–F6 [?] (MIDI 72–89).
- R50 [ALLOWED] Very pronounced vibrato on every note; glissando over the whole range.
- R51 [PREFER] Best for singing, sustained lines/melodies; resembles bowed vibraphone with motor on and maximum
  vibrato, less precise in pitch. Ex. 12-20: slow 4/4 legato melody with slides.

### Flexatone (flexatón; It. flessatono, Fr./Eng. flexatone, Ger. Flexaton)
- Thin triangular blade in a metal frame with handle; thumb bends the blade to set pitch; two spring strips
  with soft balls strike the blade when shaken.
- Range approx. (Ex. 12-21): C5–F6 [?] (MIDI 72–89).
- R52 [PREFER] Do not demand exact pitches; let the instrument "approach" them. If pitches are required, the
  player tries to match them as closely as possible.
- R53 [ALLOWED] High sound with much tremolo; short isolated sounds are also possible. Ex. 12-22: 3/4 line of
  short notes, chromatic figures, *l.v.* ending.

### Musical glasses and glass harmonica (copas y armónicas de cristal)
- Ordinary wine glasses of various sizes; can be struck, but usually rubbed with wet fingers for long, singing,
  very pure notes. Old usage (back to Gluck). Glass harmonica (bowls) interested Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven.
- R54 [ALLOWED] Modern composers may ask only for low/medium/high glasses instead of exact pitches; e.g.
  Haubenstock-Ramati, *Symphonies de timbres*: four glasses soprano/alto/tenor/bass. Porcelain bowls, glass
  bottles and jars also used.
- Ex. 12-23 (Adler): 6 glasses, long held notes forming a slow sustained chord/line in treble clef.

## Membranophones — timpani (scan p.444–447, printed p.445–448)

**Concepts**
- Membranophones: a stretched skin over a tube or shell resonator; resonator open at one end (bongos,
  tambourine) or closed (timpani). Snare, tenor and bass drums have two heads (one struck, the other vibrates
  sympathetically). Normally struck with a stick or by hand.
- Timpani (Timp.; It. timpani, Fr. timbales, Ger. Pauken): oldest regular members of the orchestral percussion.

**Ranges**
- R55 [era: before 20th c.] Total timpani range F2–F3 (MIDI 41–53), G3 (55) in parentheses.
- Modern standard set of 4 drums + optional piccolo (Ex. 12-24; sizes in cm with inch labels):
  - 81 cm / 32": D2–A2 (MIDI 38–45), C2 (36) possible (in parentheses).
  - 71 cm / 28": F2–C3 [?] (MIDI 41–48).
  - 63 cm / 25": Bb2–F3 (MIDI 46–53).
  - 58 cm / 23": D3–A3 (MIDI 50–57).
  - 53 cm / 21" piccolo timpano: F3–B3 [?] (MIDI 53–59), C4 (60) in parentheses. If unavailable, substitute
    a tuned tom-tom or a roto-tom.
- R56 [MUST] Assume 4 drums (81, 71, 63, 58 cm) in any orchestra; the piccolo timpano is optional.

**Tuning**
- R57 [era: until early 20th c., hand screws] Tuning by screws around the rim was slow → only fixed notes were
  given to timpani.
- R58 [ALLOWED] [era: modern pedal timpani] Pedal on each drum changes pitch quickly and easily; glissandi
  easy. Players still check tuning and may adjust by hand.
- R59 [MUST] Notate retunings, especially fast ones: write the change above the staff during a rest, e.g.
  "Bb – B♮" (Ex. 12-29).

**Historical roles**
- R60 [era: Classical] Two drums only, usually 71 cm and 63 cm, tuned to tonic and dominant of the bass;
  reinforce *tutti* forte passages, climaxes and cadences; paired with trumpets (standard in Haydn/Mozart).
- R61 [ALLOWED] [era: Classical] Occasionally timpani in quiet passages for a special atmosphere (Haydn,
  "Drumroll" Symphony).
- R62 [era: Beethoven] Timpani become soloistic: Beethoven tunes the two drums in octave F (F2–F3) in his last
  two symphonies and writes a timpani solo in the scherzo of the Ninth.
- R63 [era: Berlioz onward] Two or more sets of timpani and two or more timpanists; the number of drums grows
  to 3, then 4 or more today.

**Technique and colour**
- R64 [ALLOWED] Single notes (slow or fast), rolls (redobles), pedal glissandi; dynamic range is enormous.
- R65 [PREFER] Timpani mallets come in hard, medium and soft. Special effects: wood sticks, felt, the timpani
  stick handles, etc.
- R66 [ALLOWED] Beating spot changes timbre: normal ≈ 15 cm from the rim; closer to the rim → beautiful
  *pianissimo*; centre → very dense, dull, darker sound.
- R67 [ALLOWED] Muffle with a cloth over all or part of the head.
- R68 [ALLOWED] [era: 20th c.] Place cymbals, tambourines, maracas, etc. on the head to buzz sympathetically;
  play the shell (caldera) or rim with wooden sticks as unpitched percussion.

**Notation**
- R69 [MUST] Mark dynamics and the exact duration of each note, including where a trill/roll ends and how long a
  note rings.
- R70 [ALLOWED] Roll notation: *tr* with wavy line, or three-slash tremolo on the note (Ex. 12-25). Long roll
  across bars: tied notes with tremolo slashes, or one *tr* with a continuous wavy line; end the roll by tying
  into a short release note (eighth) (Ex. 12-26).
- R71 [MUST] For a tremolo between two drums, state whether it is measured (medido: written as exact 16ths) or
  unmeasured (no medido: 3 beams) (Ex. 12-27).
- R72 [ALLOWED] Two or more notes at once (double stops) — Ex. 12-28: four timpani, waltz "oom-pah-pah" with a
  low note then 2-note chords on beats 2–3, drums tuned "from F to E".

**Repertoire examples**
- Ex. 12-30 Beethoven, Sym. 9/ii, bb.261–273: octave-F timpani solo in the scherzo rhythm (dotted quarter +
  eighths), f → più f → ff, then triplet repeated notes.
- Ex. 12-31 Berlioz, *Symphonie fantastique*/iv, bb.83–89: two timpanists ("Soli"), p entries then sextuplet
  figures with crescendo to ff, chordal/divided parts — multiple timpanists as one instrument.
- Ex. 12-32 Bloch, *Schelomo*, bb.170–171: timpani solo, mf marcato, accented triplet/sextuplet rhythm on
  sharp-side notes (F#/G# area [?]).
- Pointer — passages for study: Bartók *Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta* ii & iv; Bernstein
  *Jeremiah* ii; Elgar *Enigma* "Troyte"; Hanson Sym. 2/i; Harris Sym. 3; Holst *Planets* "Saturn" (two sets of
  timpani); Mahler Sym. 1/iii opening; Nielsen Sym. 4/iv; Shostakovich Sym. 1/iv; Sibelius Sym. 1/iii; Wagner
  *Siegfried* Funeral Music.

**Agent applicability**
- Keep timpani MIDI within 36–60; assign each pitch to a plausible drum (D2–A2 / F2–C3 / Bb2–F3 / D3–A3 /
  F3–C4). In Classical pastiche use only 2 pitches (tonic + dominant, e.g. D2+A2 or F2+C3), forte tutti and
  cadences, and do not change pitch within a movement unless a rest allows hand retuning.
- Modern writing: any note in 38–57 (four drums), but limit fast changes; leave rests before retunings.
- Roll = a sustained timpani roll articulation (or rapid repeated notes); end each roll with a short
  accented release note when the score implies one.

## Roto-toms (scan p.447, printed p.448) — first part

**Concepts**
- Roto-toms (R. Tom; It. roto-tom-tom, Fr. roto-tom, Ger. Tom-Tom-Spiel): perfected by Remo (US); tuned to
  specific pitches by rotating the drum's frame; slow melodies possible. Now made with timpani-like resonators;
  blend well with all instruments, especially timpani, with cleaner, more articulate notes.

**Rules**
- R73 [ALLOWED] Use the same mallets and techniques as timpani; roto-toms far exceed the timpani's range.
