Note on octave names: Adler's text uses C4 = middle C (e.g. clarinet "RE5" sits on the 4th treble line = D5 = MIDI 74), matching vault conventions. Pitches in chord diagrams below are read from the scan and approximate where marked `[?]`.

## 8 Woodwinds sustaining harmony — end of section (scan p.257, printed p.252)

**Examples**
- End of Ex. 8-17 [?] (work named on the previous, undigested page), m.96ff: A clarinets and bassoons hold sustained chords (tenidos), horns 1–2 enter p for the cadence, 1st violins carry the ornamented melody (trills, turns), 2nd violins/violas/cellos+basses give off-beat eighth-note chords; *poco rit.* in all parts at the cadence. Shows the standard Classical layout: strings = melody + rhythmic accompaniment, winds = sustained harmonic glue.

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] When orchestrating in 18th–19th-c. style, give sustained harmonies, including pedals, to the woodwind section whenever possible (study Mozart and (J.?) Strauss overtures for the varied forms of these held-chord designs). `[era: Classical–Romantic, 18th–19th c.]`
- R2 [ALLOWED] In homophonic and contrapuntal passages the woodwinds may either carry the melody or build the harmony, depending on context.

**Agent applicability**
- In a Classical/Romantic pastiche, put long chord tones (whole/half notes, pedals) on Ob/Cl/Bsn/Hn while strings play melody and repeated-note accompaniment.

## 8 Homophonic woodwind writing (Escritura homófona para la madera) (scan p.257–259, printed p.252–254)

**Concepts**
- Homophonic woodwind writing (escritura homófona): the section (with or without horns) playing chords in rhythmic unison, from 4-bar fragments to whole passages.
- Four ways to lay out chords for woodwinds in pairs (acordes para maderas a dos), Ex. 8-18:
  1. Juxtaposed / stacked (yuxtapuestos o superpuestos): each pair occupies its own adjacent block, e.g. Fl pair on top, Ob pair below, Cl pair below, Bsn pair at the bottom.
  2. Interlocked (intercalados): the notes of two pairs alternate, e.g. Fl–Cl–Fl–Cl from the top.
  3. Enclosed (incluidos o encerrados): one pair sits inside the outer notes of another, e.g. Ob outer notes, Cl inner notes.
  4. Partial overlap (superpuestos parcialmente): two pairs share a note in unison (unison doubling of one chord tone).

**Rules**
- R3 [MUST] With stacked pairs (the most frequent layout), check that the most prominent melody note falls in a good register of the instrument that has it. *Why:* the top note is heard as the tune.
- R4 [PREFER] Interlock pairs to blend woodwind timbres (more imaginative than stacking) — but check that one instrument does not dominate a weaker partner in its register.
- R5 [AVOID] Enclosure that upsets timbral balance; it has the same risk as interlocking.
- R6 [AVOID] Partial overlap (unison doubling between pairs) in exposed woodwind chords: it blurs the colors of both pairs and over-weights a note that needs no emphasis. *Exception:* still normal in full-orchestra tutti (R7). `[era: used more often before the 19th c. than today]`
- R7 [ALLOWED] Unison doubling of chord tones across pairs in orchestral tutti.
- R8 [PREFER] For groups of 3 or 4 of each instrument, apply the same four layouts; give each player notes that suit its register and technique so the chord stays balanced and does not impede the melody's flow.
- R9 [AVOID] Chords where every note has a different woodwind timbre (one Fl, one Ob, one Cl, one Bsn). *Why:* hard to balance, often out of tune.
- R10 [ALLOWED] One-timbre-per-note chords work better in a small orchestra with single winds, especially with wide spacing so each instrument sits in its best register (exception to R9).
- R11 [PREFER] In such chords put the widest interval between the bass and the next voice up; keep the upper woodwinds (top chord notes) in close position.

**Examples**
- Ex. 8-19, interlocked pairs (chords around G4–C6 [?]): Cl/Ob interlocked = good; Ob/Cl = good; Cl over Fl = weaker; Cl/Fl lower down = weak; Fl/Ob interlocked low = bad, especially at *ppp* (low flute is swamped by oboe, inferred); Fl/Cl = better for *p*–*pp*.
- Ex. 8-20, enclosed pairs: Fl enclosing Ob = acceptable; Fl enclosing Cl = good; best balance = Fl on top, Ob pair in the middle, Cl at the bottom (the oboe enclosed between two different colors). *Why:* the clarinet's D5 (MIDI 74) is stronger than the 2nd flute's D5, and the mix adds color.
- Ex. 8-21, one woodwind per note: close cluster Fl/Ob/Cl/Bsn in the middle of the treble staff = difficult, not good; good = Fl, Ob, Cl stacked closely high with the Bsn far below.

**Agent applicability**
- For a 2-of-each woodwind chord, default to: Fl1/Fl2 top, then either stacked Ob pair or Ob interlocked with Cl; never put the low flute against oboe at *pp* or softer.
- If D5–G5 is the lowest chord tone under a flute pair, give it to clarinet rather than flute 2.
- Check: gap bass→tenor ≥ the gaps between the upper voices; upper three voices within an octave.

## 8 Spacing (Disposición), bright/dark and first inversion (scan p.259–260, printed p.254–255)

**Rules**
- R12 [ALLOWED] Chord spacing is a personal trait of composers; study the models below and pick one per style.
- R13 [PREFER] To get a bright chord (matiz claro), put flutes (and piccolo at *ff*) in their high register on top, oboes next, clarinets spread below, bass clarinet and bassoons (+ contrabassoon at *ff*) in the bass: the chord spans the whole range.
- R14 [PREFER] To get a dark chord (matiz oscuro), keep the top voice low: oboes interlocked with clarinets around the top of the treble staff's lower half, bass clarinet in octaves below, bassoons low (*f*); or, at *p*, low-register flutes on top, clarinets and bass clarinet in the middle, bassoons enclosing the bass clarinet.
- R15 [PREFER] In first-inversion chords, when the bass (the chord's third) is doubled, double it in a lower voice of the chord rather than in an upper (treble) voice. *Why:* keeps the open sound of the first inversion.

**Examples**
- Ex. 8-22, Beethoven (typical spacing): bassoons in octave Bb2/Bb3 [?], a wide gap, then Fl/Ob/Cl in close position with Fl and Cl enclosing the oboes.
- Ex. 8-23, layouts found in many 19th-c. works: (a) Fl pair stacked over Ob pair, bassoon and contrabassoon in octaves far below; (b) Fl+Cl enclosing the oboes, bassoons in unison; (c) Fl interlocked with Ob, Cl below, bassoons in octaves. All keep a large bass–tenor gap. `[era: 19th c.]`
- Ex. 8-24, Stravinsky: (a) *secco sf*: Fl/Ob interlocked high and close, bassoons and contrabassoon low, nothing in between; (b) Fl pair close at the top, oboe, English horn in the middle, contrabassoon at 8vb: extreme spacing with registral holes as a color. `[era: 20th c.]`
- Ex. 8-25: bright chords at *p* and *ff* vs dark chords at *f* and *p* (see R13–R14).
- Ex. 8-26, first inversion: "bad" = third doubled high in Fl/Ob/Cl over Bcl/Bsn; "good" = Fl, English horn, clarinets spread an octave+, bass clarinet and bassoon, with the doubling kept low.

**Agent applicability**
- Bright vs dark is a register choice, not a dynamic: to darken, lower the top note and remove flute/piccolo from the top; to brighten, add flute/piccolo above an existing voicing.
- For a 6 chord (first inversion) in woodwinds, avoid putting the bass pitch class in the top voice.

## 8 Representative homophonic passages (Pasajes homófonos representativos) (scan p.260–265, printed p.255–260)

**Examples**
- Berlioz, *La damnation de Faust*, "Menuet des follets", m.125–140 (Ex. 8-27; Presto e leggiero, half = 144): after a *ff* tutti chord, two piccolos and a flute, doubled by oboes (in thirds below), play continuous fast figures (*mf*) over a rhythmic-harmonic background of 2 clarinets, bass clarinet and strings *pizz.* *p* on the beats; bassoons a 4 and clarinets join *ff* at m.139. Shows high woodwinds as melody over a pizzicato + low-clarinet accompaniment.
- Schumann, Symphony No. 2, iv, m.272–291 (Ex. 8-28; Allegro molto): after general pauses, a *p dolce* woodwind chorale (Fl, Ob, Cl, Bsn) in which a solo viola doubles the oboe line at the octave and a solo cello doubles the bassoon line; this smooths the transition into the full string section (tutti, *p dolce*) at m.288. The oboe+bassoon color is kept for several bars after the octave G at m.291.
- W. Schuman, *New England Triptych*, iii "Chester", m.1–17 (Ex. 8-29; Religioso, quarter = c.84, *mf legato, dolce*): hymn set for Fl 1–2, Ob 1, English horn, Cl 1–2, Bsn 1–2 imitating an organ; all parts breathe together at phrase ends (breath marks). The flute neutralises part of the oboe's nasal tone, making the blend more organ-like. At m.13 the mass thins and the bass clarinet enters (solo), a welcome change. No cello or bass is used, so the string entry at m.18 sounds new.

**Rules**
- R16 [PREFER] To move from a woodwind passage to a string passage smoothly, let solo strings double woodwind lines at the octave first (e.g. solo viola with oboe, solo cello with bassoon), then bring in the full strings.
- R17 [PREFER] For an organ-like woodwind hymn, double the oboe with flute (the flute softens the nasal edge), use legato *mf dolce*, and end phrases with a common breath.
- R18 [PREFER] Save a family's timbre for the section where it should sound fresh: do not use cellos/basses in a woodwind chorale if a string section follows.

**Agent applicability**
- Chorale in woods: write 4–7 parts in rhythmic unison, put a short gap (≈ 1/8 beat) at every phrase end in all tracks at once.
- Woods→strings transition: 2–4 bars of solo-string octave doublings before the tutti strings enter.

## 8 Contrapuntal woodwind writing (Escritura contrapuntística para la madera) (scan p.266–275, printed p.261–270)

**Concepts**
- Few fugal/imitative passages written for woodwinds alone exist in Baroque and Classical works, since woodwinds were not predominant then; individual woodwinds imitating strings or each other are common. Later large-orchestra works often contain contrapuntal passages for the woodwinds alone. `[era: Baroque/Classical vs. later practice]`

**Rules**
- R19 [PREFER] Use woodwind counterpoint, especially with strong rhythms: each woodwind has such a distinct color that independent lines stay clear.
- R20 [PREFER] Write each contrapuntal gesture in the best register of the instrument that plays it; this keeps a many-part texture clear (Stravinsky).
- R21 [PREFER] Share a repeated ornament figure (e.g. grace notes) between two players of the same instrument, alternating note by note. *Why:* no player tires from repeating the figure, so the ornaments sound fresher, and each player articulates slightly differently, which individualises them (Stravinsky's technique, also in *Petrushka*).
- R22 [ALLOWED] Unison pairs (a 2) for each fugal entry; two similar instruments in unison give a fuller line (Britten).

**Examples**
- Mozart, Symphony No. 38 "Prague", K. 504, iii, m.120–138 (Ex. 8-30; Presto, 2/4): oboes and bassoons answer each other with staccato thirds and held half notes under the 1st-violin melody, strings pizzicato; later flutes, oboes and bassoons trade 3-note staccato chords in imitation. The woodwind imitation gives harmonic stability to the melody and adds brightness. `[era: Classical]`
- Britten, *The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra*, Fugue, m.1–55 (Ex. 8-31; Allegro molto, 2/4): a true fugal exposition for woodwinds alone, entering high to low: piccolo (*p*), Fl 1–2 a 2 (m.8, *p*), Ob 1–2 a 2 (m.20, *mp*), Cl 1–2 a 2 (m.27, *mf*), Bsn 1–2 a 2 (m.41, *ff*), building to *ff* at m.50–55. Uses each instrument's register characteristics fully; many unison pairs.
- Stravinsky, *The Rite of Spring*, "L'Adoration de la Terre" (Part I introduction), m.40–60 (Ex. 8-32; Lento, 2/4 and 3/4): every woodwind has its own part — piccolos, flutes (fluttertongue tuplets), alto flute (10-note runs, trills), oboes (trill, triplets), English horn solo, D clarinet solo (*sempre ff*), A clarinets (grace-note triplets), bass clarinets, bassoons, contrabassoons (grace-note triplets), horns, solo cello, solo bass; from m.57 six solo basses *con sord.* play divided harmonics (one *pizz.*). Each line is exactly notated in 2/4 or 3/4, yet the overlapping 3:2 and 4:6 rhythms make it sound free, like night sounds in spring. The piccolo's double tonguing is cited as perfectly placed. `[era: 20th c.]`

**Agent applicability**
- For a fugal exposition in woods: enter high to low (Picc → Fl → Ob → Cl → Bsn), a 2 per entry, raise the dynamic (velocity/CC1) with each entry.
- For a "nature/night" texture: give every woodwind an independent ostinato with a different tuplet (3, 5, 6, 10) in its best register, quantised exactly; layer solo string harmonics underneath.
- When a grace-note figure repeats on two players, alternate it between the two tracks.

## 8 Woodwinds for color contrast (Usos de la sección de madera para la obtención de contrastes de color) (scan p.275–277, printed p.270–272)

**Concepts**
- An obvious use of the woodwinds is to supply a contrasting color against the strings; one way is to alternate the two sections.

**Rules**
- R23 [PREFER] Alternate woodwinds and strings with short phrases, letting each section dissolve into the other (the woodwind chord fades into the string chord).
- R24 [PREFER] When a figure is passed between sections, finally give it to both sections together (tutti) for a sense of fullness or resolution.
- R25 [PREFER] When re-scoring a final chord for another section, keep the top octave that section introduced earlier, even if the spacing changes slightly.

**Examples**
- Brahms, Symphony No. 2, iii, m.233–240 (Ex. 8-33; Allegretto, 3/4): woodwinds + horns and strings alternate; the woodwind chord dissolves into the string chord (m.236, *p dim.* to *pp*); the last chord is introduced by the strings and then given to woodwinds in different colors, slightly re-spaced, keeping the top octave the winds introduced at m.235; strings end *pizz.*
- Schumann, Symphony No. 1, iii, first Trio, m.48–79 (Ex. 8-34; Molto più vivace, half = 108, 2/4): strings play a short figure (staccato–accented half–staccato, *p*), woodwinds + horns echo it; two bars later trumpets join the winds; from m.60 both sections play it together (*cresc.*, timpani roll), giving fullness. The figure unifies the movement.

**Agent applicability**
- Call-and-response orchestration: 2-bar figure in strings, same figure in Fl/Ob/Cl/Bsn + Hn, then both together at the climax (velocity/CC1 up, timpani roll).
- When winds take over a string chord, overlap by about 1 beat with the strings decaying (CC11 down) as the winds enter.
