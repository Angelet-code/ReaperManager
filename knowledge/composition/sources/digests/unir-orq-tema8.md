## 8.2 Harp (arpa) (scan p.4–8)

**Concepts**
- Modern pedal harp: **47 strings tuned in Cb major** (all pedals up = all flats).
- Range (Fig. 1): **Cb1 (23) – Gb7 (102)** (top written Gb6 under 8va).
- Tuning pegs at the top; the player can retune a string while playing with the left hand. All C strings
  are red, all F strings blue.
- Seven pedals, one per note name, each with **three positions**: up = flat, first notch = natural,
  second notch = sharp. Each string passes two pronged discs that shorten it.
- The lowest Cb string has no pedal mechanism: retune it by hand before playing.
- Pedal layout: **left foot D, C, B; right foot E, F, G, A** (order D C B | E F G A).
- Pedal diagram (notation): a horizontal line; marks above = flat, on = natural, below = sharp, in pedal
  order. Example (Fig. 5): Db, C, Bb | Eb, F, G#, Ab.

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] Continuous chromaticism: improper for the harp (each pitch class has one setting at a time;
  changes need pedal moves).
- R2 [AVOID] Requiring two pedal changes with the same foot at once (one foot can only move two pedals
  with difficulty). Plan changes so they alternate feet and fall in rests or long notes (inferred).
- R3 [PREFER] Write the pedal changes into the part.
- R4 [ALLOWED] Glissando: very effective in orchestral tutti; notate the seven/eight notes of one octave
  (showing the pedal setting), a line, and the arrival note. Can be one hand, two hands, double strings or
  chords (Debussy, *Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune* opening glissandi).
- R5 [AVOID] Trivial, cliché glissandi.
- R6 [ALLOWED] Arpeggios with one or two hands (Debussy, *Faune*).
- Test Q4 (inferred correct option): étouffés = each note damped by the same finger that played it → very
  dry staccato.

**Agent applicability**
- Before writing harp MIDI, choose a pedal setting (7 pitch classes) per passage; check every note of the
  passage belongs to it; chromatic notes outside it require a pedal change with time.
- Glissando in MIDI: write a fast run through the set's pitch classes (all 7 strings per octave, i.e.
  enharmonic set like Cb major or C major), not a chromatic run.

## 8.3 Piano (scan p.9–11)

**Concepts**
- Range (Fig. 8): **A0 (21) – C8 (108)** (8 basso / 15ma notated).
- Three uses with orchestra: soloist (concerto); obbligato within the ensemble; purely orchestral member.
- Pedals: right (sustain) most used — strings keep vibrating after the key is released; left (una corda,
  "pedal celeste") shifts the action so hammers strike one or two of the three strings; middle
  (sostenuto) least used — sustains only the notes held when the pedal is pressed.

**Rules / style facts**
- Before the 20th c. the piano was rarely part of the large orchestra; in 20th/21st-c. scores it appears
  mostly as a **percussive, versatile instrument thanks to its huge range**.
- R1 [ALLOWED] Piano doubling other instruments (Stravinsky, *Petrushka*, Russian Dance).
- R2 [ALLOWED] Piano as orchestral filler (Saint-Saëns, *Carnival of the Animals*).
- R3 [ALLOWED] Piano as accompaniment (Saint-Saëns, *Carnival*, "Tortoises").

**Agent applicability**
- In an orchestral cue, treat piano as a percussive doubler (octaves, ostinati) rather than the main
  harmonic carrier, unless it is a concerto-style feature. CC64 for sustain pedal.

## 8.4 Harpsichord (clave) (scan p.12–13)

**Concepts**
- Strings are **plucked** by quills/plectra (jack = regla/martinete/saltarelo with a leather or plastic,
  formerly bird-quill, plectrum), not struck.
- 5–8 pedals (or stops) change registration → timbral and (stepwise) dynamic variety. **No touch
  dynamics**; volume is managed by adding registers.
- Range (Fig. 13): **F1 (29) – F6 (89)**.
- Derived from the psaltery with a keyboard added.

**Rules / style facts**
- Baroque and early Haydn symphonies: harpsichord plays the basso continuo; then it vanished from the
  orchestra until well into the 20th c.; since ~1945 used again, solo and as orchestral colour (Falla,
  *Harpsichord Concerto*).
- R1 [MUST] Keep in mind its very limited volume: thin the orchestration around it.

**Agent applicability**
- Harpsichord MIDI: velocity has little meaning; shape dynamics by texture (more notes/octaves) and by
  thinning the accompaniment (inferred).

**Tags:** #instrument/harpsichord #continuo

## 8.5 Organ (órgano) (scan p.13–15)

**Concepts**
- Baroque: often played continuo; when continuo became obsolete, it stayed in church and left the
  orchestra.
- Northern-European type: three divisions — swell (órgano expresivo), great (gran órgano), positive
  (órgano positivo), each tied to one or more keyboards.
- Modern organs follow the French Romantic grand organ: 2–5 manuals plus pedalboard. Source: lower manuals
  54 keys, upper ones 27–32; pedalboard 32–34 keys (figures as given in the source).
- Range (Fig. 15): manuals **C2 (36) – C7 (96)**; pedals **C2 (36) – G4 (67)** (written; actual octave
  depends on stop footage, e.g. 16' sounds an octave lower — inferred, not stated).
- Pipes: flue (juegos de boca; wood or metal) and reed (juegos de lengüeta; always metal). Partly capped
  pipes ("chimenea") give lower pitch with shorter length. Wind chest (secreto); formerly bellows, now
  electric blower.
- No touch dynamics: loudness depends on registration; some works need an assistant to change stops.
  Messiaen marks registrations with abbreviations at the start (*Apparition de l'Église éternelle*).

**Rules / style facts**
- R1 [ALLOWED] Organ in orchestra as a **pedal to reinforce the bass** (R. Strauss, *Also sprach
  Zarathustra* opening: low organ pedal C under trumpets/timpani).

**Agent applicability**
- Sub-bass reinforcement under a tutti: organ pedal sustained on the tonic, low (inferred: ~C1–C2
  sounding with a 16' patch).

**Tags:** #instrument/organ #bass-reinforcement

## 8.6 Celesta (scan p.16)
- A keyboard glockenspiel: hammers strike steel bars.
- **Sounds an octave higher than written.** Range (Fig. 18): written C3–C7 → **sounding C4 (60) – C8 (108)**.
- Example: Tchaikovsky, *Nutcracker*, "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy" — celesta melody over pizzicato
  strings (4 soli per section), pp.

**Agent applicability**
- Celesta = soft, magical sparkle; pair with pizzicato strings or harp; register mostly C5–C7 sounding.
