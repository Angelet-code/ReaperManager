## 11 Introduction (scan p.358, printed p.357)

**Concepts**
- Brass section (sección de metal): among the oldest Western instruments, but its full orchestral potential was
  only explored from the late 19th c.
- Four main functions of the orchestral brass (funciones principales):
  1. homophonic unit (unidad homófona), alone or combined with other sections;
  2. presenter of melody (introductora de la melodía): soloist, combined with other instruments, or an
     independent voice in counterpoint;
  3. builder of orchestral climaxes (creadora de clímax);
  4. carrier of timbral effects (efectos tímbricos): mutes, jazz effects, newer sounds/techniques.

**Rules**
- R1 [AVOID] Over-using the brass: it easily eclipses the rest of the orchestra.
- R2 [era: mid-19th c.] Many composers resisted valve trumpets/horns; Wagner and Brahms still wrote for natural
  trumpets and horns in many works although valve instruments existed. Valve brass became fully accepted only
  in the 20th c. (For a period sound, keep trumpet/horn parts to natural-instrument pitches.)

## Early uses of the brass section (Usos primeros) (scan p.358–361, printed p.357–360)

**Rules / style facts**
- R3 [era: Renaissance] Brass doublings depended on the hall (room, church) and on available players; the
  number/type of brass is not fixed before the Classical period.
- R4 [era: Baroque] Typical brass: 2–3 horns, 3 trumpets almost always with timpani, trombones in threes.
- R5 [era: Baroque] The full brass section almost never played together, except in battle pieces, outdoor
  music (Händel *Water Music*) and some opera scenes. When trumpets, horns and trombones appear in one work they
  usually play in different movements.
- R6 [era: Baroque] Trumpets + horns + trombones together in unison/octaves in a Baroque piece is most likely a
  later arrangement, not the original. To sound authentic, NEVER stack all three families in one Baroque tutti.
- R7 [era: Baroque] Clarino trumpet playing and the high horn register were fashionable: Baroque trumpet/horn
  lines sit high.
- R8 [era: Classical] At the height of the Classical period most symphonies had no trombones; trombones were
  standard only in opera and church orchestras.
- R9 [era: Classical] Natural horns and trumpets, after an opening fanfare chord, are confined to tonic and
  dominant notes; trombones are far more versatile (full harmony, chromatic). So in a Classical-style tutti give
  harmonic/chromatic filling to trombones, and only I/V pitches to horns/trumpets (natural instruments).

**Examples**
- Gabrieli, *In Ecclesiis*, interlude (Ex. 11-1, bb.51–67): written for St Mark's Venice, probably 3 trumpets +
  3 trombones, possibly doubled by an identical group in another balcony (antiphonal choirs). Shows block chords
  alternating with imitative eighth/sixteenth-note entries between the two choirs (trumpets vs trombones).
- Händel, *Music for the Royal Fireworks*, Overture bb.1–19 (Ex. 11-2, Adagio, D major): homophonic tutti like
  Gabrieli; horns 1–3 double trumpets 1–3 part for part; oboes 1–3 and violins double the same three upper
  lines; bassoons/contrabassoon with cello/bass; timpani on D/A. Score printed at concert pitch; originally horns
  and trumpets were both in D, the horn sounding an octave below the trumpet. Later in the piece trumpets and
  horns alternate antiphonally before the homophonic texture returns.
- Beethoven, Symphony 5, finale bb.1–8 (Ex. 11-3, Allegro, half = 84, C major, ff): trombones (alto, tenor,
  bass) are held back until the finale and then reinforce the climactic opening; horns and trumpets in C play
  the opening fanfare chord, then from bar 5 only tonic/dominant repeated notes, while trombones sustain harmony.
- (Printed p.363 top, referent on missing pages) R10 [AVOID] Orchestrating the brass that vigorously throughout a
  whole work: it becomes grandiloquent and tedious and robs real climaxes of power and majesty.

## Brass doubling in the modern orchestra (Duplicación…) (scan p.362–363, printed p.363–364)

**Concepts**
- Section growth: late-Classical norm 2 horns, 2 trumpets, 3 trombones; tuba added later; then 4 horns
  (9 different voices possible). Modern standard: 4 horns, 3 trumpets, 3 trombones, tuba = up to 11 voices.
  Consequently in ordinary 4-part chords many notes are doubled.
- The woodwind chord-layout techniques from ch.8 (printed p.253–254) also apply to brass: juxtaposition
  (yuxtaposición), interlocking (intercalado), enclosure (inclusión), partial overlap (superposición parcial).

**Rules**
- R11 [PREFER] At mf or softer, give each brass instrument its own note; the chord balances according to each
  instrument's position in its register.
- R12 [MUST] Above mf, double the horns: two horns in unison ≈ one trumpet or one trombone in power. So at f/ff
  pair horns on each chord tone that a single trumpet/trombone carries.
- R13 [ALLOWED] Exception to R12: when the trumpet is in its low register, the 2-horns = 1-trumpet equivalence
  does not apply (a low trumpet is weaker; fewer horns needed per note) (link R12).
- R14 [PREFER] (from Ex. 11-4 layouts) Standard modern voicing, top to bottom: trumpets on the top chord tones,
  horns (2 or 4) directly below or interlocked with the trumpets, trombones filling a close/open triad in the
  tenor-bass register, tuba alone at the bottom, usually an octave below the lowest trombone (often doubling the
  root). With 2 trumpets, horns in 4 are spread over 2 notes (2 per note) to match them.
- R15 [ALLOWED] "Dark chord" (acorde oscuro, Ex. 11-4, p): trumpets kept low and close, horns 1/3 and 2 inside
  the chord, horn 4 in bass clef next to the trombones; low, close voicing for a sombre colour.

**Examples**
- Ex. 11-4 (concert pitch): about 16 sample brass chords for 2 or 3 trumpets, 2/3/4 horns, 3 trombones, tuba,
  marked ff/f/f/p, showing different doubling plans (e.g. "Trb.3 + Tuba" on the bass, "2 or 4 horns" options).
- Ex. 11-5 (transposed score: 4 horns in F, 2 Bb trumpets, Trb 1–2, Trb 3 + tuba), same progression three ways:
  ff = horns doubled in pairs, tuba in octaves; mf = "weaker" (más débil), horns spread to independent notes;
  p = "very dark" (muy oscura), whole chord lowered and closed, trumpets low.

**Agent applicability**
- Brass chord at velocity/CC1 for ff: 4 horns → 2 notes (2 each) per trumpet note; trombones 3 notes; tuba
  root an octave below Trb 3. At mf: 4 horns on 4 different notes.
- For a single-patch "Horns a4" library, a2 doubling is implied by the patch; balance against one trumpet by
  assigning each horn line about one trumpet line's weight only when 2+ horns are on it (inferred).

## Homophonic writing for the brass section (Escritura homófona) (scan p.363–374, printed p.364–375)

**Concepts**
- The brass can be used homophonically alone or with strings and winds through doublings.
- Turm-Musik (tower music): 17th–18th-c. brass music (Pezel, Pachelbel, J.S. Bach, Reiche) played from the church
  tower as the congregation left. `[era: 17th–18th c.]`

### Brass alone
- Stravinsky, *Chorale Variations on "Vom Himmel hoch"* bb.1–8 (Ex. 11-6, quarter = 82): 3 C trumpets + 2 tenor
  trombones + bass trombone, six independent parts (Bach's four plus two added), evoking Turm-Musik and organ
  pedal combinations; dense brass texture, very effective, though the last phrase can sound a little muddy.
- R16 [PREFER] When transcribing Baroque music, think, feel and hear in the Baroque idiom (as Stravinsky did).
- R17 [AVOID] Six-voice close brass counterpoint low in the trombones at the end of a phrase: risk of a muddy
  sound (inferred from the "algo sucia" remark).

### Brass combined with other sections
- R18 [PREFER] Method: reduce passages to piano, mark doublings (colour pencils), find which notes are essential
  and how they are doubled; always track the melody or melodic notes the composer wants to highlight.
- Bruckner, Symphony 7 finale bb.191–212 (Ex. 11-7, Allegro ma non troppo, ff marcato → "Breit und wuchtig" →
  "immer breiter" fff): brass (4 horns, 3 F trumpets, alto/tenor/bass trombones, 2 Bb tenor tubas, 2 F bass tubas,
  contrabass tuba) dominate a dense homophonic climax and give it tremendous gravity. bb.191–198: brass doubled at
  unison/octave by strings or woodwinds on some notes but not all → intermittent colour effect. The near-
  doubling of the opening bars returns for one bar and becomes strict octave doubling at b.210 as the climax
  builds (everyone on the same dotted unison line, fff, ending on a fermata).
  - R19 [ALLOWED] Doubling brass by strings/winds only on selected notes produces flickering colour; switch to
    strict octave doubling of all parts to crown a climax.
  - Wagner/Bruckner tubas, old notation `[era: late 19th c.]`: Bb tenor tubas sound a major 9th (14 semitones)
    below written; F bass tubas a perfect 12th (19 semitones) below; contrabass tuba an octave (12) below written.
- Elgar, *Enigma Variations*, "W.M.B." bb.25–32 (Ex. 11-8, Allegro di molto, 3/4, fff, Bb major): chorale-style
  close dominated by brass (4 F horns, 3 F trumpets, 3 trombones + tuba) with full woodwinds, timpani and strings
  doubling the same chords; accents and tenuto.
  - R20 [PREFER] For a full, rich tutti, use octave and double-octave doublings of the brass chord by
    woodwinds and strings.
- Musorgsky–Ravel, *Pictures at an Exhibition*, "The Great Gate of Kiev" bb.1–17 (Ex. 11-9, Eb major, alla
  breve, Maestoso, f): brass chorale (4 horns, 3 C trumpets, Trb 1–2, Trb 3 + tuba) over bassoons 1–2 + contrabassoon;
  woodwind choir answers at b.13 (f, then mf), brass returns at p under it.
  - R21 [PREFER] Complete and double the bass with 2 bassoons + contrabassoon so trombones 1–2 can sit in their
    most brilliant register instead of carrying the low chord tones.
  - R22 [AVOID] Copying piano low-register grace notes/ornaments into the orchestra: with tuba and contrabassoon
    the low register is already intense, so Ravel drops them.
  - R23 [ALLOWED] Timpani usually reinforce chord roots, but a long dominant pedal (Bb, octave not checked) can hold
    tension; it resolves when the woodwinds enter (b.13), after which Eb dominates the timpani.

## Presenting melody in the brass (Presentación de la melodía) (scan p.374–380, printed p.375–381)

### As soloist (Como solista)
- Hindemith, *Nobilissima Visione*, III passacaglia bb.1–6 (Ex. 11-10, 3/4, "Feierlich bewegt", quarter ≈ 80, f):
  theme in brass unison/octaves: 4 F horns and 2 C trumpets in octaves, 3 trombones doubling the horns at unison.
  - R24 [PREFER] Brass unison + octave presentation of a theme gives a strong, penetrating sound.
  - R25 [PREFER] For a dark, noble unison: keep trumpets and horns out of their top registers; put only the
    trombones in their high register (adds strength and nobility).
  - R26 [ALLOWED] For extreme brilliance, transpose the same passage up a 3rd or 4th so trumpets and horns reach
    their high register.
  - R27 [PREFER] Save the tuba as a fresh colour for a later variation: Hindemith brings it in at the 2nd
    variation (rehearsal 34) stating the theme two octaves below; later repeats add other orchestral doublings for
    new flashes of colour.
- Four horns in unison (Cuatro trompas al unísono): a favourite device for brilliance/splendour since Wagner,
  Mahler, R. Strauss (cf. Ex. 10-12, *Don Juan*).
  - R28 [AVOID] Loud, high unison passages for 4 horns: tiny intonation differences cause intense beating near
    the players, so notes crack (Schuller, *Horn Technique*, 1962, pp.82–83).
  - R29 [PREFER] Score such passages, when possible, for horns 1 and 3 only, or two horns reinforced by one
    trumpet. (Seating players ~3 m apart would remove the problem; not practical.)
  - Agent note (inferred): with sample libraries the cracking issue does not occur, but for realism use an a2
    horn patch or 2 horns + 1 trumpet rather than "horns a4" on high ff unison themes.

### In combination with other instruments (En combinación con otros instrumentos)
- R30 [PREFER] Reduce scores to piano (2 or 4 hands) to perceive all doublings (suggested on Ex. 11-15, easy, and
  11-18, harder; beyond this range).
- R. Strauss, *Don Quixote* bb.189–193 (Ex. 11-11, Allegro): solo cello line doubled by horns 1–4 with staggered
  entries (horns 1–2 and 3–4 entering in turn, triplet figures), widening the articulation options of the melody.
  - R31 [PREFER] Horn + cello is a frequent doubling for long, sustained melodies.
  - R32 [ALLOWED] Staggered horn entries on a cello line let the melody be articulated in segments.
  - Old-style horn notation `[era: 19th c.]`: horn parts in bass clef are written an octave low, so written notes
    sound a perfect 4th (5 semitones) higher (instead of a 5th lower as in treble clef).
- Weinberger, *Schwanda the Bagpiper*, Polka and Fugue bb.185–191 (Ex. 11-12, Allegro, 2/4, ff): C trumpets
  double the oboes' melody; full orchestra with organ and 4 offstage trumpets in B (dietro la scena, fff chorale);
  horns sustain ff chords, timpani ostinato.
  - R33 [PREFER] Trumpet + oboe is a very common doubling: both timbres are incisive, so the melody stays audible
    even against the full orchestra plus organ.
- Prokofiev, Symphony 5, III bb.1–3 (Ex. 11-13, Adagio, quarter = 60, 3/4 (9/8), mp): tuba doubles the double
  basses' gentle line (pizzicato-like bass with strings' triplet accompaniment).
  - R34 [ALLOWED] Doubling a soft double-bass melody/bass with tuba gives a heavier character, reinforces and
    darkens the sound.
- Wagner, *Siegfried*, Prelude to Act III bb.1–7 (Ex. 11-14, "Lebhaft, doch gewichtig", p cresc. → f):
  bass tuba solo articulating every note ("very sustained but not slurred") doubled by bass clarinet and bassoons
  playing the same line legato; 3 trombones and Eb tenor tubas swell on sustained chords; timpani roll; strings
  in staccato arpeggio figures; cellos/basses also on the theme.
  - R35 [ALLOWED] Double one line with two different articulations (detached brass + legato woodwinds): the
    result keeps attack definition with a smooth connected body.

**Agent applicability**
- Brass melody alone: horns + trombones in unison, trumpets an octave above; register choice sets dark vs bright.
- Oboe melody needing more punch in a tutti: add a C trumpet in unison (same MIDI notes), not more strings.
- Heavy dark bass line at mp: copy the double-bass notes to the tuba track (same pitch; tuba sounds as written).
- Line with "articulated legato": put the notes on tuba/brass as non-legato (short gaps, accented velocity) and
  the same notes on bassoon/bass clarinet as legato (overlapping notes).
- Avoid full brass tutti for whole sections; reserve it for the climax (R1, R10).
