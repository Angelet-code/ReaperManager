# Sonata form outline (estructura sonata)

## Sonata form (forma sonata)

**Concepts**
- Exposition (exposición): optional introduction; **theme A** (tema A) in the home key; optional
  transition/bridge (puente); **theme B** in another key — the dominant, or the relative major when the
  movement is in minor — with a character opposite to A; closing section/coda, which usually ends in B's key.
- Development (desarrollo): free play with all the material, modulating through keys; new elements are
  optional; ends on a **dominant pedal (pedal de V)** that leads into the recapitulation.
- Recapitulation (reexposición): theme A in the home key; optional bridge; **theme B now in the home key**;
  coda.

**Rules**
- R1 [MUST] Theme A in the tonic in both exposition and recapitulation.
- R2 [MUST] Exposition theme B in a contrasting key: V in major; relative major (III) in minor.
- R3 [PREFER] Theme B contrasts in character with A.
- R4 [PREFER] Exposition ends (codetta) in theme B's key.
- R5 [ALLOWED] Development modulates freely and may introduce new material.
- R6 [PREFER] Close the development with a dominant pedal to prepare the return.
- R7 [MUST] Recapitulation restates B in the tonic (the bridge is adjusted so it does not modulate —
  inferred).

**Agent applicability**
- Plan template: [Intro?] A(I) → bridge → B(V or III) → codetta(V/III) ‖ Development (keys away, ends on
  V pedal) ‖ A(I) → bridge → B(I) → Coda(I).
