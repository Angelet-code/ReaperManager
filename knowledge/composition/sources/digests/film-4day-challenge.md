# 4-Day Challenge (film-scoring deck): slides 1–10

Terse slide bullets; this digest expands them only where the slide is explicit. Style scope: **film /
trailer / media scoring** with sample libraries, not common-practice rules. Slide 1 is a title.

## Harmony → emotion basics (slide 2)

**Concepts**
- General polarity: major chords → positive emotions; minor chords → negative emotions.
- Traditional / tonal / diatonic progressions: major = love, positive; minor = sadness.
- Non-traditional (non-diatonic) progressions — parallel chord movement, chords a major/minor 3rd apart
  (chromatic mediants), tritone relations, etc.: major = magic, fantasy, supernatural, grandeur;
  minor = ominous, darkness.
- Extensions and dissonances added: on major chords they add character and can intensify the positive
  emotion; on minor chords they give tension, action, suspense, thriller.
- Clusters: darkness, horror.
- Harmonic rhythm: **fast changes enhance the emotion; slow changes support** (and can enhance) without
  getting in the way of scene or dialogue.

**Rules**
- R1 [PREFER] Pick chord quality by target emotion: major diatonic = warmth/love; minor diatonic = sadness.
- R2 [PREFER] For magic/fantasy/grandeur use non-diatonic major-chord motion (parallel majors, mediant
  moves by M3/m3, tritone pairs); for menace use the same motion with minor chords.
- R3 [PREFER] For suspense/action/thriller, add extensions/dissonance to minor chords (e.g. m(add9),
  m(maj7), added 2nds/4ths).
- R4 [ALLOWED] Clusters for horror/darkness.
- R5 [PREFER] Under dialogue, slow harmonic rhythm; for an emotional peak without dialogue, faster
  changes.

**Agent applicability**
- Map Spanish requests: "épico/mágico" → non-diatonic major (e.g. C–Ab–E–C); "oscuro/amenazante" →
  non-diatonic minor (Cm–Abm, Cm–F#m); "tensión/acción" → minor with added dissonance; "terror" → clusters
  (inferred examples).

## Telling a story with harmony (slide 3)

**Concepts (major keys)**
- I: introduces the character / place / situation. Full triad establishes the mood; an **open fifth (no
  3rd) is neutral**.
- IV: positive; starts telling the story (if the melody hasn't started yet); enhances emotion.
- vi, iii, (ii): same function as IV but negative / sad / emotional.
- V: strong statement; good for the **end of a scene, especially without the 3rd**.
- vii°: dark.

**Rules**
- R6 [PREFER] Open a cue/scene on I; use an open fifth if the mood must stay undefined.
- R7 [PREFER] Move to IV to "start the story" positively, or to vi/iii/ii for the sad/emotional version.
- R8 [ALLOWED] End a scene on V, ideally without 3rd (open 5th), for a strong unresolved statement.
- R9 [ALLOWED] Harmonic change is not always needed: the same story can be told with texture, timbre,
  rhythm and tempo variation over static harmony.

**Agent applicability**
- Scene-cue skeleton: I (establish) → IV or vi (story) → … → V5 (no 3rd) at the scene cut.
- Under dialogue, hold one chord and vary orchestration instead of changing chords.

## Template: fast action patches (slide 4)
- Full orchestra: full-orchestra long notes (sustain) and short notes (staccato) patches.
- Full sections: strings long/short (optional), brass long, brass short, woodwinds long, woodwinds short.
- Percussion: orchestral/epic percussion; sub boom.
- Others: piano (several MIDI tracks into one patch), choir, horns section legato and trumpets section
  legato (poly-legato if available), stingers.
- R10 [PREFER] For fast action sketching, start from section/ensemble long+short patches before writing
  individual instruments (inferred from "template" purpose).

## Counterpoint: creating separation or association (slide 5)

**Concepts**
- Main arrangement parameters for separating lines: **rhythm (and articulation), register, timbre/colour**.
- Secondary: dynamics, line direction/shape, articulation.
- Mix: space — panning and depth.
- The fewer elements two lines share → the more separation (listener hears them as different things);
  the more they share → the less separation (they fuse / associate).

**Rules**
- R11 [MUST] To make two simultaneous lines read as independent, differ in at least one main parameter
  (rhythm, register or timbre), ideally several.
- R12 [PREFER] To fuse lines into one gesture, give them the same rhythm, register zone and colour.
- R13 [ALLOWED] Use panning/depth (reverb, distance) as extra separation in the mix.

**Agent applicability**
- Counter-melody check: if it shares rhythm *and* register *and* instrument family with the melody, change
  one (e.g. longer note values, an octave lower, or a different family).

## Doubling (slide 6)

**Concepts / best practices**
- **Group by colour, not by range**: e.g. cello + bassoon; string pizzicato + harp; double reeds + muted
  trumpet.
- Other doubling criteria:
  - Construction: similar construction = similar sound quality, e.g. brass-built: brass section, tubular
    bells, cymbal, tam-tam; idiophones: wood blocks, marimba.
  - Waveform / decay type: fast fixed decay — pizz strings, piano, harp; medium fixed decay — tubular bells,
    celesta, crotales, vibraphone; (variable decay = sustaining instruments, implied).
  - Emotion.
- Why double: it **widens the orchestral palette**. Too much doubling has the opposite effect: it reduces
  the colour-change effect (acceptable, but be aware).
- Typical full-orchestra bass: **double basses + piano + harp + trombone + bassoon**.
- Piano's extreme ranges enhance/extend the orchestral range.
- Flute (or woodwind) melody with harp accompaniment.

**Steps (method)**
1. Pick the instrument for a layer (e.g. melody).
2. Decide what the layer needs: more definition, more weight, expanded colour, or a specific emotion.
3. Choose the doubling criterion (colour, construction, decay type, emotion).
4. Choose the actual doubling instrument(s).

**Rules**
- R14 [PREFER] Double with instruments of similar colour or similar decay envelope.
- R15 [AVOID] Doubling everything all the time: keeps colour from changing.
- R16 [ALLOWED] Piano in extreme low/high octaves to extend the orchestra's range.

**Agent applicability**
- Pizzicato line → add harp and/or piano (same fast decay). Bell-like melody → celesta/vibes/crotales
  (mid decay). Bass weight → DB + bassoon + trombone + piano low octave + harp.

## Template: strings basics (slide 7)
- Long notes: ensemble strings sustain (EQ for warmth), low strings sustain, strings sustain con sordino,
  violins I & II section legato, cello section legato, DB+cello octave legato, DB sustains.
- Short notes: strings staccato, high strings staccato, high strings octave staccato, low strings
  staccato, DB staccato, DB pizzicato.
- Other: cello section tremolo, cello divisi.

## Template: orchestral enhancers (slide 8)
- High: celesta, harp notes, harp gliss, mark tree, cymbal swells, piatti (clash cymbals), shakers,
  crotales, etc.
- Mid and low: tam-tam, snare (different sizes and panning), timpani hits, timpani swells (mf/f), timpani
  roll, gran cassa (bass drum), sub boom, drums.

## Types of reverb (slide 9)
- "Panoramic" reverb: long, wide, wet — for long sustained strings, sustained brass, choir.
- "Impact" reverb: shorter, big-room simulation with quicker decay — for spiccato, brass stabs, woodwinds.
- Separate reverbs for bass instruments (author almost never uses one), low percussion, other percussion,
  synths, special FX.
- Grouping axes for reverb sends: high vs low, long vs short.
- R17 [PREFER] Long/sustained material → long wide reverb; short/articulated material → shorter, faster-
  decaying reverb.
- R18 [AVOID] Reverb on bass instruments (author's practice: almost never).

**Agent applicability**
- REAPER: create two main reverb returns ("Panoramic" long, "Impact" short) and route long vs short
  articulation tracks accordingly; keep basses dry or nearly dry.

## Momentum: the five elements (slide 10)

**Concepts** — any passage can contain up to five *components* (not layers; may be combined or absent):
- **Direction**: anything that creates a tendency from A to B — a melody, a progression building tension,
  increasing/decreasing orchestration density. Makes the music feel like it goes somewhere.
- **Movement**: anything that creates flow/action — rhythmic pattern, ostinato, repeating/modulating
  progression, call-and-response between instruments or families. Also helps direction.
- **Background (glue)**: sits behind in the mix, not obvious but missed when absent; fills gaps and joins
  ideas.
- **Enhancer**: intensifies, augments or develops an idea, connects sections, adds sparkle / ear candy —
  runs, scales, trills, woodwind flutters behind a soaring melody, cymbal or timpani roll swells.
- **Bass**: extra low end. The modern orchestral sound needs a solid, compact, controlled low end; tools:
  low synths, electric bass, sub-boom hits, well-recorded sampled double basses.

**Rules**
- R19 [PREFER] When a passage feels static, check which of the five elements are missing and add one
  (usually movement or direction).
- R20 [MUST] (modern orchestral/film style) Provide a dedicated, controlled low-end layer.

**Agent applicability**
- Arrangement checklist per section: Direction? Movement? Glue? Enhancer at transitions? Bass layer?
  Tag tracks with their element role.
