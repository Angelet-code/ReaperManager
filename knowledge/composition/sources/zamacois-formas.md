# Zamacois — *Curso de Formas Musicales*

- **Bibliographic:** Joaquín Zamacois, *Curso de Formas Musicales*, Editorial Labor, Barcelona, 1960.
  Spanish. ~275 printed pages, § numbered 1–273 plus appendix notes A–G.
- **Scope:** phrase theory, small forms, imitation/canon/fugue (incl. a full French school-fugue method),
  variation, suite and dances, sonata/quartet/symphony/concerto, overture, symphonic poem, ballet, dances
  and character pieces, secular and sacred vocal forms.
- **Style scope / reliability:** a classroom analysis guide for **common-practice (Bach–Beethoven,
  romantic) forms**; rules are descriptive norms with many cited exceptions. The fugue part (§84–103) is
  prescriptive (Gedalge/Dupré school). Beethoven sonatas are cited as "Sonata n (op.)"; bars counted from the
  first full bar. Zamacois's *período* = English "phrase" (4 bars), his *frase* = 8-bar unit. Tempos are
  only verbal — BPM values in the notes are general knowledge.
- **Digests:** `sources/digests/zamacois-formas--p001-050.md` (§1–87), `--p051-100.md` (§87–155),
  `--p101-145.md` (§155–273 + appendix).

## Deliberately not turned into rules
- Baroque notation note (App. F: dotted rhythms against triplets align to the triplet) — performance
  practice, belongs in a performance/rendering note, not form.
- Historical narrative (origins of fugue, opera schools, liturgical history) kept only where it changes a
  planning decision.
- Fugues by augmentation/retrograde (obsolete), triple/quadruple fugue details beyond one rule.
