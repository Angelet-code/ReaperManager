# UNIR — Instrumentación y Orquestación (Temas 1–8)

## Bibliographic info
- Universidad Internacional de La Rioja (UNIR), course *Instrumentación y Orquestación*, study units
  ("Temas") 1–8, PDF course notes in Spanish (author not stated in the digests).
- Main secondary sources inside the course: Walter Piston, *Orchestration* (instrument chapters, pp. 139–373);
  Samuel Adler, *The Study of Orchestration* (range and register charts).
- Source ids used in note frontmatter: `unir-orq-tema1` … `unir-orq-tema8`, with scan/printed page numbers
  (they coincide for these PDFs).

## Scope
- Tema 1: line-ups, notation by family, clefs, transposing instruments.
- Temas 2–3: method of orchestrating a piano piece and orchestral textures.
- Temas 4–8: bowed strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion, harp and keyboards — ranges, registers,
  techniques, style facts, canonic repertoire examples.
- Style scope: Western orchestral practice from the Baroque to the early 20th c.; not film-specific, and
  nothing on sample libraries (that bridge is added in the notes, marked inferred).

## Reliability
- Good for register colours, idiomatic limits and historical roles (Piston/Adler lineage).
- The course names octaves one lower than this vault (middle C = Do3); the digests convert to C4 = 60.
- Range extremes were read from small staff figures; values marked "~" or "[?]" in the digests are
  approximate (violin/viola/cello top notes, tuba range ends, bassoon register boundaries, small trumpet
  tops, flute extreme top note, contrabassoon lowest note, tubular bell tube size).
- Known doubtful claims: Tema 1 says the bass trombone "reads an octave lower" (standard practice: concert
  pitch) and lists baritone sax and requinto under "M6 below" (baritone sax is −21; requinto sounds a m3
  higher). The notes keep the standard values and flag the claims.
- Sections skipped by the digests: study guides, "Lo + recomendado", "+ Información", tests (used only to
  confirm facts).
