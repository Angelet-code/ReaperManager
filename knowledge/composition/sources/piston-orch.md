# Source card: Piston, Orchestration

## Bibliographic info
- Walter Piston, *Orchestration*, W. W. Norton, New York, 1955 (English). Scanned two-page spreads
  (scan ids like `s112R`); notes cite printed pages.
- Source id in frontmatter: `piston-orch` + printed pages (e.g. `piston-orch p.240-242`); front matter as
  `p.vii-ix`.

## Scope and era
- Mid-20th-c. orchestral practice (1955): double horns replacing single F horns, F tuba as the all-round
  tuba, gut strings, detachable mutes, saxophone as a dance-band colour. Repertoire Haydn to Stravinsky,
  Shostakovich and Copland.
- Part One: the instruments (ch.1–18). Part Two: analysis of orchestration by texture type (ch.19–25;
  the vault's [[texture-types]] classifier comes from here via UNIR). Part Three: problems of melody,
  accompaniment, chords and voice leading (ch.26–29) and a short conclusion.
- Where it differs from Adler (2002), the notes keep both, tag Piston `[era: 1955]` and default to Adler
  or modern library practice; e.g. the "blanket of horns", the tuba choice, jeté counts, mute-change time.

## Reliability
- High for technique, idiom, doubling and balance rules; ranges come from his figures (e.g. Fig. 71 horn).
- MIDI numbers and sounding values were converted by the digest authors. Chapter numbers are inferred in
  several digests (only some chapter openings carry a number).
- Doubtful readings are kept out of rules and marked `[?]`: vibraphone "four octaves from 4-foot C"
  (48–96, not used), harpsichord bottom F1, some example pitches.
- Coverage gaps: printed p.223–224 (end of ch.11) fall between two digests; the index (p.463–477) was
  skipped as non-instructional. Nothing else is missing.
