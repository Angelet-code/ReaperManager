# Source card: Adler, The Study of Orchestration (2nd ed.) — trombone fill

## Bibliographic info
- Samuel Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, 2nd edition (English, W. W. Norton, 1989). PDF pages 327–330
  = printed p.312–315.
- Source id in frontmatter: `adler-orq-en2` + printed pages of this edition (e.g. `adler-orq-en2 p.312-313`).

## Scope and use
- Used **only** to replace the four trombone pages missing from the Spanish 3rd-edition scan
  ([[adler-orq-es]] printed p.342–345). Read: tenor range E2–Bb4 (40–70) with C5–F5 (72–77) "difficult
  and risky"; tenor registers (40–43 dark, 45–65 very strong, 67–70 very intense); positions I–VII and
  their series; pedals to E1 (28), to C1 (24) with the F trigger; bass trombone Bb1–Bb4 (34–70), B1 (35)
  only with an E attachment, F-side 2nd partials 36–41; alto trombone A2–Eb5 (45–75).
- Pages p.316–325 (tonguing, glissando, mutes, contrabass and valve trombones, tuba, euphonium "same range
  as the bass trombone") were checked as text only, to confirm the Spanish edition.

## Reliability
- Older edition (1989) than the vault default (3rd ed., 2002); trombone facts are stable between editions,
  and Adler's 3rd-edition Appendix A ranges agree (tenor 40–77, bass 34–70, alto 45–75).
- Read from chart crops (saved under `composition/_render/adler2-en/`); the alto trombone's weak-zone
  boundary (≈ Eb3, 51) comes from a tiny inline figure `[?]`.
- Tone of the slide glissando differs between editions (English: "humorous, a cliché"; Spanish: "very
  effective because uncommon"); [[trombone-slide-techniques]] keeps both.
