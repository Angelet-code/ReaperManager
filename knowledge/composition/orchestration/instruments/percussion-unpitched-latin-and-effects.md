# Wood, Latin drums and effect percussion

## Core idea
Colour instruments beyond the standard section ([[percussion-unpitched]]). Graded sets (blocks, toms,
cowbells, bongos) are unpitched but run low → high, so they can play melodic-rhythmic shapes. Adler
dates many of these as 20th-c. additions; `[era: 1955, Piston]` the Latin group is still "exotic", the
wood block a 20th-c. American colour, and sizzle or Chinese cymbals dance-band instruments.

## Wood
| Instrument | Sound | Idiom |
|---|---|---|
| Wood blocks (3–5) | high, hard, very dry | secco strokes, rolls, fast runs; waltz patterns |
| Temple blocks (5) | hollower, sweeter, roughly pentatonic | fast 16th runs, clock-like or Asian colour; no hard mallets |
| Claves | highest block + "ping" | the clave ostinato; reinforce secco chords |
| Castanets (concert / paddle) | dry clicks | Spanish dance rhythms, triplet and 16th rolls; also sharpen attacks |
| Sandpaper blocks | rough shuffle | short strokes, steady 8ths, rubbed rolls |
| Maracas | seed rattle | soft solo shake (best pp), struck note, Latin 8th ostinato; one is enough outside Latin music |
| Güiro | scrape | single scrapes and rolls; louder than ratchet or sandpaper |
| Vibraslap (quijada) | chattering buzz | single strokes only (long buzz) |
| Ratchet (carraca) | rattle | loud passages and rolls; no isolated strokes |
| Whip (látigo) | one loud crack | a single sforzando |
| Log / slit drum | dark, two pitches a 3rd–5th apart | replaces blocks with a darker sound |
| Hammer (Mahler 6) | huge thud | one or two ff strokes at catastrophic points; say wood or metal |

## Drums
- **Tom-toms** (four, high → low): single-head crisp, double-head darker; snare technique, sticks or yarn
  mallets; often in dialogue with wood or temple blocks. Notate four drums in the four spaces.
- **Timbales** (two, 33/35 cm, metal shell): metallic, penetrating; sticks, mallets, hands, shell (*cáscara*).
- **Bongos**: a joined pair tuned a P4–P5 apart; two pairs possible. `AVOID` expecting virtuoso Latin hand
  technique from an orchestral player; tom-tom technique is fine.
- **Congas**: hands best; rim higher than centre, write the two on separate lines.
- **Cuica**: rubbed rod, squeaking glides (samba, comic). **Lion's roar**: rosined cord, a roar.

## Metal and aerophones
- **Anvil**: ff strokes in march rhythm (Wagner *Rheingold*, Nibelheim). **Brake drum**: hard metallic
  ostinato (Copland). **Cowbells**: European clappered (Mahler 6) or Latin clapperless struck
  with a stick; several sizes placed by relative pitch.
- **Sleigh bells**: shaken 8ths, rolls p < ff (Mahler 4; winter or festive). **Bell tree / mark tree**: one
  up or down sweep, let ring. **Wind chimes** (bamboo, wood, glass, metal): let-ring texture, never
  rhythmic. **Thunder sheet**: long roll p < fff. **Finger cymbals**: soft, high pulses (early-music
  colour); not crotales.
- **Wind machine**: crank speed = pitch and level (Strauss *Alpine Symphony*, Ravel *Daphnis*). **Sirens,
  car horns**: city or industrial programme (Varèse sirens, Gershwin *An American in Paris* horns); state type and volume.

## Rules
- R1 `PREFER` Graded sets as melody: assign each block, tom or bell a fixed note, ordered low → high.
- R2 `ALLOWED` Latin feel: claves carry the clave, maracas or güiro an 8th/16th ostinato, at moderate level
  inside an orchestral texture.
- R3 `ALLOWED` Castanets and tambourine evoke Spain (Bizet *Carmen*) but also sharpen rhythms in any style.
- R4 `AVOID` Overusing sirens, horns and similar effects: most are clichés (Adler). One appearance with a
  programmatic reason.
- R5 `MUST` Name the exact type when it changes the sound: whistle, hammer material, tambourine size,
  cymbal kind.

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- One track per instrument family; a fixed note per block or drum. Darwin zones and Symphony Series maps:
  [[library-translation-table]]. GM fallback (inferred): high/low wood block 76/77, claves 75, maracas 70,
  cabasa 69, short/long güiro 73/74, vibraslap 58, cowbell 56, high/low bongo 60/61, mute/open high conga
  62/63, low conga 64, high/low timbale 65/66, mute/open cuica 78/79, toms 50/48/47/45/43/41.
- Whip, anvil, hammer: single notes, velocity 110–127, on the accent.
- Wind machine, thunder sheet: a long note with a CC1/CC11 swell; wind chimes and bell tree: a long note,
  let ring.
- Tom fill: 16ths running high → low over 3–4 notes, answered by blocks.

## Checklist
- [ ] Each effect has a dramatic reason and appears rarely.
- [ ] Vibraslap and whip only as single strokes; ratchet only in loud passages.
- [ ] Graded instruments use a consistent low → high note order.
- [ ] Latin ostinati sit under, not over, the orchestral melody.
