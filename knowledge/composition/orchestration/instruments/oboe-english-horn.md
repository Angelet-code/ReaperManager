# Oboe and English horn

## Core idea
Conical bore, **double reed**: pungent, nasal, penetrating, plaintive; the most individual woodwind, a
soloist more than a blender. Rarer relatives (oboe d'amore, oboe da caccia, heckelphone):
[[oboe-english-horn-damore-and-heckelphone]].

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Full (sounding) | Practical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oboe | 0 | 58–93 (90–91 rare; 92–93 never in orchestra) | 60–86 (Piston 59–86) |
| English horn (in F) | −7 | 52–84 (written B3–G6, top [?]) | 52–79 (Piston: ≤ 81 undoubled, 82 ceiling) |

Oboe registers: **58–65** lowest fifth — dense, strong, coarser as it descends (58 cannot start softly; 59
easier) · **65–81** warm, reedy, penetrating: best solo zone (Adler: effective to 84) · **82–89** thin; above
86 less oboe-like, delicate for professionals, mostly doubling · **90–93** constricted, specialist only.
English horn: **52–59** rich, deep, projecting yet softer than the oboe's low fifth · **60–74** soft,
reedy, melancholy · **75–84** weak, sounds like an oboe.

## Rules
- R1 `MUST` treat the oboe as **louder as it descends** (opposite of the flute; pair oboe low/mid with flute
  high).
- R2 `NEVER` pp in the oboe's lowest fifth (58–65); keep soft low notes short; 58 only at f. A pp line there
  belongs to the English horn.
- R3 `NEVER` 92–93 in orchestral parts; 90–91 are rare, approached by step, never in active legato
  figuration.
- R4 `PREFER` expressive melody ≤ 81; above, the oboe adds brilliance when it doubles woodwinds or upper
  strings.
- R5 `AVOID` fast slurred arpeggios and long agile figuration; `PREFER` neatly articulated rhythmic patterns
  (Ravel *Tombeau*). Scales and fairly wide leaps are fine at moderate tempo.
- R6 `ALLOWED` fast single-tongued staccato: the sharpest, driest of the winds. `AVOID` double/triple
  tonguing and very fast repeated notes (give them to flutes and clarinets).
- R7 `MUST` every oboe note has a tongued attack: no from-nothing entries (use flute, clarinet or strings).
- R8 `ALLOWED` all major and minor trills except on 58–59; `AVOID` the 60–61 trill, tremolos above the staff
  (> 77) and tremolos wider than a P4 (Piston; Adler allows up to a P5).
- R9 `MUST` frequent rests: the oboe uses the least air, so phrases can be long, but lips, tongue and stale
  air need relief (breath every ≈ 6–8 bars, general knowledge).
- R10 `PREFER` the oboe as soloist for plaintive, folk-like, pastoral or exotic tunes, often within one
  octave (69–81), restated an octave lower.
- R11 `AVOID` oboes as soft blending filler in woodwind chords (they stick out). `ALLOWED` them in chords and
  rhythmic tutti figures for incisiveness. Two oboes in 3rds are "tangy"; three in close triads make a crisp
  staccato block.
- R12 `PREFER` doublings for an important line only (it can eclipse the partner): + Vln I or Va unison
  (edge); + flute unison or flute 8va; + clarinet unison (forceful, Piston; smoother, UNIR).
- R13 `ALLOWED` muted (handkerchief, veiled pp; *Petrushka*), bells up (strident, Mahler), flutter-tongue
  (only moderately effective), quarter-tone bend by embouchure.
- R14 `PREFER` the English horn for a darker singing colour and for oboe-range low notes that must be soft;
  oboe ↔ EH phrase exchange is idiomatic (Berlioz *Fantastique* III).
- R15 `PREFER` English horn low and middle (52–72); `AVOID` its top for characteristic colour and double
  anything above 81.
- R16 `ALLOWED` EH in all woodwind figuration (as agile as the oboe); `PREFER` it over Ob 3 in a 3-part
  double-reed chord (smoother). EH + solo cello in unison fuse into one voice over wide pp strings
  (Debussy *La Mer*).

## Period practice
- `[era: Baroque]` fuller, more robust oboe tone than the modern one (Bach obbligati).
- `[era: early Classical]` 2 oboes hold tonic/dominant pedals, often in octaves with 2 horns, plus melodic
  bits; standard pairing strings + 2 ob + 2 hn (Mozart 29).
- `[era: Haydn to Wagner, Germany]` English horn almost abandoned (Berlioz, Meyerbeer excepted); first
  played by the 2nd oboist for a featured solo; a specialist from the mid-19th c.
- Sections: 2 ob; 2 ob + EH (default); 3 ob + EH (large); 4 ob + EH is exceptional (Stravinsky).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks `Ob 1`, `Ob 2`, `E.H.` at concert pitch.
- Floors: 58–65 CC1 ≥ 50 and vel ≥ 50; 58 only at CC1 ≥ 75. Notes 82+: CC1 ≤ 85.
- Staccato note length ≈ 35–50 % of the grid value (inferred); attacks always at a real velocity.
- When the oboe has the tune, other mid-register parts ≤ p (CC1 ≤ 45); breaths per [[user-taste]] R17.
- Muted oboe: low CC1 plus a high-cut; bells up: brighter layer, higher CC1 (inferred).

## Checklist
- [ ] Oboe 58–91, mostly 60–86; nothing ≥ 90 by leap; no 92–93.
- [ ] No pp (CC1 < 40 or vel < 40) on 58–65.
- [ ] No fast slurred arpeggios or fast repeated-note runs on oboe tracks.
- [ ] English horn 52–84, mostly 52–79; notes > 81 doubled.
- [ ] Oboe not used as a soft inner voice unless it is the intended colour.

## Examples
- Oboe solo zone: A4–C5–E5–D5 (69–72–76–74) over string pp.
- Berlioz: oboe phrase answered by EH a 5th lower (C5 → F4 sounding).
- Low double-reed snarl: Ob 1–2, Ob 3, EH sfz low in their ranges (≈ 52–67, illustrative), then dim. (Strauss *Till*).
