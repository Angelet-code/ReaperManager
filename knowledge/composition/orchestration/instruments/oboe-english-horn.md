# Oboe and English horn

## Core idea
Conical wooden tube with a **double reed**. Piston's adjectives: acrid, nasal, penetrating, cutting. Its
timbre is the most individual of the woodwinds: a melodic soloist, not a blender. Treble clef.

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Full (sounding) | Practical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oboe | as written | 58–93 (89 normal top, 90–93 extreme) | 60–86 |
| English horn (in F) | −7 | 52–84 (written B3–G6; 81 normal top) | 52–79 |

Oboe registers: **58–64** low (strongest, coarse; 58 cannot be attacked softly) · **65–81** middle
(characteristic, best solo zone) · **82–89** high (thins from A5; above D6 86 loses character, mostly
doubling) · **90–93** extreme (approach by step only). English horn: **52–59** low (dark, strong; general
knowledge) · **60–74** characteristic melancholy middle · **75–84** thinner top.

## Rules
- R1 `MUST` treat the oboe as **louder as it descends** (opposite of the flute).
- R2 `AVOID` soft (p/pp) attacks on Bb3 (58) and generally on 58–60.
- R3 `AVOID` notes from F#6 (90) up; if used, approach by step.
- R4 `AVOID` relying on oboe colour above A5 (81); above D6 (86) use only to double other woodwinds.
- R5 `AVOID` fast arpeggios and long agile figuration: less agile than flute or clarinet.
- R6 `ALLOWED` crisp rhythmic articulation, repeated staccato (Ravel *Tombeau*, Prélude).
- R7 `ALLOWED` all major and minor trills; `AVOID` tremolos wider than a P4 (5 st) and tremolos above the staff.
- R8 `PREFER` the oboe as solo melodic instrument, especially for pastoral, exotic or folk-like tunes
  (Falla *El amor brujo*).
- R9 `AVOID` oboes as blending filler inside the woodwind choir: the timbre penetrates flute, clarinet and
  bassoon chords; only in a very full tutti does it blend.
- R10 `PREFER` English horn for a clearly different melodic colour; alternating phrases oboe ↔ English horn
  is idiomatic (Berlioz *Fantastique* III).
- R11 `ALLOWED` breathing (general knowledge): the oboe uses little air, so the problem is stale air — long
  phrases are possible, but give rests every ≈ 6–8 bars for exhalation.
- R12 `PREFER` doublings: oboe + Vln I unison (adds edge); oboe + flute at unison or flute 8va; oboe +
  clarinet unison (smooths the oboe); oboes + horns (Classical pairing: strings + 2 ob + 2 hn).
- Style fact: oboes are in the orchestra from the start, in pairs with two horns (Mozart Sym. 29).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks `Ob 1`, `Ob 2`, `E.H.` at concert pitch.
- Articulations: legato, sustain, staccato (velocity), trills.
- Dynamics floor by register (inferred): 58–60 CC1 ≥ 50 (no ppp entries); 82+ CC1 ≤ 85 (thin anyway).
- Expose it: when the oboe has the tune, keep other mid-register instruments ≤ p (CC1 ≤ 45).
- Pan: centre-right in the woodwind row.

## Checklist
- [ ] Oboe notes 58–93; mostly 60–86; nothing ≥ 90 approached by leap.
- [ ] No pp/ppp entries (CC1 < 40 or vel < 40) on 58–60.
- [ ] English horn notes 52–84; mostly 52–79.
- [ ] No fast arpeggio figuration on oboe tracks (give it to flute/clarinet).
- [ ] Oboe not used as an inner voice in a soft woodwind chord unless it is the intended top colour.

## Examples
- Oboe solo zone: D4–D6 (62–86), e.g. A4–C5–E5–D5 (69–72–76–74) over string pp.
- Berlioz: oboe phrase answered by E.H. a 5th lower (C5 → F4 sounding).
