# Trombone (tenor and bass)

## Core idea
Slide instrument with seven positions, each a semitone lower (I = Bb series … VII = E series, lowest normal
note E2). Bass and tenor clefs, **non-transposing**. Homogeneous tone, brighter as it rises. Section since
the late 19th c.: **2 tenors + bass** or **3 tenors**.

| Instrument | Full (sounding) | Practical |
|---|---|---|
| Tenor trombone | 34–77 (40–70 normal; 71–77 extension; 34 pedal Bb1; 28–33 pedals insecure) | 40–70 |
| Bass trombone | 24–70 (general knowledge: F/D triggers fill the low range) | 31–65 |

Tenor registers: **28–39** pedals/gap (Bb1 34 usable; 35–39 need an F attachment, general knowledge) ·
**40–44** dark, rather nondescript · **45–66** very strong (approaches horn timbre in the middle, but more
solid and hard) · **67–70** very intense · **71–77** extension (strained; solo/jazz).
Bass trombone (general knowledge): **24–33** trigger/pedal zone, heavy, slow notes · **34–45** dark,
powerful bass, its signature zone · **46–65** like the tenor · **66–70** strained.

## Rules
- R1 `AVOID` fast passages that force extreme slide jumps (e.g. alternating positions VII and I).
- R2 `AVOID` pedal notes Bb1–E1 (34–28), especially below G1 (31): possible but insecure on tenor.
- R3 `ALLOWED` very delicate pianissimo in the low register (Schubert *Unfinished* I: soft trombone chords).
- R4 `ALLOWED` low-register fortissimo: dramatic, crushing, metallic, with huge crescendo power.
- R5 `AVOID` three trombones in unison against an orchestra you want to hear: they dominate the whole
  mass (Rimsky-Korsakov *Scheherazade* opening).
- R6 `PREFER` roles: Classical = cadential support and harmonic filler (with trumpets); 19th c. onward =
  solid bass of the brass, chorales, occasional solos (Puccini *Tosca* I).
- R7 `ALLOWED` legato is harder than on valve brass (slide glissando between notes); fast legato in
  distant positions smears (general knowledge). Glissando is idiomatic within one harmonic series.
- R8 `PREFER` breathing (general knowledge): large air use in the low register; rests every 2–4 bars of ff.
- R9 `PREFER` doublings: bass trombone + tuba (octave or unison) for the brass bass; trombones + horns in
  chorales; trombone + cellos/bassoons on a firm bass or tenor line; trombones + low strings for dark
  ff unisons (general knowledge).
- R10 `NEVER` treat the bass trombone as transposing in MIDI: tema 1's "reads an octave lower" note is
  doubtful; standard practice is concert pitch (inferred).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks `Tbn 1`, `Tbn 2`, `B.Tbn` (or a trombone ensemble patch) at concert pitch.
- Brass chorale: trombones on 45–65, bass trombone on the bass (34–50), adjacent tenor voices 3–7 st
  apart; below ≈ 48 prefer 5ths/octaves over 3rds (muddy) (general knowledge).
- Articulations: sustain, legato (slide), marcato/short, sfz, crescendo swells, muted, glissando.
- Dynamics: low pp chords CC1 20–40 are idiomatic; climaxes CC1 100–127 in 45–62.
- Pan: centre/right rear, behind trumpets.

## Checklist
- [ ] Tenor trombone notes 40–70 (34 or 71–77 only as rare exceptions); bass trombone 24–70.
- [ ] No fast (8ths ≥ ~140 bpm or 16ths) lines that jump between 40–45 and 46–58 repeatedly (VII↔I positions, inferred).
- [ ] Three-trombone unison melodies only where the brass should dominate.
- [ ] Rests every few bars in loud low passages.

## Examples
- Soft chord (Schubert-like): B.Tbn E2 (40), Tbn2 B2 (47), Tbn1 G#3 (56), pp.
- Climax: unison A2–D3–F3 (45 50 53) ff, doubled by tuba an octave below.
