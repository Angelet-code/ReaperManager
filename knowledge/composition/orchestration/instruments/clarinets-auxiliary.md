# Auxiliary clarinets

## Core idea
Same fingering as the Bb clarinet, so clarinettists double on them; each extends the family's range or
colour. Main notes, registers and techniques: [[clarinets]]. Ranges below are sounding MIDI.

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Vault range | Variants by author |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eb clarinet | +3 | 55–94 (safe top) | Piston: written G#6/A6 (95/96) appear more often than on Bb |
| D clarinet | +2 | 54–93 (inferred) | parts are played on Eb |
| Bass clarinet (Bb) | −14 French notation; −2 German | 34–82, practical 38–72 | Adler: standard floor 37, top 70, extreme 74; Piston: count on 37, highest advisable 75 |

## Rules
**Eb and D clarinets**
- R1 `PREFER` the Eb in its high register: it strengthens high woodwinds, reinforces violins and flutes,
  and extends the clarinets upward (penetrating tone, incisive staccato, agile).
- R2 `ALLOWED` its two lower octaves (55–≈79) only for parody, caricature and grotesque colour (Strauss
  *Till*: D clarinet squeal, sfz high note, falling to pp).
- R3 `AVOID` a quick switch from Bb/A to Eb for one player: it is a specialist's instrument.

**Bass clarinet**
- R4 `MUST` treat sounding 37 (C#2) as the floor you can count on; 34–36 only when the players or patch
  have a low-C instrument (Russian scores of the 1930s–40s ask for 34).
- R5 `PREFER` the chalumeau, about 37–53: dark, mysterious, even sinister. The clarion (≈ 55–65) is paler
  and less incisive than the same pitches on Bb clarinet; use the Bb clarinet for brilliance there.
- R6 `ALLOWED` the top (≈ 65–75) as thin solo colour, never a powerful lead; cap generated parts at 75.
- R7 `PREFER` it as the woodwind bass for soft chords: its low pp is much softer than the bassoon's.
- R8 `ALLOWED` "shadowy fleetness": fast, light low runs and trills at p–pp; lyrical legato; all
  staccatos, with a slightly less incisive attack than the small clarinets.
- R9 `MUST` state the notation in a written part (French treble clef −14, or German bass clef −2) with a
  footnote; Adler recommends French.

**Alto clarinet, basset horn, contrabass clarinets**
- R10 `PREFER` the alto clarinet only in its lowest two octaves (43–67), for soft harmony and soft solos;
  it is weak and mainly a band instrument.
- R11 `PREFER` basset horns for a veiled, solemn mid-low clarinet colour; narrower bore than the alto, so
  it sounds distinct. `ALLOWED` substitute an alto or bass clarinet.
- R12 `PREFER` the contrabass clarinet for soft deep pedals and rich low sustains. `AVOID` agile writing,
  and `AVOID` counting on it in an orchestra: common in band and wind ensemble only.

## Period practice
- `[era: Classical, Mozart]` basset horns in *Magic Flute*, Requiem, Serenade K.361; Mozart's concerto
  K.622 was written for a basset clarinet. `[era: late Romantic]` revived by Strauss (*Elektra*,
  *Capriccio*).
- `[era: late-Romantic notation]` basset horn in bass clef was written to sound a P4 **higher** (old horn
  convention); in treble clef, a P5 lower.
- `[era: 19th c.]` bass clarinet in A, for its low C#2; now played on Bb. German bass-clef notation
  belongs to late-19th/early-20th-c. German scores.
- `[era: late Romantic]` large sections pair Eb and bass clarinets around 3–4 Bb/A (see [[clarinets]]).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- `Eb Cl`: keep lines in 79–94 when it reinforces flutes/violins; staccato and runs.
- `B.Cl`: floor 37; bass of woodwind chords at pp (CC1 15–40); double the cello line in unison or the bass
  line an octave up. Soft low entries move here from the bassoon.
- `Bst Hn`: 41–77 for Mozart-period pastiche, between clarinet and bassoon (top inferred).
- Contrabass clarinet: long notes ≤ 50; with no patch, use contrabassoon or bass clarinet an octave up
  (inferred).

## Checklist
- [ ] Eb clarinet 55–94; low half only for parody.
- [ ] Bass clarinet 37–75 (34–36 only with a low-C patch); core lines in 37–53.
- [ ] Bass-clarinet clarion lines that need brilliance moved to Bb clarinet.
- [ ] Alto clarinet ≤ 67; contrabass clarinet slow and sustained.

## Examples
- Bass clarinet pedal under clarinet tune: Db2–F2–Ab2 (37 41 44) whole notes pp, Cl 1 in the clarino.
- Eb clarinet doubling Vln I an octave up at ff: 84–91, staccato 8ths.
