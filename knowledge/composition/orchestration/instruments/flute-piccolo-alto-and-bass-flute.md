# Alto flute and bass flute

## Core idea
Auxiliary flutes extend the family downward: bigger tube, same fingering, so they transpose. Built to play
lower, they favour their low notes and lose character at the top. Main note: [[flute-piccolo]].

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Full (sounding) | Useful |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alto flute in G | −5 (P4 down) | 55–91 (written C4–C7; top [?] in Adler) | 55–79 (vault practical 55–86) |
| Bass flute in C | −12 | 48–~84 (written C4–~C7, top [?]) | 48–72 |

Alto registers (written C4 = sounding G3): **55–~60** deep, rich, sonorous · **~60–67** full, very effective
· **68–79** differs only subtly from the flute · **80–91** plain, no brilliance (Piston: avoid 85–91).

## Rules
- R1 `PREFER` the alto flute in its low octave (55–67), its most individual zone (Adler, Piston).
- R2 `MUST` accompany it lightly or not at all, and keep other parts out of its octave. Sources differ on its
  strength: UNIR finds it as soft as the low flute, Piston and Adler richer, stronger and better projecting
  than the flute's low notes. Either way it loses against loud brass or strings.
- R3 `AVOID` its upper register (≥ ~80): the flute and piccolo cover it better.
- R4 `ALLOWED` all flute techniques, including fast double and triple tonguing; `MUST` give more breath
  room than for the flute (bigger bore, more air).
- R5 `ALLOWED` the alto flute as the bass of a 3–4 flute group (Holst *Planets*: soft oscillating figure
  under 3 flutes' pp chords), or as solo voice under a high trill layer of flutes (Stravinsky *Rite*,
  "Rondes printanières").
- R6 `PREFER` the bass flute in its low register (48–~67): rich, muffled, singular, warm.
- R7 `AVOID` writing a bass flute into a standard orchestral part without checking availability: expensive,
  few orchestras own one (solo, chamber, some band and film scores).
- R8 `MUST` keep the bass flute's accompaniment very light: it is soft and easily covered.

## Period practice
- `[era: c.1890–1930]` the alto flute enters the orchestra (Ravel *Daphnis*, Stravinsky *Rite*); `[era:
  1955, Piston]` still not a regular member; `[era: 2002, Adler]` accepted but used in few works. Often
  miscalled "bass flute".
- `[era: 1930 on]` first practical bass flute (Rudall, Carte & Co., bent headjoint); Zandonai *Francesca da
  Rimini*, later chamber and film use.

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks `Alto Fl` / `Bass Fl` at concert pitch; most sample patches play sounding pitch (check).
- Alto flute solo: notes 55–72, CC1 40–75, accompaniment ≤ pp (CC1 ≤ 40) with nothing in 55–67.
  Line rises above ~79 → continue it on `Fl 1`.
- Bass flute solo: 48–72; no other parts in 48–72 above p; plenty of breath gaps (≥ 1 beat every 2–3 bars at
  moderate tempo, inferred).
- Flute choir: `Fl 1`, `Fl 2`, `Fl 3` close triads on top, `Alto Fl` on the bass, same rhythm, staccato or
  soft legato.
- No alto or bass flute patch: a low C flute at 60–67 in a very thin texture, or a clarinet in its chalumeau
  for a darker colour (inferred).

## Checklist
- [ ] Alto flute 55–91, mostly 55–79; bass flute 48–84, mostly 48–72.
- [ ] Nothing loud in the same octave while either plays a solo.
- [ ] Breath gaps more frequent than on the C flute.
- [ ] Bass flute only where the brief allows a rare instrument (film, chamber, band).

## Examples
- Holst *Saturn*: alto flute solo pp legato, low register — dark, hollow line.
- Alto flute pp tune D4–F4–G4–A4 (62 65 67 69) under Fl 1–2 trilling A5–B5 / F#5–G#5 p.
