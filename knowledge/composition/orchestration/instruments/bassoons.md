# Bassoon and contrabassoon

## Core idea
Maple-wood **double reed** instrument; bass and tenor clef like the cello. Registers blend into each other
without sharp boundaries. Contrabassoon is the family's auxiliary: thicker, sounds an octave below
written, with audible slow vibrations in the lowest notes.

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Full (sounding) | Practical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bassoon | as written | 34–75 | 34–72 |
| Contrabassoon | −12 | 21–60 (21 only extended instruments; 58–60 extreme) | 22–53 |

Bassoon registers (approximate boundaries [?]): **34–44** low (sonorous, dark, vibrant, a bit rough, rich in
overtones) · **45–61** middle (sweet, subdued, expressive; softer and easily neutralised) · **62–70** upper
(thin but intense; tender lines) · **71–75** top (thin, often pinched; deliberately strained effect).
Contrabassoon: **22–33** sub-bass, **34–47** solid foundation, **48–60** weak (general knowledge).

## Rules
- R1 `AVOID` pp or soft attacks in the low register (34–44); give soft low entries to bass clarinet.
- R2 `AVOID` leaving middle-register bassoon (45–61) unsupported under heavy scoring: it is covered.
- R3 `PREFER` the upper part of the middle register (≈ 58–70) for tender, delicate solos.
- R4 `ALLOWED` the top (71–75) for a deliberately strained, primitive effect (Stravinsky *Rite* opening).
- R5 `PREFER` staccato: especially effective, light or comic (Dukas *Sorcerer's Apprentice*).
- R6 `ALLOWED` fair agility: scales, arpeggios and wide leaps are idiomatic (general knowledge); fast low
  staccato is clear.
- R7 `PREFER` roles: bass of the woodwind choir; doubling the cellos on the bass; tenor counter-melodies;
  19th-c. solos. Baroque: continuo with cello/keyboard; trio of 2 oboes + bassoon.
- R8 `PREFER` doublings: bassoon + cello (bass or tenor line); bassoons + horns on mid sustained harmony
  (Humperdinck chorale: 4 horns + 2 bassoons); bassoon an octave below clarinet or oboe melodies
  (general knowledge); contrabassoon + double bass or tuba (sub-octave).
- R9 `MUST` give the contrabassoon regular rests: low notes use a lot of air.
- R10 `AVOID` fast figuration on contrabassoon below ≈ 33 (slow-speaking, buzzy) (general knowledge).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks `Bsn 1`, `Bsn 2`, `Cbsn` at concert pitch (contrabassoon patches play sounding pitch).
- Articulations: legato, sustain, staccato (velocity 50–100), marcato, trills.
- Dynamic floor (inferred): 34–44 CC1 ≥ 45 / vel ≥ 45; in 45–61 under tutti, double with cello or horn
  rather than raising CC1.
- Contrabassoon: put rests of ≥ 1 bar every ≈ 4–6 bars of sustained low notes (inferred from R9).
- Pan: centre-right, second woodwind row.

## Checklist
- [ ] Bassoon notes 34–75; mostly 34–72.
- [ ] No pp (CC1 < 40) entries on 34–44.
- [ ] Middle-register bassoon line is not alone under brass/tutti.
- [ ] Contrabassoon notes 21–60 (≤ 53 normally) with regular rests.
- [ ] Woodwind chords: bassoon has the lowest woodwind note unless bass clarinet does.

## Examples
- Comic staccato: G2–D3–B2–D3 (43 50 47 50) 8ths, vel 70–85.
- Tender solo: E4–D4–C4–B3 (64 62 60 59), p, CC1 40–55, strings pp underneath.
