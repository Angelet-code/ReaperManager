# Tuba family and relatives

## Core idea
"Tuba" is an ambiguous instruction: the player picks the tube. **No modern tuba transposes**; MIDI is
always concert pitch. Defaults and writing rules: [[tuba]].

| Type (tube) | Fundamental | Chromatic, sounding (Piston figs.) | Extremes, notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tenor tuba / euphonium, Bb (9 ft) | Bb1 34 | B1–Bb4 (35–70), 4 valves | pedals to F1 (29–34; 29–32 doubtful); players go higher. Adler: Bb1–Bb4 |
| Bass tuba F (12 ft) | F1 29 | to F4 (65) | pedals D1–F1 (26–29), good but hard; G4 (67) extreme |
| Bass tuba Eb (14 ft) | Eb1 27 | to Eb4 (63) | F4 (65) extreme; heavier tone, slight low advantage |
| Contrabass CC (16 ft) | C1 24 | ≈ D1 [?] (26)–C4 (60) | pedal C1 (24) doubtful; D4 (62) extreme |
| Contrabass BBb (18 ft) | Bb0 22 | ≈ E1 [?] (28)–Bb3 (58) | pedal Bb0 barely possible |

## Rules
- R1 `MUST` valves: 1 = −2, 2 = −1, 3 = −3 st, **4th = −5 (P4)**; 5th usually −7, 6th −1 (rare). With
  three valves nothing exists between the fundamental and an augmented 4th above it; the 4th valve fills
  that gap and replaces the out-of-tune 1+3 (Piston, Adler).
- R2 `AVOID` low notes combining the 4th valve with others: hopelessly sharp unless the instrument
  compensates (B1 on a euphonium comes out ≈ C2) (Piston). Treat the notes just above the pedal as risky.
- R3 `PREFER` the type to the style: CC tuba's ponderous tone suits Wagnerian music; an F-tuba sound suits
  Debussy/Ravel textures (Piston, inferred contrast). `[era: 1955, Piston]` F = best all-round orchestral
  tuba; `[era: 2002, Adler, US orchestras]` CC and BBb are the symphonic players' favourites.
- R4 `AVOID` the BBb contrabass in combination: unwieldy heavy sound, though a fine pianissimo (Piston).
- R5 `ALLOWED` **euphonium** (bombardino): miniature tuba, conical, 4 valves, mellow and smooth, no
  intonation problems; lighter, more flexible than the bass tuba for lively figures (Strauss *Don Quixote*).
  Orchestral use: tenor-tuba parts (Holst *Planets*, *Don Quixote*), high solos ("Bydlo"). Common in
  bands and wind ensembles. Write it at concert pitch, bass clef (Strauss's Bb notation, −2, is an
  exception, Piston).
- R6 `ALLOWED` **baritone**: band only; same tube as the euphonium, narrower bore, 3 valves, suits higher
  parts; E2–Bb4 (40–70), agile, blends well (Adler, Piston). Sousaphone: band tuba that can play any tuba
  part, never in the symphony orchestra.
- R7 `AVOID` **Wagner tubas** unless the request names them: few orchestras own them (Piston 1955); new
  ones and new parts appear recently (Adler; Rouse Symphony 1). Horn-like: bore between horn and tuba,
  horn mouthpiece, 4 left-hand valves; played by horns 5–8. Ranges like horns of the same pitch (tenor
  Bb, bass F); tone broader, less focused and less agile than horn; intonation problems (Adler).
- R8 `ALLOWED` ophicleide parts go to tuba, or to bassoon/contrabassoon (Adler).

## Period practice
- `[era: c.1820–1850s]` **Ophicleide**: keyed brass bass shaped like a metal bassoon, wide conical bore,
  padded keys, cup mouthpiece, in 8-ft C or 9-ft Bb; bassoon-like range, mellow euphonium-like tone;
  non-transposing bass clef. Berlioz, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Meyerbeer, Verdi; one of the last: Wagner
  *Rienzi*. The tuba replaced it completely.
- `[era: from c.1875]` The bass tuba enters the orchestra (Adler, Piston); parts for earlier small tubas or
  ophicleide are played on today's tubas.
- `[era: Wagner *Ring*, Bruckner 7–9, Strauss *Elektra*, Schoenberg *Gurrelieder*]` Wagner-tuba quartet.
  Piston: 2 tenor (Bb) + 2 bass (F) plus a contrabass tuba in C; Adler (both editions): tenors in Bb and Eb,
  basses in F and BBb. Bruckner 7: a solemn p chorale of four Wagner tubas + contrabass tuba.
- `[era: score reading]` Wagner-tuba notations differ (sounding vs written): Wagner early, tenor −2, bass −7;
  later, tenor (Eb) −9, bass (Bb) −14; Bruckner 7 and *Elektra*, tenor −14, bass −19; Bruckner 9, tenor −2,
  bass in bass clef **+5**. Treble-clef parts on euphonium: −14 (Adler).
- `[era: French practice]` A small 8-ft C tuba with six valves still reaches the large tubas' low notes.

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- One `TUBA` track; clamp to the chosen type: F 26–65 (67), CC 26–60 (62), BBb 28–58; default [[tuba]].
- `EUPH` track (tenor tuba): 34–70, at home ≈ 41–65 (Piston); use it for agile tenor lines and high solos.
- Wagner tubas: a Wagner-tuba or euphonium patch, else low horns; parts in about 36–65, chorale-style,
  p–mf, placed between `BTBN` and `TUBA` (inferred).
- Ophicleide pastiche: tuba or euphonium patch in its middle register, optionally doubled by bassoon
  (inferred).

## Checklist
- [ ] Tuba type implied by the style; range clamp matches it.
- [ ] No notes 1–5 st above a 3-valve fundamental; no long exposed notes just above the pedal.
- [ ] Wagner tubas, baritone or ophicleide only when the request names that colour or period.
