# Oboe d'amore, oboe da caccia and heckelphone

## Core idea
No oboe-family instrument extends upward; these extend it downward and, like every downward auxiliary,
favour their low notes. All have a bulb bell (except the heckelphone's bassoon-like build) and are rare in
modern orchestras. Main note: [[oboe-english-horn]].

| Instrument | Written → sounding | Sounding range | Useful |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oboe d'amore (A) | −3 | 56–~85 (written B3–~E6, top [?]) | 56–76 |
| Oboe da caccia (F) | −7 | as English horn, 52–~84 | 52–72 |
| Heckelphone / bass oboe | −12 | 45–~76 (written A3–~E6; optional ~G6 [?]) | 45–72 |

## Rules
- R1 `NEVER` write the oboe d'amore below written B3 (sounding 56): no low Bb key.
- R2 `PREFER` its low notes: full, dark, sweeter and gentler than oboe or EH. `AVOID` its upper register:
  weak, almost useless; double it if used (Schuller does).
- R3 `PREFER` a transposed modern part; Bach sometimes wrote sounding pitch (*Christmas Oratorio*).
- R4 `PREFER` notating an oboe da caccia exactly like an English horn (same range and colour).
- R5 `PREFER` the heckelphone as the bass of a double-reed choir (oboe / EH / heckelphone): rich, reedy
  lower octave. `AVOID` its upper tones.
- R6 `AVOID` requiring a heckelphone or bass oboe in a normal orchestral part: very few exist. All
  heckelphone parts are playable on the bass oboe.

## Period practice
- `[era: Baroque, Bach]` oboe d'amore very popular; 2 oboi d'amore + 2 oboi da caccia form a pastoral reed
  choir alternating with flutes and strings (*Christmas Oratorio* Sinfonia, 12/8). Da caccia parts were
  often in alto clef at sounding pitch.
- `[era: after Baroque]` both fell out of use as halls grew; the d'amore returns in Strauss (*Sinfonia
  domestica*, solo p "sehr zart" over ppp violin tremolo), Debussy *Gigues* (melancholy solo), Ravel
  *Boléro*, Mahler.
- `[era: late Romantic, 1904 on]` heckelphone: Strauss *Salome* (lyrical cantabile solo), *Elektra*;
  Delius *Dance Rhapsody* (bass oboe under an EH tune, over divided cellos and pizz basses).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Tracks at concert pitch: `Ob d'amore` 56–76, `Heckelphone` 45–72.
- No patch: oboe d'amore → oboe softly in 60–76 or EH in 56–72; heckelphone → bassoon or low EH in 52–65
  (inferred).
- Bach-style reed choir: 2 tracks oboe d'amore (≈ 64–79) + 2 tracks da caccia/EH (≈ 55–70), legato,
  antiphonal with flutes + strings; light vibrato.
- Low reed duet: EH melody pp at 60–72, heckelphone/bassoon triplets at 45–57.

## Checklist
- [ ] Oboe d'amore ≥ 56; nothing exposed above ~76 undoubled.
- [ ] Heckelphone 45–76, mostly ≤ 72; only when the brief allows a rare instrument.
- [ ] Period request (Bach, Strauss) matches the instrument chosen.

## Examples
- Debussy *Gigues*: unaccompanied oboe d'amore solo, p, 6/8, tenuto notes with grace notes.
