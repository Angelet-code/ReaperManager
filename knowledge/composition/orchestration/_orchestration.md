# Orchestration

Per-instrument notes (ranges in MIDI, registers, articulations, transposition): **[[_instruments]]**.
Film-specific practice (template, reverb, five elements, HTTYD case study): [[_film-scoring]].
Terms in Spanish: [[glossary-orchestration]]. Indented names (↳) are split notes of the hub above them.
Adler (2002) is the modern default; Piston (1955) and older practice are era-tagged variants;
[[user-taste]] overrides all books.

## Texture types
| note | use_when | summary |
|---|---|---|
| [[texture-types]] | Classifying a section before orchestrating it, or analysing a reference | Piston's seven types; list the elements and their planes, then load the recipe note; change layout every few bars, type at form boundaries |
| [[texture-unison-and-tutti]] | "Unison", "tutti", "everyone together", a climax needing the whole orchestra | unison only with instruments whose good register holds the line; tutti over ≈ 4 octaves weighted in the middle; reserve the grand tutti |
| ↳ [[texture-unison-and-tutti-layouts]] | Laying out a tutti octave by octave; massive vs brilliant tutti | unisons add weight, octaves brightness; massive = 2 adjacent octaves, brilliant = 4–5 with every family per octave |
| [[texture-accompanied-melody]] | A tune with accompaniment; moving a melody between instruments | melody / bass / filler on their own instruments; 2–4 subelements with own rhythms; hand over at phrase boundaries with a dovetail |
| ↳ [[texture-accompanied-melody-doubling-the-tune]] | Choosing and doubling the melody instrument (unison, octave, partial), handing a wide line over | expressive solos undoubled; octave = brighter, unison = new colour + weight; one-note overlap hand-offs |
| ↳ [[texture-accompanied-melody-concerto-soloist]] | Orchestra under a solo instrument (concerto, SWAM soloist, film solo) | separate actively: dialogue, colour contrast, rhythmic independence, thin beds; drop the tutti to p at solo entries |
| ↳ [[texture-accompanied-melody-voice-and-choir]] | Orchestra under a singer, vocal ensemble or choir; vocal ranges | lead singers to entries, keep pitch references; thin in the voice's weak low 5th; violins double the voice only at the climax |
| [[texture-secondary-melody-countermelody]] | Adding a countermelody or answers in the gaps | two melodic lines differ in colour, contour, rhythm; secondary one level below, never above |
| [[texture-part-writing-chorale]] | Chorales and 3–5 voice harmonic writing | a few real parts, every other staff a unison/octave doubling; mixed groups or pure-colour choirs; close upper spacing |
| ↳ [[texture-part-writing-chorale-doubling]] | Turning a voice-led progression into a full orchestral layout | never double the bass among upper voices or below the bass; chorale tutti template (strings SATB, trumpets top, low brass) |
| [[texture-counterpoint]] | Two or more independent lines of similar importance | rank the lines; own colour mix, register band and rhythm per line; no octave doubling of close lines; no pads |
| ↳ [[texture-counterpoint-entries-and-canon]] | Fugato, fugal exposition, canon or stretto | separate entries by family and register; subject alone first; entering voice f marcato; era-tagged entry orders |
| [[texture-complex-mosaic]] | Layered shimmer, action beds, suspense clouds, no primary element | few elements, each with its own band, colour and grid; introduce motives alone; micropolyphony by offset divisi copies |
| ↳ [[texture-complex-mosaic-combined-textures]] | Busy tutti with several simultaneous elements, or two textures at once | plan 3–4 elements (chorale, countermelody, scale, pedal) on separate families, bands and rhythm levels |

## Chords, doubling and clarity
| note | use_when | summary |
|---|---|---|
| [[chord-voicing-family-stacking]] | Voicing chords, pads or stabs across families | four pair layouts (superposition, overlap, interlocking, enclosure); harmonic-series spacing; balance = players per note × register strength |
| ↳ [[chord-voicing-family-stacking-tutti]] | Forte/ff chords for full orchestra, buttons, stabs, polychords | brass first (2 horns per trumpet note), high woods above, low reeds on the bass; cap trombones and timpani in a held crescendo |
| ↳ [[chord-voicing-family-stacking-soft]] | p/pp chords, soft final chords, ppp tutti, ff → pp chords | open gaps only when soft; high soft tops on violins, not undoubled flutes (≈ 89+); fading chord doubles only bass, melody and top |
| [[doubling-colour-and-decay]] | Choosing a doubler, or checking for over-doubling | double for definition, weight, colour or emotion; octave rather than unison; add doublers to crescendo, remove them to fade |
| ↳ [[doubling-colour-and-decay-section-pairings]] | Concrete cross-family doublers, how many balance at p and f | congenial unisons Vln+Fl, Vla+EH, Vc+Bcl, Cb+Cbsn; one wind per octave at p, a2 at f; horns link woods and brass |
| [[bass-line-doubling]] | Writing or fixing the bass and low end | Vc + Cb an octave below; 4-string limit E1 (28); film bass adds piano, harp, trombone, bassoon and a dry low layer |
| ↳ [[bass-line-doubling-recipes]] | Bass doubling by colour or dynamic (majestic, brass forte, light, pedal) | solid = Trb 3 + tuba + Vc + Cb; DB pedal with Hn 4 + contrabassoon; light bass drops or bridges the basses |
| [[line-separation-and-fusion]] | Making lines independent, or fusing instruments into one gesture | rhythm/articulation, register and timbre separate; dynamics least; beware accidental fusion between neighbours |

## Section writing
| note | use_when | summary |
|---|---|---|
| [[string-section-writing]] | The string section as a whole: which section carries the melody, FG/MG/BG inside the strings | five voices of one colour; label every line FG/MG/BG; balance by register before dynamics; save the theme's top octave |
| ↳ [[string-section-writing-homophony]] | String chords, chorale, pad; which section takes which tone; divisi | harmonic-series spacing Cb–Vc–Vla–Vln II–Vln I; divide Vln II/Vla/Vc, never Vln I; Cb one note |
| ↳ [[string-section-writing-counterpoint]] | Fugato, canon, theme vs countermelody for strings alone | separate by register band and rhythm; subject alone first; Baroque entry orders era-tagged |
| ↳ [[string-section-writing-accompaniment]] | Strings under a soloist; piano accompaniment → strings | five templates; 1–2 dynamic steps under the soloist and out of its band; piano figures → repeated, held or pizz strings |
| [[woodwind-section-writing]] | What the woodwinds do; woodwind melody or counterpoint | one function at a time; solos single, a2 only in tutti; octave pairs of changing colour; fixed colour per motive |
| ↳ [[woodwind-section-writing-chords]] | Woodwind chords, chorales, pedals, held harmony; bright vs dark voicing | stacked / interlocked / enclosed pairs; widest gap at the bottom; Classical wind pedals; at f vs brass, woods on top and doubled |
| ↳ [[woodwind-section-writing-with-strings]] | Winds with strings: accompaniment, alternation, antiphony, doubling | off-beat dyads under a string tune; winds double strings at the octave, one per octave at p, a2 at f |
| [[brass-section-writing]] | Voicing a chord or chorale for the whole brass; balancing horns, trumpets, trombones | ≤ mf one note per player; above mf 2 horns ≈ 1 trumpet ≈ 1 trombone; trumpets top, horns interlocked, trombones tenor, tuba under |
| ↳ [[brass-section-writing-melody]] | A theme in the brass, alone or doubled with strings and woods | unison + octaves = strong; low brass + bassoons + low strings on one line among the strongest sounds; re-colour repeats |
| ↳ [[brass-section-writing-counterpoint]] | Brass canon, fugato, stretto, chorale variations | separate entries by family and register; entry f ben marcato over softer voices; stretto one bar apart |
| ↳ [[brass-section-writing-effects]] | Section-level brass colours: harmon, hat, rips, niente, loud mutes, flutter | harmon jazzy; hat solo sf>p; ensemble rip into an sffz stab; muted ≠ quiet; notate mutes exactly |
| ↳ [[brass-section-writing-period-practice]] | Brass section in the manner of Gabrieli → Wagner/Bruckner, French or Russian | Classical 2 natural horns + 2 trumpets on I/V, trombones for climaxes; valves accepted only in the 20th c. |
| [[percussion-section-writing]] | Whether and how percussion enters; players; track order; with keyboards and harp | every entry needs a job; ff timp + BD masks everything; 3–4 players + timpanist; track order timp → unpitched → harp → mallets → keys |
| ↳ [[percussion-section-writing-climax-and-openings]] | Percussion to build or crown a climax, open a piece, march/alla turca/Spanish colour | build by stages that each add a colour, or reserve the timpani; soft timpani openings; Turkish kit = BD, cymbals, triangle |
