# Mode mixture and simple alteration

## Core idea
"Simple alteration" = chords obtained by changing scale notes with accidentals while staying in the key.
Two sources: (1) the other forms of minor (melodic ascending, natural/descending); (2) chords exchanged
between parallel major and minor. They add colour without a change of tonic.

### Chord palette
| Key | Chord | Notes (C / A minor) |
|---|---|---|
| Major | minor iv | F–Ab–C |
| Major | bVI | Ab–C–Eb |
| Major | Neapolitan bII (6) | Db–F–Ab, bass F |
| Major | iiø7 | D–F–Ab–C |
| Major | vii°7 | B–D–F–Ab |
| Minor (melodic asc.) | ii, IV major, vi° | B–D–F#, D–F#–A, F#–A–C |
| Minor (natural) | III, v, VII | C–E–G, E–G–B, G–B–D |
| Minor | Neapolitan | Bb–D–F, bass D |
| Minor | raised VI | F#–A–C# |

## Rules
- R1 `ALLOWED` Mix modes: minor chords in major and vice versa; even the tonic may change mode where no clear
  major/minor feeling is wanted. `PREFER` this in the middle of the harmonic development rather than at the
  beginning or end.
- R2 `PREFER` Altered II mainly as Neapolitan sixth (first inversion). Root position is also possible.
- R3 `ALLOWED` (recommended) Nap6 → V: one voice goes b2 → leading tone (diminished third, C: Db→B) and a
  cross relation between b2 and the natural 2 is accepted — both normally avoided elsewhere.
- R4 `PREFER` Deceptive variant I–V7–bVI (C: G7 → Ab).
- R5 `ALLOWED` End a minor piece on a major tonic (Picardy third).
- R6 `MUST` Secondary sevenths produced by alteration follow the same fifth policy as diatonic ones
  ([[diatonic-seventh-chords]] R8).
- R7 `MUST` Altered chords easily blur the key: bring back the tonic and main harmonies often and place them
  on strong positions (no-modulation stage).
- R8 `ALLOWED` For heavy, sombre expression a whole bar (or more) on the Neapolitan sixth can lead with the
  melody to a soft ending.
- R9 Read enharmonics by function: a "B major" chord in Bb major is really Cb, the Neapolitan.
  - *Outside this style:* iv, bVI, bVII and bIII in major are the backbone of "epic", nostalgic and heroic
    film/pop harmony (e.g. I–bVI–bVII–I; I–iv–I) (general knowledge). Use them freely there, but still
    re-anchor the tonic. See [[_film-scoring]].

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Borrowed pitch classes in major (tonic t): b3 = t+3, b6 = t+8, b7 = t+10, b2 = t+1. A chord is "borrowed" if
  it contains t+3, t+8 or t+1 in a major key.
- Neapolitan sixth voicing in C (B/T/A/S): F2, Ab3, Db4, F4 (bass doubled, general knowledge) → I6/4 G2,
  G3, C4, E4 → V7 G2, F3, B3, D4 → I. Or skip the 6/4 and take Db directly to B (diminished third, R3).
- Mixture move in a pad: keep common tones (C, and G when present) held; the inflected note (E→Eb, A→Ab)
  moves by a semitone in the same voice — this hides the colour change smoothly (general knowledge).

## Checklist
- Borrowed chords in mid-phrase, tonic re-established after them?
- Neapolitan mostly in first inversion, going to V (or I6/4–V)?
- Picardy third only on the final tonic?

## Examples
- C major: I – iv6 – V – I; I – V7 – bVI (deceptive); I – bII6 – I6/4 – V7 – I.
- E minor: … F (Neapolitan, root position) → B (V) → Em.
- D minor ending: … A7 → D major (Picardy).
