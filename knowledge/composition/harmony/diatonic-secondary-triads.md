# Secondary diatonic triads

## Core idea
Major key: II, III, VI minor, VII diminished. Minor (harmonic) key: VI major, II and VII diminished, III
augmented. Adding these to I/IV/V gives stepwise root motion, variety for repeated phrases and the deceptive
cadence. Diminished and augmented triads need care with doubling.

## Rules
- R1 `MUST` Connect major/minor triads on II, III, VI (and inversions) exactly like I, IV, V
  (see [[voice-leading-basics]]).
- R2 `PREFER` Diminished triads in inversion, mostly first inversion; double the tone outside the tritone.
- R3 `PREFER` Resolve the diminished fifth inward and the augmented fourth outward.
- R4 `NEVER` Double the leading tone in the augmented triad (III+ of minor).
- R5 `ALLOWED` Doubled leading tone when it is not the third of a dominant, or when the dominant goes to II,
  III, IV or VI and the two leading tones move in different directions, or when it clearly improves the lines.
- R6 `ALLOWED` When soprano and bass are given and strongly constrain the texture (typical with III+), accept
  hidden fifths/octaves in inner pairs as the least bad option; `AVOID` them between soprano and bass.
- R7 `PREFER` V(7)→VI in place of V→I at an expected ending = deceptive cadence (cadencia rota). Variant in
  major: V7→bVI (see [[mode-mixture-and-neapolitan]]).
- R8 `PREFER` iv6→V at a phrase end in minor = Phrygian half cadence (see [[cadences]]).
- R9 `PREFER` Harmonise corresponding bar groups differently (e.g. bars 1–3 vs 6–8) — secondary triads are the
  first tool for this.
- R10 `ALLOWED` From here on vocal ranges are relaxed; at the keyboard fuller or thinner chords are fine.
  - *Outside this style:* vi, ii, iii and the deceptive V–vi are everyday pop/film harmony; vii° is rare in
    pop (replaced by V or bVII). III+ is mostly a film "uneasy" colour (general knowledge).

## Apply in MIDI / REAPER
- Deceptive cadence voice-leading (standard practice, general knowledge): bass rises a step, leading tone up to
  tonic, the other upper voices move down (step or third) against the bass; the third of VI is doubled. In C: G2, B3, D4, G4 →
  A2, C4, C4, E4 (C doubled = third of A minor).
- Stepwise bass sequences using all degrees (e.g. IV6–III6–II6) give smooth descending lines for strings.
- Pool selection: prefer II/IV before V (pre-dominant), VI after I or after V (deceptive), III sparingly
  (weakest function).
- Diminished VII: write vii°6 (in C: D3 bass, D doubled, F, B) and resolve to I or I6.

## Checklist
- Diminished triads in first inversion with their third doubled?
- No doubled leading tone in III+?
- Deceptive cadence: third of VI doubled, leading tone to tonic?
- Repeated phrase re-harmonised (not identical)?

## Examples
- E major rhythm drill style: I III | IV II III | I IV6 V6 | III6 VI | IV I6/4 VI | V6/5 I II6 | I6/4 V7 | I.
- G major: … II6/5 V13/7 | VI (deceptive) … V7 I.
- A minor Phrygian close: F3 bass (iv6) → E3 (V).
